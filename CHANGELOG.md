# SonoLune release notes

Every SonoLune release, newest first, from 1.0 in Feb 2026 to 2.4.4. These are the same notes the app shows in its Field Journal.

Releases marked **Insider** went to testers and never reached the public store.

## 2.4.4 · 26 Sep 2026

**Apps it will leave alone, and the work it was doing while you slept**

- You can now name apps SonoLune will leave alone entirely. Help, then Apps SonoLune leaves alone. Put an app on that list and no tint, wash or dimming settles over it again - not from the evening light, not from the Presence Field, not from a pack. Nothing else about the app changes. The same page shows what SonoLune already steps aside for on its own, which it has never shown before: UPI apps like Google Pay, Paytm and PhonePe, permission requests, the Play Store, signing in to Google, your settings, and any call on any app.
- There is a second pack on each of the three Sound rails, and they arrive on their own rather than with this update. Passages, Tonal Warmth and Human Warmth have carried one pack each since the rails opened; each has two now. Open Country is a far dog, distant cowbells and a rooster across the valley - things that pass through open country rather than loop in it. Last Light is a slow harmony, a sleeping drone and a floating tone, all in one key, later in the day than First Light and darker for it. Other Rooms is a far market, a supper room and a waiting hall: people at a distance, never words. Each one keeps a layer open to everyone, always, exactly as the first three do - a rooster, a sleeping drone, a waiting hall - and Patron of the Lab carries the rest. Nothing downloads until you tap a pack, and nothing about how the rails work has changed.
- If you are coming from 2.4.2, everything from 2.4.3 is in here and is new to you: the light that would not come back on from the notification bulb or the Quick Settings tile, the Night invitation that could not arrive at night, a door that can now tell you when it deliberately stayed quiet, the moon in Mind that refused to let you back into a space you had held, and the hint on the Sound cards that was cut off mid-word.
- When your light stops on its own, SonoLune now tells you. The light is not a picture the app draws while you are looking at it - it is a service that keeps running after you leave, which is the only way it can still be there at three in the morning. Phones are allowed to stop that service, and several manufacturers do it by default to save battery, without asking and without saying so. Nothing crashes. The light simply is not there any more, and until now the app never noticed: it checked that it still had permission, which it did, and concluded all was well. It checks whether its own service is actually running now, and if the field was stopped while you were not looking a sheet says so, with one tap to bring it back and one to read why your phone does that. On the phones most likely to do it there is also a quiet word beforehand, while everything is still working. SonoLune cannot stop your phone doing this and no app can. What it can do is notice, and say so.
- The rest of the release is invisible and is about work the app was doing while nobody was watching. A sleep timer was saving your whole session to storage once a second - about thirty-six thousand writes an hour, with the screen off, to keep one number current. Two animations kept running after the thing they animate had left the screen. The bottom bar and the moon in Mind were being redrawn sixty times a second to show something that changes about thirteen. Nothing should look different; the detail is below.
- A wash could still arrive somewhere it was being kept away from. While the field was correctly held off a protected screen, like a permission dialog or the Play Store, three things could still switch it back on: the wind-down beat at the end of a Presence session, a cue card handed to the overlay, and the light service being restarted by the phone in the middle of a purchase. The first two told the drawing layer a field was live when the whole point was that it was not, and each now asks whether a field is genuinely on screen first. The third had simply forgotten: the note saying a purchase was open was only ever held in memory, so a service the phone killed and restarted came back without it, read your saved light settings and drew the field over the sheet you were still reading. It is written to disk now, and it clears itself after a few minutes so a purchase that never finished cannot leave your light off.
- An answer in Help was not true, and it was the one about privacy. Asked whether SonoLune reads your screen or content, it ended: there is no accessibility service. There is one, and there has been since 1.9.3 - it is how the calm layer reaches the notification shade and the lock screen. It stays off until you turn it on and it never reads the text or the content of your screen. That is what the answer says now.
- The sleep timer was writing your whole session to storage once a second. Only one thing changes from second to second while a drift runs - the time left - but what was being written down each time was everything: every saved soundscape, every layer and volume, your history, your settings. An hour of drifting off came to something like thirty-six thousand writes, with the screen off, to keep one number current. It writes the number now. Nothing about the timer changes: close the app mid-drift and it still returns where it was, a finished drift still ends properly, and your saved rooms are still there.
- Two animations were still running after the thing they animate had gone. The small moon badge that greets the start of Night hides itself after about forty seconds - but its glow and its drift went on running for as long as the app was open, drawing nothing, on a screen they had already left. A running animation also keeps the phone producing frames, so a completely still screen was never actually still. They rest now, and start again the next time the badge appears.
- The bar at the bottom of the screen was being rebuilt sixty times a second to show a glow that changes about thirteen. It still breathes, exactly as before - that is deliberate and it stays. But four out of five of those rebuilds were the app producing precisely what it had just produced, on every screen, for the whole time the app was open. It now rebuilds when the glow actually moves.
- So was the moon in Mind, which is the first thing you see. Its aurora, its corona and the shadows under the moment cards are drawn on the breath, and the breath moves about eight times a second. They were being redrawn sixty. They follow the breath now instead of the clock, and the breath itself is unchanged.
- The Night page was listening to the entire app. Anything that changed anywhere - a sound toggled in another room, a focus session counting down once a second somewhere else - made the whole Night page redraw itself, even though none of it was on that page. It now listens only for the things it actually shows you.
- Feather mode settles the bottom bar as well, and the same card now tells you what your phone actually measured. The free switch in Help, for phones that find the app heavy, already dropped the frosted blur behind the bottom bar; it now also lets that bar hold still rather than breathe. Underneath it there is a new line: SonoLune has been checking how smoothly it runs on your phone, a few seconds after every launch, since 2.4.1 - and until now it used that number once, to decide whether to mention Feather, and then forgot it. It keeps it now, with the version it was measured on, so the line can say whether an update actually helped rather than leaving you to guess. It is a reading, not a verdict, and nothing acts on it by itself.
- Sound opens with less on a first visit. It was showing everything it had - about 595 words and 33 separate controls before you had touched any of them - in the room the strongest door opens into, and Fall asleep has already started playing by the time the page finishes drawing, so the thing you arrived for is handled before the list even starts. On a first sitting the far end of the room now folds behind a single row, More in Sound Atmosphere. One tap opens it, it stays open, and it stops folding for good once you have properly used Sound. Nothing was removed, nothing was renamed, and nothing moved to another room. If you are reading this you are updating, so you will not see it - the fold is only ever shown to somebody arriving for the first time.
- The first screen got three small repairs. The row of seven suite icons was being drawn under the very first screen you ever see - the one asking what you would like SonoLune to do first - so it could be tapped straight past without answering. It waits until you have crossed the door now. The small pill under the moon used to say "Shape SonoLune with us", which is a lot to ask of somebody who has owned the app for ninety seconds; it points at Feather mode instead, the switch that turns off the soft blurs and background motion for a phone that finds them heavy, and which until now was four sections deep in Help. And the "Help & Settings" button was clipping its own label at larger system font sizes.
- Focus and Presence were given the same treatment, for the same reason. Every room a first-run door opens was measured on the rendered screen rather than in the source, and Focus came out worst of all of them: 441 words and 38 controls, among them an offer of a quiet hour made to somebody who had never yet held a session. Its tail now folds behind one card, More in Focus Session, and a first sitting meets fewer than half of what was there. Presence had a different shape of the same problem. It arrives at a comfortable 176 words, and the moment you granted it permission it became 691 words and 81 controls with nine switches among them - by a distance the heaviest screen in the app, and it arrived in a single step. Depth, Timing and Around now wait behind one card until you have named an app for the field to settle over, which was the room's own advice all along: it was already telling you to start with Apps while drawing three panels of tuning for a field that had no shape yet. Choosing an app opens all of it at once. As with Sound, an existing install never sees any of this.
- Do Not Disturb is only switched off by SonoLune now if SonoLune switched it on. Night and Focus used to put back whatever Do Not Disturb had been doing when they started, so if a bedtime schedule was already on when Night began and ended in the morning, Night put it back on as it finished, and calls went unheard until somebody noticed. If it was already on when Night or Focus began, it is now left completely alone.
- New for rooted phones: SonoLune now works with Lune Bridge. With it switched on, Warm Light moves your phone's own Night Light, Screen Brightness goes darker than its usual minimum, and Quiet Field keeps the apps you choose from waking your screen. It has a page of its own, open to everyone: once the module is installed, the Light room leads you there.

## 2.4.3 · 13 Sep 2026

**The light that would not come back, and three refusals that never said so**

- Autumn and three new sounds arrived in the last release, and if you are coming from 2.4.1 or earlier they are new to you. Autumn is a room made of dry wind, cicada haze and falling leaves, free, and first on the Soundscapes shelf. Salt Air is an open coast with the sea underneath it. Evening Pond is still fresh water at dusk - the first still water this app has ever had, because every water in it was either falling or moving. And Cicadas is a high unbroken haze that joins Birds and Leaves as a third micro texture. Lay cicadas over leaves and the field names the season rather than a place: Autumn Wind, Autumn Forest, or on its own, the Turning Year. All three are free, and each says so until the first time you turn it on.
- The light comes back from the bulb and the tile, wherever you are. Switching it off from the shade always worked; switching it back on only worked while SonoLune was the app on screen, and there was nothing on the phone to say why. The two halves are not symmetrical in the code - taking the light away answers immediately, and putting it back asks nine questions first - so a fault could live in one direction for a long time without touching the other.
- The Night invitation reaches you at night. It checks, before it speaks, whether you are already in Night - which is right, because an invitation into a room you are standing in is noise, and noise gets a whole channel muted. But that answer is only written while SonoLune is running, and an invitation wakes when it is not. After one evening in Night the answer froze at "you are in Night" and stayed there until the app next ran, so the invitation was swallowed on exactly the evenings it exists for: the ones where you have been away. It arrived in the daytime because opening the app in the daytime wrote a fresh answer over it. An answer older than six hours is now treated as not knowing, and not knowing has never been a reason to take something away.
- The light one was a question held open and never closed. While you are away from SonoLune the light waits rather than settling somewhere it cannot yet identify, and that wait ends as soon as the app can name the screen you moved to. A phone that has never granted usage access cannot name it, ever - so the wait never ended, and everything that asked for the light back was answered with nothing. A scheduled Night arriving while the phone sat on the home screen was refused the same way. Waiting for an answer is now allowed to end, and with nothing on screen to protect there is no longer any reason to withhold.
- And a door that stays quiet on purpose can now say so. The app has recorded every one of these deliberate silences since the day the feature was written, and nothing has ever read that record - so a door aimed correctly and swallowed every night looked exactly like a door that never fired, while the panel went on promising the next one. The doors panel now says, in plain words, when the last one stayed quiet and what had the screen.
- The moon in Mind can bring your space back during Night. Holding your space while Night is running is still refused - holding it takes the light to nothing, and Night is a light state, so undoing that in silence is the one thing this app must not do. It says so now instead of doing nothing. The return, though, was being refused by the same line, and that is a dead end: entering Night leaves a held space held, so it is an ordinary evening, and the app tells you in six places that the moon is the way back. A control may be stopped from making things worse. It may never be stopped from being undone.
- A hint on the Sound cards was cut off mid-word. "Hold to preview" read as "Hold to previ...", because the two hints in that little bar split the row down the middle before either had been measured - so the shorter one sat in its half with room left over while the longer one was trimmed to fit. Not a large-font problem: the card grows with the font, so it was worst at the ordinary size. They take the width they ask for now.
- The thirty-second preview on the deeper sound controls now says something at the end of it. Reach for one of those controls and SonoLune opens it free for half a minute before anybody mentions money, and when that closes it asks whether you want to keep it. The asking only happened if you were still watching the Sound screen - so the ordinary ending, where you set the field and put the phone down, was that you heard the thing, the free preview was counted as used for the day, and nobody said a word. The ask now waits and arrives the next time you open Sound. It stays away while Night is holding the phone, stays away between ten at night and seven in the morning, and is dropped altogether after a day. It is still one preview per control per day; this is the sentence that was owed at the end of it, not a second window.

## 2.4.2 · 11 Sep 2026

**Autumn, three new sounds, and the moment that settled in silence**

- A moment can settle with its sound playing. This is the report 2.4.1 wrote down as unfixed, and the notes for that release said plainly that two attempts had failed because both were reasoned from reading rather than measured. The third was measured. The sound controller was never the thing that was wrong - it was refusing correctly, on layers that had never been loaded, and its honest answer, "no layers are chosen", was true and led nowhere.
- What was actually wrong was one flag, written in two places at once. Whether your space is HELD and whether it is ACTIVE are the same fact stored twice, and they are meant to move together. Three paths moved only one of them: letting go of a moment, starting fresh, and entering Night from a space that was already held. After any of those the app went on believing your space was put away - so the part that loads a moment's sound applied its light, saw a space it thought was resting, and returned without loading a single layer. Every moment applied for the rest of that session settled its colour over nothing.
- And a cold start cleared it, which is why this hid for so long. The flag is not written to disk, so opening SonoLune fresh always looks correct. The fault only appears once you have held your space and let it go in the same sitting - which is an ordinary evening, and impossible to catch by reading.
- The instrument that was supposed to name the cause could not speak where the fault was. 2.4.1 added a line that names which of six guards refused to start the sound, and put it behind a debug build - while every report of this came from the released app. So for three rounds the one thing that could have answered was, by construction, never running where the question was being asked. A moment that names a sound and arrives mute now says so in plain words, in the app everybody actually has.
- A tapped Hour lands in the room, not the catalogue. It used to open Arrivals - a full-screen page listing every moment, sitting above the rest of the app - so an invitation to one named room was answered with a shelf of ten, and the page covered the very light the moment had just laid down. That is the whole reason it could look as though nothing had happened. It opens Mind now, where the moment is already drawn as the active card, and where the light is visible behind it.
- Three lists of moments were asked whether they come back where you left them, and all three were wrong. The Space rail wrote your place down only when the app closed properly - and swiping SonoLune out of Recents is not closing properly, it is Android ending the process, so the ordinary way to leave was the one way that lost it. Both rails also abandoned your place whenever a moment was active, jumping to that moment's card instead. And Arrivals had no memory at all, opening at the top every time. All three now write your place as you scroll and return to it, and a moment you have open no longer overrules where you actually were.
- The Presence Field is an authority now, like Focus and Night. Those two turn away a scheduled arc that would fight them. The field could not: an arriving Mind arc quietly removed it and applied itself over the space where it had been. Mind has had a guard against exactly that since 2.0 - and the guard was dead, because the field was taken down a moment before it was asked. An arc carrying a space or a living moment waits now, and says so, and your quiet stays where it was. The first version of this fix was dead too, for a different reason, and a phone found it: the check read the arc's own description looking for a colon that the scheduler had already written as %3A. Every Mind arc that names a moment is written that way, so the guard was reading a string no arc has ever carried.
- A Night arc no longer walks into Night over a settled field. With the field up, the notification landed in Night and the light did not arrive until you tapped something - two things writing to one overlay, one of them undoing the other. It stops at the Field room instead and tells you Night is ready once you lift. Nothing about Night changes when the field is not up.
- The Night depth card was showing two numbers and contradicting itself. One is how deep the light is; the other is how far through the night the clock has gone. Both were right, and nothing said they measured different things. But the sentence underneath was wrong: at two-thirds through the night it still read "most of the depth still to come", when the curve had already delivered four fifths of its travel. It now names what is actually left, worked out from the same curve the number above it uses, so the two explain each other instead of arguing.
- The first screen says what it is before it asks what you want, and each door names the room it opens. The three things that separate SonoLune from everything in its category - no account, no ads, nothing leaves your phone - were written at the very bottom of that screen, under all eight doors and the button past them, which is the one place nobody reads: the whole point of the doors is that you tap one and go. They lead now. And every door carries the name of the room it opens, so tapping "Calm my screen" teaches you where the Light suite is instead of just arriving there.
- And SonoLune can finally ask what you think of it. The review ask was armed by two events in Focus and shown on one Focus screen - while the rule that decides who is worth asking has always counted nights and returns to a held space. So somebody who uses this app for sleep, which is what most people use it for, became eligible and was never once invited. It is asked from anywhere now, and never inside Night, never during a session, never over a held space or under the field.
- SonoLune can offer to come back to you again. Every way it reaches a person - the evening door, the focus return, the wake reminder, moments that arrive at their own hour - ships off, and that stays true: each is a real choice, asked for as it is flipped. But the screen that offers those choices lives inside the introduction, and tapping a door on the first screen finishes the introduction and takes you straight to the room, so the offer was never shown to anybody who came in the front way. An app for daily use had no way into anyone's day, a second time, by a different road than the first. It is offered on a later visit now - once, never in the first sitting, and closing it is a complete answer.
- Opening SonoLune while Night is running no longer starts your sound by itself. The audio service keeps one flag that answers a single question - is anything actually playing right now - and a freshly opened app asks it so the play button can match a soundscape that survived being swiped away. That flag was also being set by the call that merely opens the audio gate, and Night sends that call whenever anything changes. So a cold start in Night asked "is anything playing?", was told yes over complete silence, and did the honest thing with a dishonest answer: it brought back the last field you had. Permission to play is not playing, and it no longer says it is.
- Atmosphere Depth and Spaces were measured together for the first time, and the sound engine turned out to have been right all along. The two share one filter per layer on purpose, their curves add, and Spaces works with Depth at zero. What was wrong sat everywhere else: Spaces had never been introduced to the six shared systems its sibling belongs to. It was missing from the one place that hands back what is not yours, from the thirty-second window that lets you hear a refinement before it is ever offered, from the line that tells you what your field currently is, and from the moment an arc ends and the processing should let go.
- And one of those could trap you. If your support lapsed while the field was set to Room, the glass stayed - and the only control that could take it off had just been locked, including the button marked Open. A control may be stopped from making things worse. It must never be stopped from being undone. Taking the glass away is always allowed now, whatever your support says, and a lapse hands it back the same way it has always handed back Atmosphere Depth.
- Spaces is something you can hear before you are asked to pay for it. Every other refinement in Sound opens for thirty seconds in your own field, and only afterwards mentions support - the app does not sell what has not been felt. Spaces was the one paid control that had never been given that, so tapping Window or Room met a wall where the rule says a window. It opens now, the chip lights while it plays, and it settles out gently instead of stopping.
- The offer to let SonoLune reach you arrives while you are still here. It used to wait six hours, for a good reason - the question means more to somebody opening the app again than to a stranger a moment after an advert. The reasoning still holds; the assumption behind it did not. Almost nobody was coming back to be asked. It comes after five minutes now, in whichever room you are sitting in rather than only one, and never over playing sound - somebody five minutes into an ocean at bedtime is not to be interrupted. It still never comes between your first tap and what that tap does. The offer itself says the thing that earns it: there are no push notifications in SonoLune and no server that could send one, so nothing decides on your behalf that something is worth interrupting you for. The Hours joined it, which is the feature that actually arrives at an hour a moment was written for and was the one thing missing from a card named after exactly that. And once you have switched something on, the way out reads Done rather than Not now - that button dismisses, it never undid anything, so the only word it carried described the one thing tapping it would not do.
- Every door now names its room in full. Five of the eight said Light, Sound, Night, Focus and Field, while the rest of the app - the guide, the about page, the store - has always said Light Suite, Sound Atmosphere, Night Environment, Focus Session and Presence Field. The short forms read like a single setting. "Light" is a switch; "Light Suite" is a room with things in it. Somebody arriving for a screen dimmer had no way to learn the app was larger than the one control they came for, and the Calm my screen door now says so in its own line.
- Two doors now carry the words people actually searched for, and the app says its own name again. Blue light filter is in the store listing and appeared on no door at all - so somebody who installed for exactly that read eight cards and found nothing that said it. The sleep door had the opposite problem: a second store listing aimed at sound searches opens by saying most sleep apps hand you a finished track while SonoLune hands you the layers, and the door those arrivals landed on offered a finished track. And the first screen, which somebody reaches thirty seconds after tapping an advert, carried a crescent and three promises and never once said SonoLune. A picture is not a name. It sits beside the crescent now, in space that was already empty.
- A list that was asked to return to a place it can no longer reach stops at the end instead of the beginning. Both moment rails saved where you left them, and both refused to move at all if that place was further along than the list had so far been built - waiting for it to grow. A list like that does not grow on its own; it builds as it is scrolled. So it waited for something that could only happen if it moved, gave up, and left you at the very start, which is the furthest possible place from where you were. It walks now, as far as the list goes.
- Three sounds that were not there before. Salt Air is an open coast - wind that has crossed water, with the sea underneath it. Evening Pond is still fresh water at dusk, which this app had never had: every water in it was either falling or moving. And Cicadas is a high unbroken haze with distant birds behind it, taking its place beside Birds and Leaves. All three are free, like every other element.
- Lay Cicadas over Leaves and the field knows what you have made. With Wind it reads Autumn Wind; with the forest, Autumn Forest; with rain, Autumn Rain; and on its own, the Turning Year. The field reading has always named what you built rather than listing it, and this is the first time it names a season instead of a place. The light follows the same reading: what the field is called and how the light moves are written from one description of the room, so the two can never disagree about it.
- A new sound has to be cut to sit with the ones already there, and that is a measurement rather than a taste. There was no house level to aim at: the six elements SonoLune shipped with do not agree with each other, and the loudest and the quietest of them stand twenty-seven decibels apart. So each of the three was aimed instead at the narrow band it will actually be heard in, and checked there against whatever already lives in it.
- The cicada recording was ten seconds long, and the engine rejoins a loop every eight and a half. Ten seconds is a sound you would hear repeating inside a minute. The usual answer - laying a file over a slowed copy of itself - would have made it three times as busy, which is not the calm thing that was recorded. It was cut into overlapping slices instead, reordered, and crossfaded back into a minute that never repeats. That only works on a recording that never changes, and this one does not: across the whole of it the level moves by barely a decibel, and there is no call in it distinct enough to hear twice. The rebuilt minute measures the same in every band as the ten seconds it came from.
- Autumn is a room, and it leads the shelf. It is the dry end of the year heard from indoors, it is free, and it is built entirely from sounds that did not exist in SonoLune a week ago. It leads for that reason: a sound nobody opens is a sound nobody has.
- You can still hear the leaves underneath the wind, and that took measuring rather than taste. A room built around a rustle cannot be balanced by turning the rustle up, because leaves is by a wide margin the quietest thing SonoLune has and an element stops at full. It had to be balanced the other way about, by lowering everything else onto it - which is the opposite of how a mix is usually reached, and it is the difference between standing in autumn and listening to wind with something going on behind it.
- A room you had just chosen could not be carried into a focus session. The list that offers a field when a focus session starts was written out by hand, one entry per room, with a matching branch each - so Autumn arrived on the Sound shelf and simply was not there. That list is now built from a table, the eighteen branches have become one, and a test fails if a room is ever added without joining it. That is the fourth hand-written copy of the same set found in this release, and the fourth to have quietly fallen behind.
- A button on the Mind screen was losing the end of its own name. Lune Guard read as Lune Gu on a Galaxy F13 with nothing turned up, which is the part worth saying: this app has a whole suite of checks for the largest system font, and not one of them could see this, because it was wrong at the ordinary one. The card decided to put its buttons side by side whenever it had 286 pixels, and 286 was a guess about how much room the words need. It was about eight pixels short. It measures the words now, so the answer moves with the font instead of being fixed in advance.
- Two gated taps answered the wrong thing, and one of them answered nothing at all. The row that offers to let the light move with your sound carries a control that names support — and its tap did nothing whatsoever once the day's free look had been spent, which is most of the time, since the look is once a day. It asks now, and when the light has not yet been opened past SonoLune it says that instead, because support is not what is missing there. And tapping Open on the Spaces card raised the offer to pay. Open is the state you are already in: nothing was reached for, so nothing should have been asked for. It says what Spaces would add and leaves it there.
- That row also stopped underselling itself. It used to say the light could move with this, which describes a capability and sells nothing. It names the thing now — the Light Suite moving with the Sound Atmosphere — and says why it is worth having: every sound moves it differently. Rain is fine and constant, wind leans and passes and reaches furthest of the six, and a fire is the only thing in the app that genuinely flickers.
- The Hours could not be switched on at all, and the reason it gave was not the real one. It refuses when it has no moment it could bring you - which is right - but it could not tell "there is nothing here for you" apart from "nothing has been downloaded yet". On a phone that had never reached the feed, and on one where every arrival is already open, it said the same thing: that opening the other arrivals would give it hours. That was simply untrue. An empty library now means ask again later. The switch stays where you put it, nothing is scheduled while there is nothing to schedule, and the day a moment does arrive it starts working by itself. If the feed then turns out to hold nothing you can use, it closes honestly, with the real reason.
- The very first screen ran off the side of itself at the largest system font. The name beside the crescent - SonoLune, and Soundsoft Lab under it - was the one thing on that screen still sized to whatever width it wanted, so at the biggest font setting it pushed almost a hundred pixels past the edge of the phone. The door tiles below it carry a comment saying they were fixed for exactly this, once. The header was simply never looked at. Nothing moves at ordinary text sizes; it only wraps now when it cannot fit.
- And the card that offers Atmosphere Depth now speaks about the room you are in. It used to describe the control - more warmth, space and presence - which is the right sentence for somebody shopping and the wrong one for somebody already sitting in a field they like. In Autumn it now says that depth gives the wind a floor to move across and lets the leaves fall somewhere with walls. Nothing is gated differently and nothing is being sold: tapping it opens thirty seconds of the real thing in your own field, once a day, before support is mentioned at all. This is only a better reason to tap.

## 2.4.0 · 28 Aug 2026

**The light reaches the screen, and every room puts its door where you would reach for it**

- The light has not been reaching the screen. If you granted SonoLune the draw-over-apps permission at any moment other than the one it happened to be watching, the warm light stayed inside SonoLune and never reached your home screen, your reader, or anything else — while the Light suite showed the preset lit and correct. There was one dependable way out, and it was the extend-light row inside the Light suite. Anyone who never found that row had an app insisting the light was on while the screen said otherwise, and no reason to believe anything but the screen.
- The reason is small and worth saying plainly. SonoLune keeps a note of whether the light may leave the app, and that note was only ever updated at the instant the permission changed. Grant it a moment earlier, or simply open the app again afterwards, and the check found nothing had changed and stopped before the line that lets the light out. It is checked on its own terms now rather than on a change, so it corrects itself on any launch instead of waiting for a moment that had already passed.
- Opening SonoLune to sound in the room is one tap. The first screen offers Start the sound now beside Next, and it does exactly that: soft rain begins, the light comes up, and you arrive in the Sound room with the field already playing and the transport in front of you. It used to be eight taps and five hundred words of description before anything could be heard, and the way to skip that was a quiet line of text beside a bright button, wearing a name only someone who had already used the app would understand.
- Nothing begins on its own, and that has not moved. The tap is still yours, still deliberate, and still on a button that says what it will do. The only thing that changed is that your one choice is now spent on sound rather than on another page of words.
- The sleep timer is where someone reaching for it would look. It had been the very last thing in the Sound room, below the stereo control, the lab and the arriving packs — so the person most likely to want it, winding down and looking for a length, had to scroll past everything they had not bought in order to reach it. It sits directly under the layers now. Nothing was removed and nothing else moved; the order is simply the order somebody would actually use.
- And the moon says what it does. Holding the moon in Mind keeps your whole space — the light, the sound, the room — so you can come back to it exactly as you left it. That is the one thing in Mind belonging to no other room, and a long press has no way of announcing itself. It was explained during onboarding, which is precisely the part most people now skip. A single line under the moon says it instead, and retires the first time you hold it.
- A light you set could be switched off again by a change in screen brightness. Whenever SonoLune noticed the brightness had moved, it rebuilt the light from a list of parts written out by hand — and anything missing from that list quietly went back to nothing. The whole living field was missing from it: whether the light follows your sound, the mode it moves in, its colours, its speed. This had happened once before with the paper finish and been fixed by adding that one part to the list, which left the same trap set for every other. It is built by copying now, which cannot forget.
- Patronage that had not finished loading was read as no patronage at all. About a second into every start, while SonoLune was still working out what you support, a check ran that clears anything a non-supporter should not have — and wrote its answer down. So the light-follows-sound arrangement was switched off on your behalf, permanently, by something that had simply not read far enough yet. It now waits until the question can actually be answered. There is a real distinction underneath: "no" and "not yet" are not the same answer, and only one of them is a decision.
- A saved soundscape comes back with its name still lit. This is the fifth attempt at that one sentence, and the first four all worked on the wrong half. The name was being written down correctly and kept correctly — and then deleted a moment after the app opened, by the very check meant to protect it. That check asks whether the soundscape still exists before keeping its name, which is right; it was simply asking before the list of saved soundscapes had been read, so the answer was always no. It waits for the list now.

Whether the light moves with your sound is a different kind of choice, made on a different screen, and the preset was answering it anyway by carrying a silent no. Choosing a look no longer overrules something you set elsewhere.

It now appears once, for somebody who has not seen it, and opens the guide rather than replaying the introduction.

Each one, as you turn it on, says what it needs and why, asks for one thing at a time, and ticks each off as it lands.

Wanting something and having the bytes for it are two different facts, and only one of them was worth forgetting.

They are different kinds of thing: one is an hour you pick for this room, the other is a standing arrangement about moments arriving when they were written for.

## 2.3.0 · 21 Aug 2026

**The quiet reaches the sound, and everything it touches was measured**

- The quiet has a sound now, and its own place to live. The Presence room gains a section for it, kept apart from the three ways of making a screen duller — because these are heard rather than drawn, they are the only part of the field that reaches you with the phone face down, and they cost something the visual ones do not. Both are off unless you choose them, per app or for all of them.
- The talking blurs lays soft rain under a covered app, so speech stops carrying. The volume eases down brings the app itself to a little under half while the quiet is on it, and lets it back up when you leave. Two switches rather than one, because the choice is yours — though each is weaker alone and the room says so: a volume that drops by itself reads as a fault and gets turned back up, and a bed with nothing brought down under it can only ever make a room louder. With both on the rain holds exactly where it is while the app falls, so the total goes down and what falls furthest is the thing you were trying to leave.
- It never fights you for the volume. Touch the volume keys at any point and it lets go on the spot, keeps its hands off for the rest of the session, and hands back the level you chose. A control may be stopped from making things worse; it may never be stopped from being undone.
- The sound leaves with the screen, not with the clock. Switching to another app used to leave rain playing over it for as long as twelve seconds, and the volume held down with it. The field holds its own clock through a short grace on purpose — a two-second glance at a message must not hand back a fresh free-use window — but what is HEARD was waiting on that clock too, which is how a quiet meant to stay behind in one app ended up following you into the next. The light has always lifted the moment a chosen app is no longer in front. The sound does now as well, and comes back gently rather than abruptly if you step straight back in.
- The field works in YouTube again. It had been excluded outright, along with a handful of other Google apps, for a reason that is real and has no clean answer: the account switcher draws INSIDE whichever app called it, so the phone reports YouTube rather than the account screen, and there is no signal that says one is open. Excluding the whole app was the safe reading. It was also the app most people most wanted the quiet over, and the cost was total — no field, no levers, no sound, ever. That trade is now made the other way for this one app.
- The three ways of making a screen duller now work without the per-app field. They were written as shapes on the dwell ramp, so they belonged to chosen apps and nothing else — which meant every refinement since the per-app field arrived went to the people who had granted Usage Access, and the whole-screen quiet kept exactly what it shipped with. The edges, the words and the bright hooks now ride the depth you set instead of the one a clock produced. The two sound switches stay where they are, deliberately: they begin and end by entering and leaving an app, and a whole-screen version would simply play from the moment you switched it on.
- And an app can be told not to fade, or not to be released. Both have been real settings inside every app tuning since per-app arrived, and both were read every time the quiet was decided — but nothing anywhere could set them. They came from the universal switches, or from an attention pack. A pack that had ever written one off would have left an app that silently never quieted, with nothing on any screen to show why or put it back.
- And the guide finally mentions the rest of the room. It described the field, the delay and the apps you chose, and stopped there — so the five ways of making an app duller were discoverable only by scrolling the settings and reading switch labels. It names them now, and names the ones YOU have on, because a guide that lists features you have not chosen is a brochure rather than an explanation.
- It stays out of the way when you already have sound. A soundscape is already a bed, and laying a second one underneath is the one way this could make a room louder instead of quieter. With anything of your own playing, or with Night holding the audio, the lever simply does not arrive.
- Rain, and not the brown noise everyone asks for — including us. Speech carries between 500 Hz and 4 kHz, and the consonants that make words legible sit at the top of that. Brown noise has almost nothing left up there. It is the loveliest thing to sit inside and the worst at this particular job.
- A Visiting Hours arc brings it along with everything else. A visit is the one moment consent is unambiguous — chosen, for these apps, for this long, before the pull started — so it arrives at full strength. It is still borrowed, and still handed back at the end.
- It cannot mute an app, and would not if it could. Android gives no app control over another app's volume; only the phone itself can do that. And a hard mute would be the first thing in SonoLune that seals something shut, which is not what any of this is for.
- Keep drifting now keeps you where you were. The breath at the threshold can only be offered by bringing SonoLune to the front, so asking the question already interrupted the drift — and two of its three answers mean "I am staying here", yet both left you sitting in SonoLune looking at the one screen the moment was not about. They hand the screen back now. Only stepping out has a destination, and only stepping out goes there.

Arming one used to set the chosen apps aside entirely, so agreeing to twenty minutes of a single feed left every other app you had chosen with no field at all until it ended — the opposite of what agreeing to it meant. The visit adds its app to yours now. Its app runs at the arc's full strength; yours keep the field on your own settings, and nothing of yours pauses.
