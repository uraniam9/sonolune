# SonoLune 2.1.0

**A lighter SonoLune, and gentle new ways it meets your day** · 31 Jul 2026

A calm, free release that lets SonoLune sit more easily in a real day. Feather mode lets any phone set down its heaviest visual work, so the app stays smooth even on modest hardware. Your rhythm quietly mirrors the time you have spent here, a private record with no goals and no streaks. Night watch checks that this particular phone will let sound and light run undisturbed until morning, and opens the right settings for you when it will not. A gentle wake is easier to find now, resting in Night and on the app icon itself, and a small painted night sky for your home screen, now razor-sharp at every size, carries tonight's moon with its phase and its light, your own dusk or Night window, and three quiet doors, always a tap away. And Field backup lets you carry your whole space, moments, soundscapes, alarms, and light, out to one file, saved straight to this device or shared to wherever you keep things, and bring it back on a new phone, with nothing ever sent anywhere on its own. For the apps that pull hardest, Fade to quiet can now settle in a warmer, more personal way and, when it lands, offer a single breath and a gentle way to step out, returning quietly now and then if you drift on. Night's timing grew a quiet set of levers: every automatic night, scheduled or sun-timed, now ends like a slow sunrise instead of a switch; Friday and Saturday can keep their own later hours; dusk and dawn can be nudged to fit evenings that run long; one tap stands the whole automation down for a single night; and a night you switch on by hand is never taken from you. Support is gently reshaped, too: everyone who ever supported keeps everything forever, the one-time tiers stay one-time, an optional monthly patronage now carries the sessions still arriving, and a quiet seven-day Guest Pass lets anyone walk the full field once — and every one-time tier now begins with months of that patronage included, three to twelve by tier, so the sessions keep arriving from day one. The quiet ways SonoLune can reach you were rebuilt on the same timing its Lunar Arcs use: a door at the hour you chose, a moment arriving at the hour it was made for, and the occasional soft word now come when they say they will, and tapping one opens the room it named rather than wherever you happened to be. Both switches sit together in one place now, each cadence stated beside the other, so you can see exactly what you are agreeing to. Everything else here is free, off until you reach for it, and adds to SonoLune without changing anything you already have.

## Feather mode

- Stills the decorative motion across the app, the breathing bars, shimmers, and drifts, while every functional fade stays gentle rather than snapping.
- Releases the frosted-glass blurs: the navigation bar, Mind's glass buttons and atmosphere veil, the Focus session card and its session lock, the completion card, and more. Each surface keeps its own colour and depth; only the frost goes.
- Free for everyone and default off. It changes how much the screen carries, never what SonoLune does, and turning it off restores everything instantly.

## When SonoLune reaches you

- A door at your hour, The Hours, and the quiet invitations now arrive the same way a Lunar Arc does, handed to the phone's own timekeeper. An hour you choose is the hour it comes.
- Tapping one opens what it named: settled Night, a focus hour, a gentle morning, or, for an Hour, the whole moment at once, its light and its sound together.
- An invitation that has already arrived is never taken back. It rests where it landed until you deal with it, even if you open SonoLune in the meantime.
- The Hours keeps exactly the cadence it promises: at most one an hour, only a few in a day, and never between 11pm and 7am.
- Both switches now sit together under When SonoLune reaches you, so each cadence can be read beside the other before you agree to either. The Hours moved here from Arrivals, where it had only ever lived because that is the room its moments come from.
- A quiet word about your reflections lands on them if you have written any, and in Focus if you have not, with a line saying that a reflection is written at the end of a focus session.
- Nothing speaks into the room you are already in. Night does not arrive during Night, a focus invitation does not interrupt a focus session, and none of them arrive into a space you have held. Checked at the moment it would land, not when it was set — a day can pass in between.
- The Night door asks for the light window before it opens, since Night is a room made of light, and closes itself again if that permission is later withdrawn. Focus and a gentle wake never touch the screen, so they never ask.
- The Hours waits for a clear moment. It will not be set while your space is held, the Presence Field is settled, a focus session is running, or Night already owns the screen — and it says which, rather than simply refusing.

## Lune Guard, honestly grounded

- The eye resets are now named for exactly what they are: digital eye strain relief, the widely shared comfort practices, the 20-20-20 habit, blink breaks, near-far shifts, gentle neck release, palming, that eye-care organizations commonly suggest for screen use.
- A grounding note now closes the suite: Soundsoft Lab did not design these practices and is not an eye-care professional, SonoLune only carries them. They are comfort, not treatment; skip anything uncomfortable, and let persistent strain be seen by an optometrist or ophthalmologist.
- Three trusted sources sit right below it, the American Academy of Ophthalmology, the American Optometric Association, and Mayo Clinic, each one tap away in your browser.
- Every number in the suite was checked against the literature; the few that could not be traced to a real source were rewritten into plain, honest language.

- The colour slider beneath a moment now moves whichever kind is playing, your own or one that arrived, rather than only the ones built in. It used to appear over a moment it could not move, and then vanish for the next one.
- And a moment you kept can be let go from the rail itself with a long press, not only from the Arrivals room. A moment that arrived is never yours to remove, so it never offers.
- Keeping a moment now suggests a name for it, read from the hour and the light you are actually in — Ember Hour, The Desk at Dusk, Late Hours — so a saved room is recognisable weeks later. It is only ever a suggestion; type over it freely.
- A delivered moment can now carry a living field of its own, so an arrival can breathe rather than only settle. Only the two slow kinds ever arrive: nothing sent to your phone can make the screen flash, by construction.
- And tapping a moment while the Presence Field is settled now says so, instead of appearing to do nothing at all.
- Delivered moments now answer the same gestures your own do: tap an active one to rest its sound, tap again to bring it back, and hold it to set its light down. They shared a rail with different rules before, which read as something broken rather than something different.
- The Hours, off until you ask for it: a moment can arrive at the hour it was made for, and tapping it settles the whole thing at once. At most one an hour and only a few in a day, never between 11pm and 7am, and nothing is counted — a moment you ignore leaves no trace at all.
- Atmosphere memory has been retired, and nothing you shaped is lost. It used to ask "Update this moment?" and then quietly change a curated moment underneath you; keeping your own moment now does that job out loud instead. Every tweak you had remembered arrives as a saved moment of your own, named for the atmosphere it belonged to, resting in your rail where you can keep it or let it go.
- A run of nights that ends. Where a session arrives, it can be begun and then simply finished: it shows where you are, never a streak, closes with its own words, and stepping out part-way is offered as plainly as carrying on.
- And the first minute. When the window over your screen is already open, the field now arrives by itself once onboarding settles, rests for a minute, and lets go on its own — so the thing SonoLune actually does is felt before it is ever described. Nothing is asked for at that moment; if the window is closed, nothing happens at all.
- The door at your hour rests in Help and Guides, under This device, beside Feather mode and Night watch — it shapes how SonoLune behaves on this phone, spans Night, Focus and a gentle wake at once, and is meant to be left on rather than treated as an experiment.
- And they now actually arrive. Both had been noting the hour they were aiming at as though it had already happened, so the next time SonoLune opened it cancelled the waiting invitation and declined to make another. They were quietly unable to reach you at all.
- Both the arriving moments and the door at your hour now ask for notification permission the moment you switch them on, and stay honestly off if you decline. And the hour you choose is now kept to the minute, where it used to quietly fall back to the hour.
- A door you set for a chosen hour now asks, once, for permission to arrive on time. Android delays reminders for apps that have not been given exact timing, so a door set for nine could wander well past it. You can decline and the door still opens — a little later than you asked, and we say so plainly rather than letting it drift without explanation. The arriving moments never ask, because a moment that comes sometime around three is honest about itself.
- And when either of them arrives, it now actually shows itself: a quiet banner, once, with no sound and no vibration. Both were previously set so far down that they landed in the notification shade unseen — which for a door you chose the hour for is the same as never arriving at all.
- A door at your hour, for the rituals themselves: Night, Focus and a gentle wake can each offer a quiet invitation at a time you choose, and each is asked for separately. Nothing arrives on a day the thing already happened — settle into Night at seven and nine o'clock stays silent. Nothing is counted, there is no streak to keep, and a day you skip simply passes.
- And the moments themselves now keep their own hours honestly: one named for eleven at night carries eleven-at-night light, warm and dimmed with the blue drawn down, where before it wore the same light as three in the afternoon.

## The Lune Field, awake and at rest

- Feather mode now reaches the live wallpaper. It used to stop at the app's edge while the Lune Field kept a motion sensor and a drifting redraw running on the home screen, the one surface that is always on.
- The field also rests on its own under your phone's battery saver, and when your system settings ask for animations to be removed.
- Resting is not switching off. The sky, the moon and its true phase are all still drawn and still keep their hours; only the things that cost continuously stop. It is the same picture, holding still.
- The four skies finally do what they are named for. Mist lays a real band of haze across the lower sky, drifting sideways over hours, so the moon sits above it. Winter gathers a thin ice-light at the top of the sky and gives the brightest few stars a cold, faceted glint. Ember lifts a slow hearth-glow from just below the horizon, breathing over hours as if something warm is burning out of frame. They had been colour tints alone, which was too little for a sky with a name.
- None of them falls, flashes, or races: they are drawn from the clock and the stars already there, they add no timer and no extra frame, and each one stands still the moment the field rests.
- A double tap on the sky can now open the Mind hub, if you ask for it. A single tap is always ignored, so your home screen, its icons and its swipes keep working exactly as they do now. Off until you turn it on.
- Passing lights, a new free choice in the Lune Field: every few hours one slow light crosses the sky and is gone in under two seconds, rare enough that catching one is luck. It never flashes, it never draws in daylight, it rests whenever the field rests, and it costs nothing at all when nothing is passing. Off until you invite it.

## The Almanac, and a Settle that reads the hour

- The Almanac is a small free page for the sky itself: tonight's moon and how much of it is lit, whether it is growing or thinning and what tomorrow will look like, your own sunrise and sunset, the length of the day and which way the year is turning. It is worked out on this device from the date alone, with no network and no location service.
- It is free, and it stays free. It is arithmetic that will keep being right for years with nobody maintaining it, and nothing that runs itself should ever be charged for by the month.
- Settle in Light is no longer one fixed preset. It now composes itself from the actual hour, the season, tonight's moon, whether you have just come out of Focus, and how you have been using the suite, so a morning Settle and a midnight Settle are genuinely different rooms.
- Each Settle names what it made and why in one plain line, so it reads as a considered choice rather than a dice roll, and it can still be undone in a tap.
- Settle now reaches the whole suite, not only the sliders. It composes a Colour Veil for the hour (free for everyone), asks for the paper grain when the hour wants it, and at dusk and through the night can set a very slow, very dim Living Field beneath it. It never composes anything that flashes.
- A quiet note now rests above Settle the first few times you pass it, saying plainly what it weighs and that it composes rather than recalls. A cross closes it for good.
- A tap is now visibly worked out rather than instant: a light sweeps once across the button while the hour, the sky and what you have just been doing are weighed in turn, and the light lands as the sweep finishes. Reduced motion skips straight to the result.
