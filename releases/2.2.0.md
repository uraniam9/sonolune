# SonoLune 2.2.0

**The field moves like what you are hearing, and the way in is one tap** · 12 Aug 2026

The Living Field stopped lurching when you change what is playing, and the reason is worth saying plainly: how FAR the light breathed was being multiplied into how FAST it breathed, so depth counted twice, the wash snapped sideways every time you chose a new sound, and — because that error compounded with every minute — a field left running for ten minutes was changing brightness several times a second. It moves with the sound now, and only ever moves its depth. Sound sync itself has come out of hiding: it lived in the Light suite under a speed slider, which is the one place that cannot know what you are listening to, and it now sits in Sound where the layers are chosen, saying what the light will make of THIS atmosphere and showing you, in a small strip that breathes exactly as the real field does. And the Presence Field finally opens on the thing it is for: the per-app quiet sat at the bottom of a long page behind a permission, an app picker, two timing pickers and four switches, so most people met a whole-screen wash, decided that was what it was, and left. Three one-tap sets now choose the apps and the timings together — and the clock behind them survives the app being killed, which it did not before.

## Highlights

- Seven rooms now carry their own living light. Natural Forest leans slowly between canopy shade and the green a leaf turns with the sun behind it; Firelight runs quicker between deep ember and the flare above it; Dawn moves once, from blue hour into first gold. A room composes the colour and what you are hearing supplies the weather.
- The Long Dusk is built to be fallen for — three colours rather than two, last gold to rose to the blue before dark, and it never turns back. It opens free for thirty seconds, field and all.
- The light can follow your sound, and it now lives where the sound is. Choose your layers and the Sound suite tells you what the light will make of them — an ocean as a slow swell across the whole field, rain as fine quick movement — with a small strip beneath that breathes exactly as the real thing will.
- And that light moves with what you are actually hearing. Rain, a hearth, a forest and an ocean each have their own pace and depth rather than one shared pulse, and the field takes its movement from the room you built.
- The Presence Field opens on the thing it is for. Three one-tap sets — the scrolling ones, the watching ones, the talking ones — choose the apps and set the timings together, with times that have reasons: ten minutes for feeds, fifteen for video, thirty for conversation.
- Every chosen app can keep its own timing and its own quiet. A feed and a film and a conversation are not the same visit, so they no longer share one setting — tap any app to give it its own.
- Listening counts as time spent here. Light and Sound were invisible to your rhythm, so someone who came every evening for the sound was told they had never been.
- The first atmosphere is one tap in rather than eight pages away, and the onboarding names all seven rooms — Field and Arc were never mentioned at all.
- Rooms you keep, rooms delivered to you and rooms that shipped with the app now stand apart in the moments rail, and a finished focus session offers a quiet way to hand SonoLune to someone.
- Included Lab months grew to three, nine and twenty-four. The months grew rather than the prices, so nothing costs more and nothing was taken from anyone.
- SonoLune finally notices a new version. The check asked Google Play and only Google Play, which answers "nothing new" for any build not installed from the store — so across many releases the notice never once appeared.
- And the quiet you set is the quiet you get: a per-app field now lets go the moment you leave, its clock survives the app being killed, and "Field depth" in the notification is the depth rather than a shaped figure that could never reach 100.
- The quiet reaches full depth again. It had been capped so the card explaining the release could be read through it — a workaround that made every deep field shallower than you set it. The card is drawn above the wash now, so nothing needs reading through.
- Three new ways to make a screen dull without covering it: the edges close in on what is arriving at the corner of your eye, the words lose focus while the screen stays lit and every tap still lands, and the bright hooks — badges, likes, buy buttons — lose their colour while text keeps its own.
- Night stops sealing the app. Sound, Mind, Arcs and the Light suite are all reachable while Night runs; what is held is only what would actually break it, and each one says so where you meet it rather than at a locked door.
- Reading Light: a new focus light built to imitate a printed page — paper grain, warm off-white, no glare — for reading something long without the hour costing you what a screen hour usually does.

## The field and the sound

- Reported: with sync on, choosing a different element in the sound atmosphere made the Living Field lurch. The guess that came with the report — that depth and speed were being applied twice — was exactly right.
- How far the light breathes and how fast it breathes are meant to be separate things: one is loudness, the other is tempo. A single line multiplied the first into the second, so a deep field was also a fast one, and depth was counted twice over.
- That is also why it snapped. Where the light sits in its breath was worked out as time multiplied by speed, so changing the speed part-way through relocates it instantly — like recalculating where a runner would be forty minutes in if they had been going faster all along. They arrive somewhere else, at once.
- And it compounded. Because the tempo was multiplied by something that itself rises and falls, the effect grew with every minute the field ran: about eleven brightness changes a second after ten minutes, and far more after an hour of Night.
- This app holds itself to under three flashes a second, deliberately below the band that provokes photosensitive seizures, and has refused strobe outright twice. That ceiling watches things declared as rates. This one was hiding inside a position, so nothing was watching it. It is gone, and the speed calculation can no longer be handed the loudness at all.
- The sound still moves the light — that part was always separate and always correct. It moves its depth, and never its speed.
- Every room also breathes the same amount now. In the smooth ambient mode the field's own drift already spent the whole brightness allowance, so the sound's breath landed on a full glass and clipped flat at the peak — and the rooms, which should differ most at their widest, all flattened against the same ceiling. Drift and breath share it now: what differs is how the swing divides, not how big it is.

## Where the sync belongs

- Sync with the sound atmosphere is one of the quiet centrepieces of this app, and it lived in exactly one place: the Light suite, inside the ambient system, below a speed slider, worded as a setting. Reachable and unfindable at the same time.
- What it does is a property of what you are LISTENING to, and the only screen that knows that is the Sound suite. So it is there now, at the foot of the field reading — the card that already describes the room you have built — beside the layers being chosen.
- It is one switch in two places, not two switches. Set it in either and the other already agrees; there is no second copy to fall out of step.
- It says what the light will do with the atmosphere actually in front of you. An ocean is a slow swell across the whole field; rain is fine, quick movement through it; a fire is irregular and close. That sentence is worked out from the movement itself rather than looked up per room, so a combination nobody planned for still describes itself honestly.
- The Light suite says the same thing now. It used to tell every atmosphere it would "swell, pulse, and move more noticeably" — the identical-rooms problem the living field exists to end, written into the label above it.
- And a small strip breathes beneath it, so the choice can be seen before it is made. It is drawn with the same movement as the wash on your home screen rather than an impression of it, which is the difference between a sample and a picture. Shown whether the switch is on or off, and to everyone: there is no charge for looking.
- It holds still, as a single frame, under Feather mode or the system's "Remove animations" setting.
- And it opens the field itself. It used to set a flag and nothing else, so unless a Living Field happened to already be open in Light it did nothing at all — a switch offering to do something it could not do alone. It now opens one, tuned to be worth seeing: the mode that slides between colours rather than stepping, and a palette with enough travel in it that the movement the sound drives is actually visible.
- Switching it off closes that field again — but only if it was ours to close. A field you opened yourself in Light stays exactly as you left it, and only the following stops. An app that undoes your own setting is worse than one that does too little.
- It steps aside when something else owns the light. The Presence Field holds the whole overlay while it is settled, and a resting SonoLune holds it too — so instead of a switch that cannot act, it names what is holding the light and what would free it. A control that does nothing reads as broken and leaves you nothing to do about it.
- And it can be seen before it is decided on. Thirty seconds of the real thing — the field open, following your sound — inside your own room. When the window closes the field closes with it and the choice is offered then, rather than the light simply going out with no explanation.

## The way into the Presence Field

- A field over your whole screen is a mood. A field over the two apps you lose evenings to is the point of the room — and that was the part sitting at the bottom of a long page, behind a permission, an app picker, two timing pickers and four switches.
- Every one of those is a reasonable thing to be able to change and an unreasonable thing to have to understand first. So most people met the Presence Field as a whole-screen wash, decided that was what it was, and left.
- Three sets now sit near the top, before any of that: the scrolling ones, the watching ones, the talking ones. One tap chooses the apps that are actually on your phone, sets the timings, turns on the parts that make a field do anything, and offers a way straight to the button that starts it.
- The times are arguments rather than round numbers. Feeds quiet after ten minutes, roughly where an intended visit has become an unintended one, and long enough that a real errand finishes first. Video after fifteen, because the unit of watching is longer than the unit of scrolling and ten would land mid-thought. Conversation after thirty, with the most forgiving grace, because there is another person in it and the switching back and forth is real.
- Nothing is replaced. A second set adds to the first, apps you chose yourself stay, and a set whose apps you already have says so rather than reporting a success that changed nothing.
- And the sets stay side by side. Choosing one used to make the other two disappear, so wanting a field over both your feeds and your video was not something you could say — and the set you had picked could not be undone except by removing its apps one at a time. Each carries its own way out now, and taking one back leaves your timings and switches alone, because by then they may be yours rather than the set's.
- Each set also shapes the quiet differently, because the categories are not lived in alike. Scrolling happens in your hand, so it leans on the cue you can feel. Video is propped up or across a room where a haptic reaches nobody, and its sessions run long enough for the living grain to be worth its battery. Messaging keeps a readable paper grain and stays silent — a chime in the middle of a conversation is heard by the room and reads as a notification, which is the one thing this app should never be mistaken for.
- And the way in ends where it should. The card offered to carry you to the button that starts the field; now it starts it.
- Each chosen app now keeps its own timing and its own quiet, rather than every app sharing one. That is what the sets were promising and could not deliver: choosing a second set used to retune the first, so a feed ended up on a film's fifteen minutes. Tap any app to open or change its own — how long you have, how long a detour is forgiven, how the quiet settles, and each of the four switches. An app you add by hand simply follows the settings, exactly as before, until you decide otherwise.
- And what it keeps, it keeps. The tuning survives leaving the page and closing the app, and an app with a half-written record falls back to your settings rather than to a blank one — which would have meant a screen dimming the instant it opened.
- The permissions are met where they are needed rather than assumed. Without draw-over-apps there is no field at all, and it points at the card the page already shows. Without Usage Access the set is still saved and correct — it simply cannot tell your apps apart yet, so it offers to open that instead of quietly covering everything.
- It asks for nothing it does not use. There is no notification permission here: the release cue is drawn over the app, not sent to the shade. A setup that collects a permission it will never use is how an app stops being trusted with the ones it does.

## The clock that kept starting over

- "Quiet begins after thirty minutes" counts while you are in another app, so the whole thirty minutes is spent with SonoLune in the background — and that clock lived only in memory.
- If the app was killed in that time, by a battery manager, a low-memory moment or a restart, the clock silently began again from nothing. No message, no sign. The quiet you were waiting for simply never arrived, and the longer your setting the more likely it was: half an hour has thirty times the window to be interrupted that a minute does.
- It is written down now, and picked back up where it stopped — but only once the app you are in is confirmed to be the one it was counting. A saved clock can never be applied to a screen it does not belong to.
- And it deliberately refuses to resume a clock already past due. There is no way to tell "the app was killed two minutes before the quiet was owed" from "you closed it an hour ago and have just come back", and one of those, resumed, would darken the screen the instant you opened it. A second full window is a small cost; that is not.
- It also refuses a clock from the future, which is what a timezone change or a corrected phone clock looks like from in here.

## The quiet goes as deep as you set it

- Reported from a phone: at full depth, with the field covering system surfaces, you could not see anything — including the card that tells you the screen has let go.
- The card was drawn by the app. The wash, in that mode, is drawn by the accessibility service, and a window of that kind sits above every app window by design. Nothing on the app side can win that ordering; it is not a matter of trying harder.
- The first repair capped the wash so the card could be read through it. That made it survivable rather than right, and it quietly made the quiet shallower than you had asked for.
- So the service that is on top now draws the card itself. It arrives above its own wash, where it was always meant to be — and with nothing left to read through, the cap is gone. A chosen app can go back to being genuinely hard to use, which is the entire point of setting it that way.
- The eye-break card goes the same way. It is fired by an alarm and never passed through the app, so it had none of this — it was drawn, counted as shown, and seen by nobody whenever the field was deep.
- And the card leaves with the field. Step out of the chosen app and the wash and the card go together, rather than the card staying behind to describe a screen that is already clear.

## Three more ways to make a screen dull

- The quiet already dims and greys. These three do something different: they make a screen less worth staying on without covering it, blocking it, or interrupting you. All three are off unless you switch them on, for one app or for all of them.
- The edges close in. A feed does not catch you with what you are reading — it catches you with what is arriving at the edge of your eye, where movement is noticed first and decided about last. So the quiet closes from the sides, and the clear part narrows the longer you stay. It meets the pull where it starts rather than where it lands.
- The words lose focus. On Android 12 and newer the screen behind the field can genuinely be blurred, which is the one thing this app can do to another app's own pixels. It stays lit, warm and pleasant to look at, and simply stops being readable. Nothing is covered and every tap still goes through. It arrives late on purpose: an errand you meant to run must not become unreadable halfway.
- The bright hooks dull. Badges, likes, streaks and buy buttons are red for a reason — a saturated warm patch is close to the fastest thing an eye can find. A faint wash in the opposite direction takes the colour out of those while leaving text readable, so it works on what is pulling at you rather than on what you are reading.
- Where the blur is unavailable — an older phone, or a battery saver that has switched it off — the narrowing edges carry it instead. That is why the two were built together rather than one.
- And the three one-tap sets each use a different combination, because the categories are not read alike. A feed is skimmed, so it gets all three: making it hard to read costs almost nothing there and removes almost everything. Video gets the closing edges alone — that takes away the autoplay rail and the next-up thumbnails without touching what you sat down to watch, and blurring a film is not reducing stimulation, it is destroying the thing itself. Messaging gets the dulled hooks alone: what pulls you back to a chat is the unread badge, not the words, and an app that blurs a message while a person waits for an answer is not calm, it is rude on your behalf.

## Night stops sealing the app

- Night used to shut every other room. The tabs dimmed and turned your tap away with a line about coming back in the morning, which answers "can I look at this" with "no" when the honest answer is "yes, and here is what it would cost".
- So the holding moved inward, onto the things that actually break Night rather than onto the doors.
- Sound opens. Night is not a silent mode — it has its own hum, made by the same engine an atmosphere plays through, and two things cannot drive one engine. But that hum is off unless you turn it on, so for most people Night was holding a channel it never touched. It holds it now only while it is using it.
- And an atmosphere already playing now carries straight through into Night, instead of being stopped as Night begins. That stop made sense while Night owned all sound; it stopped making sense the moment the Sound suite opened in Night, because the app would have taken your rain away and then let you start it again from the room next door. With the hum on it still stops, and should — the hum is what Night is for.
- And it comes out of Night still playing. Letting sound survive Night exposed an older assumption on the way back: the app restores your layers after a mode ends, deliberately without starting them, and that restore had only ever met a field that was already stopped. Run against one still playing, it marked the sound paused without touching the sound — so the button said paused while the room said otherwise, and with a moment it came back as something that could not be started at all. It now leaves a playing field alone, because there is nothing to restore to something that never stopped.
- The Light suite opens, and is held. Night IS a light setting — warm, dim, without blue, until morning — so the controls there would be editing the room you are standing in. The page stays legible behind a soft blur, with one button: open Night. A warning you can tap past would let the protection be undone without anyone noticing.
- The Presence Field opens, and opening it ends Night. Both hold the whole screen, so there is no state where both run. It asks first, because Night ending gives your brightness back and lets go of the morning release — far too much to hang on a mis-tap in a dark room.
- Mind opens and reads normally. The moments still wait, because a moment sets light and sound together, which is the middle of what Night is holding.
- Arcs open. A scheduled atmosphere set for eleven at night is FOR being in Night, so it keeps its appointment now instead of being turned away — and so does anything that only opens a page. What still waits for morning is anything that applies an environment. And a scheduled arc will never end your Night for you: the tab asks first, and an arc cannot ask.
- A focus session still holds everything, and that lock is right. A session already carries its own light and sound intent, and is not the moment to go exploring.
- The bar says which of these is which, without turning anyone away. Night used to dim every tab but its own, which was honest while it sealed them all and became misleading the moment it stopped. Now it dims only what Night genuinely has a claim on — Light, the Field, and Sound while the hum is running — and at two different weights: the one door that will actually refuse you is dimmer than the rooms that are simply not yours until morning. Everything else reads exactly as it does by day.

## A light for reading, and your own lights in Focus

- Reading Light is new, and sits at the top of the focus light chooser. It is built to imitate a printed page rather than to look pleasant on a screen.
- What a page actually is: a warm off-white that reflects the room rather than emitting at you, very little blue, a matte surface that scatters glare instead of mirroring it, and a fine tooth for the eye to rest on. A screen is the opposite of every one of those, which is why an hour with a paperback costs nothing and an hour of the same words on glass does.
- So it leans on the paper grain, sets an off-white paper tone rather than an amber wash, and stops the blue reduction short of where black text begins to lose its bite. Contrast is legibility; a warm blur is not restful, it is tiring.
- And the chooser finally offers your own. It listed ten of ours and none of yours, so the only way to work under a preset you had shaped and named was to open the Light suite, apply it, and come back. They are listed now, each saying what it does.

## Reported, and repaired

- The Light suite went blank in Night. It had always faded itself to nothing there, which nobody saw while the tab was refusing entry — and the moment the page became reachable, that fade was all you got. It stays on screen now, softened behind the hold, so there is something to look at and a sentence saying why it is not yours until morning.
- Opening the Field tab in Night asked whether to end Night before showing you anything, which turns looking into a decision. Reading that page takes nothing from Night; entering the field takes the screen. The question moved to the button that does that.
- And a moment tapped in Night said a session was holding your space, when there was no session — there was Night, and the way out of it is different.
- The Sacred Night hum could be switched on while an atmosphere was already playing. They are made by the same sound engine, which is the whole reason Sound is held while the hum runs — but that lock only ever pointed one way, so the switch stayed live and turning it on would have silenced what you were listening to, by a rule you had no way to see. It points both ways now, and can always be switched off.
- A Suggested Focus moment scheduled inside Night was turned away. It is a focus session wearing a Mind name — it sets the minutes, the light, the sound and Do Not Disturb, and lands you on Focus without touching anything Night is holding. A scheduled focus was already allowed; its twin is now too. Mind moments that genuinely set a room still wait for morning.
- The release card now stays while the screen is genuinely hard to use. Six seconds is right for a cue over a screen you can still work in, and wrong over one that has been blurred past reading, narrowed at the edges and drained of its buttons — where that card is the only thing left that does anything. Fading it out there left you looking at an app you could not use with nothing to tap. It waits now, and still goes the moment you tap it, leave the app, or the field lifts.
- And the per-app settings sheet says that it keeps what you set. Every control there has written itself the instant it was touched since the day it shipped, so nothing was ever lost to a back gesture — but a sheet with no Save and no word about it does not read as "already saved", it reads as "you have not finished". It says so now, and offers a Done that closes rather than a Save that would be a lie about how it works.
- And back no longer refuses to leave the Night page. That was consistent while Night sealed the app, since nowhere else would take you either — and it became the one gesture still forbidden once every tab would. Leaving the page has never ended Night; it carries on.
- The switch that lets the light follow your sound could be left on with no way to turn it off. When something else took the light — the field, Night, a resting app — the whole row was replaced by a sentence explaining that, and the switch went with it. A setting that was on became invisible, and came back the moment the light was free. The explanation stays now, and so does a way to switch it off. A control can be stopped from making things worse; it must never be stopped from being undone.
- A saved soundscape now remembers whether the light was moving with it. It kept your layers and forgot that, which for anyone using the living field meant the room you brought back was never quite the room you kept.
- Paper and Ember could not be chosen when the quiet was set to begin instantly. That was true once — all three settled to the same near-black at full depth — and it stopped being true when each was given its own colour there. The reason had gone and the lock had not, so nothing was wrong with them except that the app would not let them be picked.
- The one-tap sets gave more than the free field allows. A tap on the scrolling ones chose every feed on your phone at ten minutes, while the picker directly below refused a second app and the timings refused anything but instant — giving with one hand and refusing with the other, on one screen. A set now fits what is free: the one app that pulls most, quiet at once, and it says plainly what it held back.
- And a set says what it is about to ask for. Tapping one used to land you on Android's permission screen with nothing to explain why a calm app wants to draw over other apps. One quiet screen comes first now — what each permission is for and, as plainly, what is not being asked for: SonoLune reads which app is in front, never what is in it, nothing leaves the phone, and no notifications are wanted. It appears only when something is actually missing.

## Saying what these actually do

- "Quiet begins after" now says that the clock starts when you open the app and counts only while you are in it, that the quiet is deepest exactly at your time rather than a moment after, and what Instant is for — an app you want to stop opening, rather than one you want to use less.
- "Grace period" now says what it actually governs. Stepping out of a chosen app lifts the wash at once either way; the grace is about the CLOCK, and whether a quick reply hands you a fresh start.
- And it names which is which, because the labels suggest the opposite of what they do. Brief is the most forgiving — leave for six seconds and the clock starts over. Lenient holds you to it: half a minute away, and you return exactly where you left off.
- The settings on the Presence page say who they reach, now that they are no longer the only ones. They are the shared settings — they apply to every chosen app that has not been given its own — and the line above your apps says how to give one different ones, whether or not any app has yet.
- Paper Grain stopped apologising. It dimmed itself, renamed itself Matte and turned your tap away whenever accessibility surfacing was on, because the system overlay had never been given the grain to draw. It has been given it, the grain draws there, and a control that explains a limit which no longer exists is its own small untruth.
