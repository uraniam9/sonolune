# SonoLune 2.4.4

**Apps it will leave alone, and the work it was doing while you slept** · 26 Sep 2026

There is one new thing you can do in this release, and it is the opposite of everything else in the app. Every per-app setting SonoLune has ever had says where the calm should go. None of them said where it must never go, so somebody who wanted the evening light everywhere except their banking app had no way to say so, and the only honest answer was to turn the whole thing off. Help now has a page called Apps SonoLune leaves alone. Name an app there and no tint, wash or dimming settles over it again - not from the evening light, not from the Presence Field, not from a pack, not from a schedule. Nothing else about that app changes. The same page shows, for the first time, what SonoLune already steps aside for without being asked: UPI apps like Google Pay, Paytm and PhonePe, permission requests, the Play Store, signing in to Google, your phone settings, and any call on any app. Most of that list cannot be switched off and the page says why - your phone hides overlays there anyway, so a switch would be a promise nothing could keep. Seven of them can be handed back, and each one explains itself before you do. While that was being built, one older fault came out with it: the field was correctly staying off protected screens, like permission dialogs and the Play Store, but three things could still put it back - the wind-down beat at the end of a Presence session, a cue card passed to the overlay, and the light service being restarted by the phone in the middle of a purchase. The first two announced a field that was being deliberately held off, and they ask whether one is genuinely on screen now. The third knew nothing at all: the note saying a purchase was open lived only in memory, so a service the phone had killed and brought back came back without it, read your saved light settings and drew the field over the Play Store sheet you were still looking at. That note is written down now. And an answer in Help was simply not true: asked whether SonoLune reads your screen, it ended by saying there is no accessibility service. There is one, there has been since 1.9.3, it stays off until you turn it on, and it never reads the text or content of your screen - which is what it says now. The rest of the release is invisible, and is about how much work the app was doing underneath all this, and where the work was going when nobody was watching. The clearest example is the drift timer. Set one, and the only thing that needs remembering from second to second is how much time is left - a single number. What the app was actually doing every second was writing down the whole of your session: every saved soundscape, every layer and its volume, your recent history, your timer settings. An hour of falling asleep to rain came to somewhere around thirty-six thousand of those writes, on battery, with the screen off, to keep one number current. It keeps the number now and leaves everything else alone. The timer itself behaves exactly as it did - close the app in the middle of a drift and it still comes back where it was, a drift that runs out still ends properly, and your saved rooms are still there afterwards. It simply stops writing the rest of it down while you sleep. The same shape turned up twice more in things you cannot see. The small moon badge that appears when Night begins hides itself after about forty seconds, and for the rest of the time the app was open its two animations were still running - drawing nothing, on a screen it had left, keeping the phone awake for frames nobody would ever see. And the bar along the bottom of every screen breathes, which is deliberate and stays; but it was being rebuilt sixty times a second to show a glow that only changes about thirteen times a second. Roughly four out of five of those were the app carefully producing exactly what it had just produced. The same was true of the moon in Mind, which is on the first screen you see. The Night page had a different version of it: it was listening to everything in the app and redrawing itself whenever anything at all changed, including once a second for the whole of a focus session happening somewhere else entirely. It now listens only for the things it actually shows. And Feather mode, the free switch in Help for phones that struggle, now also settles the bottom bar rather than leaving it breathing. Separately from all of this, and arriving without an update, there is now a second pack on each of the three Sound rails: Open Country, Last Light and Other Rooms. Each keeps a layer open to everyone, as the first three do. If you are coming from 2.4.2, the repairs from 2.4.3 are in here too and they are new to you: the light that would not come back from the notification bulb or the Quick Settings tile, the Night invitation that could not arrive at night, the moon in Mind that refused to let you back into a space you had held, and a hint on the Sound cards that was cut off mid-word. None of this release should be visible. If any of it is - if something that used to move smoothly now looks stepped or cheap - that is a fault and worth saying so, because every one of these has a dial that can be turned back.

## Apps SonoLune leaves alone

- Help, then Apps SonoLune leaves alone. Name an app and no tint, wash or dimming will settle over it again - not from the evening light, not from the Presence Field, not from a pack, not from a schedule.
- Nothing else about the app changes. Sound keeps playing, schedules keep running, and the app itself is untouched. The only thing that stops is SonoLune drawing over it.

- If an app is already chosen in the Presence Field and you then leave it alone, the page says so before it saves. Leaving alone wins; the Presence choice stays where it is in case you change your mind.

- The same page shows what SonoLune already steps aside for without being asked, which it has never shown anywhere before: UPI apps like Google Pay, Paytm and PhonePe, permission requests, the Play Store, signing in to Google, your phone settings and status bar, and any call on any app.

- The Light room says so too, until you allow it or close the line: the light can only keep off protected screens, like sign-ins and the Play Store, once it can see which app is open.

## A wash that could still arrive where it was kept away

- This one is older than the page above and came out with it.
- SonoLune has always lifted the field off protected screens, like permission dialogs and the Play Store. That part worked.
- But two things could put it back: the wind-down beat at the end of a Presence session, and a cue card passed to the overlay. Both said a field was live, which is precisely what was not true at that moment. A third, older than either, is at the foot of this section.
- So a release beat landing while a protected screen was open could wash the screen it had just been kept off.
- Both now ask whether a field is genuinely on screen before they announce one. The wind-down and the cue behave exactly as before everywhere else.
- The deepening pulse got the same treatment: it is a felt nudge that the field is closing in, and it no longer fires when there is no field to close in.
- A third way in was found on 2026-09-22, while checking this release rather than while building it. It is older than both of the others and has been in every version since at least 2.0.
- Opening the support page and tapping a card has always cleared the field correctly, and still does. But the note that says a purchase is open was only ever held in the app's memory, and the light is not part of the app you are looking at - it is a service the phone is allowed to stop.
- So if the phone killed SonoLune while the Play sheet was on screen, Android brought the service straight back, and it returned knowing nothing about a purchase. It found your light settings saved on disk, did the helpful thing, and drew the field over the sheet you were still reading - with nothing left to take it off, because the part of the app that says a purchase has ended had died with it.
- The note is written to disk now, so a service that comes back reads it and stays off. It also expires by itself after a few minutes, because nothing is coming back to clear it, and a light stuck off for good would be a worse fault than the one being fixed.
- The phones most likely to kill the service are the same ones Night watch warns about, which is much of why this went unseen: it needs the phone to do the killing, so it rarely happens to whoever is testing.
- And the new warning that tells you when your light has stopped knows the difference. A light held off for a purchase has not stopped, so it says nothing.

## An answer about privacy that was not true

- In Help, asked whether SonoLune reads your screen or content, the answer ended: there is no accessibility service.
- There is one, and there has been since 1.9.3. It is how the calm layer reaches the places an ordinary overlay cannot - the notification shade and the lock screen - and how the app can tell which app is in front.
- It stays off until you turn it on, and it never reads the text or the content of your screen. Neither does Usage Access, which the Presence Field asks for separately and which answers only that same which-app-is-in-front question.
- All of that was already true. The sentence was simply wrong, on the one answer a person deciding whether to trust the app actually reads, and where anyone could disprove it from their own settings in ten seconds.
- It says the true thing now. Nothing about what the app does changed.

## Do Not Disturb, given back only if it was ours

- Night and Focus can turn on Do Not Disturb while they run. When they finished, they put back whatever Do Not Disturb had been doing when they started.
- That went wrong whenever something else changed it in between. If a bedtime or Do Not Disturb schedule was already on when Night started, and switched itself off in the morning, Night then switched Do Not Disturb back on as it finished - and the phone stayed silent until somebody noticed.
- SonoLune now only undoes what it did itself. If Do Not Disturb was already on when Night or Focus started, it is left completely alone, and whatever turned it on is what turns it off.
- If it was off, SonoLune turns it on and turns it off again when it is done, exactly as before.
- One difference you might notice: if that other schedule ends before Night does, the rest of Night is not silenced. A phone that rings a little early is a safer mistake than one that stays silent all day.

## When the light stops, you get told

- The light is not a picture the app draws while you are looking at it. It is a service that keeps running after you leave, which is the only way it can still be there at three in the morning.
- Phones are allowed to stop that service. Several manufacturers do it by default to save battery, without asking and without saying so. Nothing crashes and nothing fails. The light just is not there any more.
- Until now the app never noticed. It checked that it still had permission, which it did, and concluded all was well. What that left you with was I turned it on and it stopped, which is indistinguishable from the app being broken.
- It now checks whether its own service is actually running, at launch and when you come back. If the field was stopped while you were not looking, a sheet says so and offers two things: bring it back, or read why this happens.
- There is a second half that arrives earlier. On phones known to stop background work, a quiet note appears while the field is still up and nothing has gone wrong yet - so the first you hear of it is not a lost night. It does not appear on phones that leave apps alone.
- To be straight about the limit: SonoLune cannot stop your phone doing this, and no app can. What it can do is notice, tell you, and put the setting that prevents it one tap away instead of buried in Help.

## The timer that wrote everything down, every second

- A drift timer needs to remember one thing between seconds: how much of it is left. That number is kept so a drift survives the app being closed, which is worth doing.
- What was actually saved each second was the whole session - every saved soundscape, every layer and its volume, your recent rooms, your timer settings - and then written to storage. Around ten separate writes a second.
- For an hour of falling asleep to rain that is roughly thirty-six thousand writes, on battery, with the screen off, for one number that fits in a corner.
- Nothing else in that bundle was changing. Every second it was carefully saving values identical to the ones already there.
- It saves the number now. Everything else is written when it actually changes, which is what the rest of the app has always done.
- The timer is unchanged in every way you can see: close the app in the middle of a drift and it comes back where it was, a drift that runs out still fades and ends, and your saved rooms are untouched.

## Things that kept moving after they left the screen

- The small moon badge that appears as Night begins takes itself away after about forty seconds. That was always true of the badge. It was not true of its two animations, which kept running for the rest of the time the app was open.
- They were drawing nothing - there was nothing on screen to draw - but a running animation asks the phone for a new frame sixty times a second whether or not anything has changed.
- That is the part worth knowing: it is not only the animation that costs something. It is that a perfectly still screen could never settle while one was running.
- Both now rest while the badge is away, and begin again the next time it appears. The badge looks and behaves exactly as it did.

## Drawing only when there is something new to draw

- The bar along the bottom breathes on every screen. It is meant to, and it still does.
- But the breath is drawn in small steps - about thirteen a second - while the bar was being rebuilt sixty times a second to show it. Four out of five of those produced exactly what had just been produced.
- That happened on every screen, for as long as the app was open, which made it the single largest standing cost in SonoLune.
- The moon in Mind was the same story on the first screen you see: its aurora, its corona and the shadows under the moment cards follow a breath that moves about eight times a second, and were being redrawn sixty.
- Both now redraw when their breath actually moves. The breathing itself is unchanged - same speed, same depth, same feel.
- Alongside that, a few hundred small pieces of the app that never change are now built once rather than rebuilt every time a screen is drawn.

## Night, listening only for what it shows

- The Night page was listening to everything in the app at once. Any change anywhere caused it to redraw itself entirely.
- That included things with no presence on the page at all - a sound switched on in another room, or a focus session counting down once a second somewhere else entirely.
- A focus session is twenty-five minutes long, so that was fifteen hundred complete redraws of a page you were not even necessarily looking at.
- It now listens for the specific things it draws: your scheduled times, the switches on the page, whether Night is running. Everything on it still responds exactly as it did.
- The light overlay had a smaller version of the same habit and has been given the same treatment.

## Feather mode, one step further

- Feather mode lives in Help, under This device. It is free, it is off unless you turn it on, and it exists for phones that find SonoLune heavy.
- It already removed the frosted blur behind the bottom bar, which was the most expensive thing the app drew.
- It now also lets that bar hold still, at the middle of its breath, rather than breathing continuously.
- The bar change is only visible on a phone where you have turned the switch on. Every other phone breathes exactly as before.
- Under that switch there is now a line about your own phone: how many of its frames ran late, and on which version of SonoLune.
- The app has been taking that measurement since 2.4.1 - a few seconds after each launch, once things have settled. It used it for one thing, deciding whether to mention Feather at all, and then dropped it. Worse, once it had mentioned Feather it stopped measuring, which meant the phones worth watching were the first to go quiet.
- It measures every launch now and keeps the last few readings, each with the version it was taken on. A percentage on its own cannot tell you whether an update helped; the same percentage next to the version before it can.
- On a new install it says it has not measured yet, because it has not. It will never say a phone is fine before it has looked.
- Nothing acts on this by itself. It is there so that “the app feels slower” can be a number rather than an argument - yours as much as ours.

## A shorter first visit to Sound

- Sound is the largest room in the app, and it was showing all of itself to everybody, every time. Counted rather than guessed: about 595 words and roughly 33 controls across nine stacked sections, before you had touched a single one.
- It is also the room the strongest door opens into. Fall asleep is the most reported use of SonoLune by a distance, and it has already started playing by the time the page finishes drawing. The thing you arrived for is handled. Everything after it is length.

- One tap opens it and it stays open. It also retires by itself once you have properly used Sound, so somebody who listens for an evening and never explores is not left with a folded room.
- Nothing was removed, nothing was renamed, and nothing moved to a different room. If you know what you came for, it is one tap from where it always was.
- Anybody who has used SonoLune before never sees the row at all. This is the shape of a first visit, not a setting and not a mode.

## And to Focus, which measured worst of all

- Among them was an offer of a quiet hour - 67 words of it - put in front of somebody who had never held a session. The sheet that makes that offer deliberately leaves Focus out, on the grounds that it belongs in the room where somebody who has held a session meets it. This room was showing it anyway.

- Sound and Focus now share one copy of this rule rather than keeping two that could quietly drift apart.

## Presence, staged instead of handed over at once

- That is not day-zero density. It is a cliff, and it is exactly what a blanket lite mode would have produced: everything withheld, then handed over all at once. It already existed here without anybody having chosen it.
- The signal came from the room itself. Its jump rail highlighted Apps while nothing had been chosen, beside a note saying the other three were tuning for a field that is not shaped yet. The page knew, and kept drawing them.
- Depth, Timing and Around now wait behind one card, Shape the field, until the field has somewhere to settle. Nine switches on arrival became one.
- Choose an app and the whole room opens by itself, immediately, with nothing to discover. Tapping the card opens it too. Either way it stays open.
- The four-chip jump rail is gone, because four chips pointing at one destination is not a map. The sentence under it still tells you to start with Apps.
- On any existing install the card is never shown. Somebody who deliberately wants a phone-wide field never names an app, and without that rule they would be staged forever.

## The first screen, three small repairs

- The very first screen asks one question - what would you like SonoLune to do first - and the row of seven suite icons was being drawn underneath it, live and tappable. Tapping one crossed into a room without ever answering the question. The bar waits until you have gone through the door now, which is also when it starts meaning anything.
- It still appears straight away if you open those pages again later from Help, because there it is the way back out.
- The small pill under the moon used to read "Shape SonoLune with us". It was the most visible place in the app and it was asking somebody who had owned SonoLune for ninety seconds to help design it.
- It now points at Feather mode, which is worth far more on the first day: it turns off the soft blurs and the background motion for a phone that finds them heavy. It was previously four sections deep in Help, or you waited for SonoLune to notice your phone struggling - which is no use at all to somebody who has already decided to uninstall it.
- The words only speak to somebody who is already feeling it, and it is not a smaller SonoLune. Nothing is removed or hidden; the blurs and the motion stop. Shaping SonoLune with us is still there, further down the same page, where somebody who has actually used it will be.
- And the "Help & Settings" button on that screen was clipping its own label at larger system font sizes - by about a third of the word at the largest. It shortens properly now.

## Names that fit at any text size

- At the larger system font sizes, the names of the profiles in Light ran out of their chips and were cut off mid-word. They shorten properly now.
- It is the same small fault as the Help & Settings button above - an icon beside a label that was never asked whether it fitted - and it turned up when every room was checked at the largest text size, upright and on its side.
