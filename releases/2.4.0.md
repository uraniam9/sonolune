# SonoLune 2.4.0

**The light reaches the screen, and every room puts its door where you would reach for it** · 28 Aug 2026

Two halves, and the same fault underneath both. The warm light had not been leaving the app: granting the draw-over-apps permission at any moment other than the one SonoLune happened to be watching left the light shut inside it, while the Light suite showed the preset lit and correct. And every room in the app had put the thing it exists to do underneath everything that can be adjusted about it, so a first visit read as a list of settings for a feature nobody had switched on. The light is fixed. Six rooms now put their action where somebody would reach for it. The home screen says, in one line, what SonoLune actually is. And the quiet door SonoLune can open at an hour you choose, which has always shipped switched off and hidden four sections deep in Help, is now offered during onboarding and set afterwards in the room it opens.

## Highlights

- The light has not been reaching the screen. If you granted SonoLune the draw-over-apps permission at any moment other than the one it happened to be watching, the warm light stayed inside SonoLune and never reached your home screen, your reader, or anything else — while the Light suite showed the preset lit and correct. There was one dependable way out, and it was the extend-light row inside the Light suite. Anyone who never found that row had an app insisting the light was on while the screen said otherwise, and no reason to believe anything but the screen.
- The reason is small and worth saying plainly. SonoLune keeps a note of whether the light may leave the app, and that note was only ever updated at the instant the permission changed. Grant it a moment earlier, or simply open the app again afterwards, and the check found nothing had changed and stopped before the line that lets the light out. It is checked on its own terms now rather than on a change, so it corrects itself on any launch instead of waiting for a moment that had already passed.
- Opening SonoLune to sound in the room is one tap. The first screen offers Start the sound now beside Next, and it does exactly that: soft rain begins, the light comes up, and you arrive in the Sound room with the field already playing and the transport in front of you. It used to be eight taps and five hundred words of description before anything could be heard, and the way to skip that was a quiet line of text beside a bright button, wearing a name only someone who had already used the app would understand.
- Nothing begins on its own, and that has not moved. The tap is still yours, still deliberate, and still on a button that says what it will do. The only thing that changed is that your one choice is now spent on sound rather than on another page of words.
- The sleep timer is where someone reaching for it would look. It had been the very last thing in the Sound room, below the stereo control, the lab and the arriving packs — so the person most likely to want it, winding down and looking for a length, had to scroll past everything they had not bought in order to reach it. It sits directly under the layers now. Nothing was removed and nothing else moved; the order is simply the order somebody would actually use.
- And the moon says what it does. Holding the moon in Mind keeps your whole space — the light, the sound, the room — so you can come back to it exactly as you left it. That is the one thing in Mind belonging to no other room, and a long press has no way of announcing itself. It was explained during onboarding, which is precisely the part most people now skip. A single line under the moon says it instead, and retires the first time you hold it.
- A light you set could be switched off again by a change in screen brightness. Whenever SonoLune noticed the brightness had moved, it rebuilt the light from a list of parts written out by hand — and anything missing from that list quietly went back to nothing. The whole living field was missing from it: whether the light follows your sound, the mode it moves in, its colours, its speed. This had happened once before with the paper finish and been fixed by adding that one part to the list, which left the same trap set for every other. It is built by copying now, which cannot forget.
- Patronage that had not finished loading was read as no patronage at all. About a second into every start, while SonoLune was still working out what you support, a check ran that clears anything a non-supporter should not have — and wrote its answer down. So the light-follows-sound arrangement was switched off on your behalf, permanently, by something that had simply not read far enough yet. It now waits until the question can actually be answered. There is a real distinction underneath: "no" and "not yet" are not the same answer, and only one of them is a decision.
- A saved soundscape comes back with its name still lit. This is the fifth attempt at that one sentence, and the first four all worked on the wrong half. The name was being written down correctly and kept correctly — and then deleted a moment after the app opened, by the very check meant to protect it. That check asks whether the soundscape still exists before keeping its name, which is right; it was simply asking before the list of saved soundscapes had been read, so the answer was always no. It waits for the list now.
- And tapping a light preset no longer switches off the light-follows-sound arrangement. A preset says what the light should look like — its warmth, its dimness, its paper surface. Whether the light moves with your sound is a different kind of choice, made on a different screen, and the preset was answering it anyway by carrying a silent no. Choosing a look no longer overrules something you set elsewhere.
- Nothing on the home screen said what SonoLune is. A first visit showed a moon, its phase, a whisper and a greeting, and the first words on the page naming anything the app actually does were Lune Guard, Lunar Arc and Lune Field, which mean nothing to somebody who installed it an hour ago. One plain line now sits under the name on a first run and retires once it has been read: light and sound, settling as one, so your phone asks less of you. That is the one thing this app does that nothing else does, said in words a stranger can read.
- The button that offers to show you around was pointing at a door that had already closed behind you. Start Here on the home screen was shown whenever no sound happened to be playing, which is not the same question as whether somebody is new, so a person six months in with a quiet phone was invited back to the beginner tour while a genuinely new person lost the offer the moment anything began. It now appears once, for somebody who has not seen it, and opens the guide rather than replaying the introduction.
- In the Presence Field, the app picker was the last thing on a page five screens deep. That picker is what the whole room is for, and the page said so four times in copy that pointed below, past three cards of tuning, to a control most people never reached. Until apps have been chosen it now comes first, and a row of four anchors at the top carries you to any part of the field in one tap, with the one you probably want lit.
- Lune Field asked you to tune a wallpaper you had never seen. Opening it from its own link forced the card open, so twelve sliders for atmosphere, sky, moon size, star density and horizon stood between a first reader and the single button that puts the moon on their home screen. Nobody tunes a thing they have not got. The button sits under the picture that sold it now, and the tuning waits behind one tap for anybody who wants it.
- The button that begins a focus session was the seventeenth thing on its own page. Ten switches across two cards sat between choosing a length and starting, and the timer ring itself could not be tapped, so that one button was the only way in. It has moved up to sit directly under the length you just chose, and the ring is now the button too: tap it to begin, to come back to a paused session, or to pause a running one.
- There was also a purple button four hundred lines above it, the same size and the same colour as the one that starts a session, that opened the support page instead. It confirms a typed length, which is a small job wearing a large coat, and it is quiet text now. The first prominent thing a free reader met on that page should never have been an offer to pay.
- An app you set up yourself did nothing. Choose an app under the per-app field, open its own sheet, turn on every switch inside it — and no quiet ever arrived. Each of those switches was being checked against a matching one on the Presence page, so both had to agree before anything ran, while the header of that sheet promises the exact opposite: that the shared settings will not affect this app. Worse still, with the two shared switches off, the machinery that reads per-app tuning never started at all, so the whole sheet was inert rather than merely halved. The tuning belonging to an app now governs that app, which is what having one was always supposed to mean.
- The attention packs could report themselves undone. A pack writes its length, its grace and its style into each app it covers, and then the tick beside it checked four settings the pack does not write — two that go stale the moment a shared switch is turned off, and two the pack never touches at all. It now asks the only question a pack can answer: are these apps still carrying the tuning this pack wrote. An app you have since tuned by hand correctly stops counting, because it is no longer the tuning the pack wrote.
- Blur that stopped working, for no reason you could see. The words-lose-focus lever asks Android to blur what sits behind the field, and the phone is allowed to refuse that for everything at once — battery saver does exactly this, mid-session, without a word. The request comes back with no blur, no error and no way to tell. It now says so once, plainly, and names the closing edges as what carries the quiet meanwhile. Nothing about it was ever broken; there was simply no way to know that.
- In Arc, three cards saying you have nothing did nothing. On a first visit the page reported no arcs, no wake rituals and no night rituals, one after another, and the button that could create any of them was below all three. The obvious thing to tap was the empty card sitting under its own heading, and that card had no tap at all. Each of them now opens the same sheet its own Add button opens.
- SonoLune can offer to meet you in your day, and it now says so out loud. Every way this app had of reaching anybody arrived switched off and lived four sections deep inside Help, behind the guides and the common questions, on a page most people open only when something has already gone wrong. Onboarding now offers all four once, with the hour each keeps written beside it. Nothing is ticked in advance, because a switch shown on before its permission exists is a promise the app cannot yet keep. Each one, as you turn it on, says what it needs and why, asks for one thing at a time, and ticks each off as it lands.
- Nothing is sent from anywhere to do it. There is no server, no account and no message from outside: your phone holds the hour and opens the door itself, offline, exactly as it always has. What changed is that somebody is now asked whether they would like it, instead of having to go looking for a switch they had no reason to know existed.
- The pages that explain SonoLune could be read exactly once. Onboarding was unreachable the moment it finished: nothing linked to it, and the router only ever sends somebody there who has not seen it — so the one explanation of the whole app was available only at the moment a person knew least about it. Help now offers it back, and reading it again changes nothing you have set.
- Two smaller signposts. Arriving in Sound at the end of onboarding, a single line says where home is, because the arrival deliberately lands on the sound controls and a new person would otherwise never see Mind at all. And the card on the home screen offering to shape SonoLune with you now arrives at the part of the page that does that, rather than twenty-eight cards above it.
- A call now takes the screen back, whatever the field is doing. The Presence Field had no telephony awareness of any kind — no dialer in the protected list, no call state read anywhere — so a phone-wide field at depth could settle over a full-screen incoming call, and a per-app one could do the same to a video call arriving inside an app you had chosen. It lifts the moment a call arrives and returns when the call ends. This covers a silenced phone, and it covers calls inside other apps as well as ordinary ones.
- The Sound Atmosphere packs are less than half the size. All nine layers were re-encoded, and the three Passages ones had been carrying six minutes of near-mono ambience at a bitrate meant for music — fourteen megabytes for a pack whose sound stops entirely above twelve kilohertz. Every octave band was measured before and after and matches within a fifth of a decibel, which is far below anything anyone can hear. A full set is now under eleven megabytes instead of twenty-two, and the free layers under four instead of seven.
- Your sessions no longer vanish when SonoLune cannot reach the internet. The app asked the site for its list of packs, and read no answer as an empty answer — so it erased the list it had saved for exactly this, along with every record of what you had already downloaded. That check runs each time Sound opens, so an outage was enough: twenty-two megabytes of audio on your phone, three sessions gone from the screen, and a line underneath saying they were still being prepared.
- A layer you had added to your field comes back after it re-downloads. When the audio was missing, SonoLune stopped remembering you had ever asked for it — so re-downloading the pack brought the sound back but not the arrangement, and nothing on screen said so. Wanting something and having the bytes for it are two different facts, and only one of them was worth forgetting.
- The Field page no longer rearranges itself under your thumb. It puts the apps section first before you have set the field up and second afterwards, which is right — but it decided that fresh on every rebuild, so turning on “Only these apps” swapped the two sections while you were looking at one of them, and left you somewhere you had not scrolled to. It settles that order when the page opens and keeps it for that visit.
- The Hours has its own heading in Night. It had been sitting directly beneath the evening door with fourteen pixels between them, so it read as a second setting belonging to that door. They are different kinds of thing: one is an hour you pick for this room, the other is a standing arrangement about moments arriving when they were written for.

## Five ways to lose something you had chosen

- Every fault fixed in this release is the same fault. Something that can throw a setting away consulted evidence that had not arrived yet, and threw it away on the strength of the silence.
- The light may leave the app only if the permission was granted — but the question was asked once, at the single moment the answer could change, and never again afterwards.
- A non-supporter must not keep supporter settings — but the question was asked before it was known whether you support anything.
- A name must not point at a soundscape that has been deleted — but the question was asked before the saved soundscapes had been read, so everything looked deleted.
- A brightness change must not disturb the rest of the light — but the rest of the light was listed out by hand, and what the list forgot was thrown away rather than kept.
- None of these is visible by reading the code. Each function is correct on its own; the fault lives in the order two of them happen to run. All five were found by watching a real phone say what it was doing, which is the only place that order actually exists.

## A light that was on and could not be seen

- The light has two reaches. One tints only SonoLune; the other draws across the whole screen, and that one needs the draw-over-apps permission.
- Granting the permission is not the same as the app noticing. It kept a separate note about whether the light may leave, and that note was written only at the moment the permission changed hands.
- So the timing decided everything. Granted during the one check the app happened to make, it worked and always had. Granted at any other point, or simply seen again on a later launch, the check found nothing had changed and stopped before the line that mattered.
- What made it hard to doubt is that everything else was right. The preset applied, the Light suite drew it, and the app believed it. Only the screen disagreed.
- It is decided by what is true now rather than by what changed, so a launch finding the permission granted and the light still shut in corrects it there and then.
- Nothing you chose is overridden. There has never been a way to ask for the light to stay inside the app, so there is no such choice to undo — and where the permission has been withdrawn the light comes back in on its own, exactly as before.

## The first minute, counted

- Somebody who had been given SonoLune said there was too much information in it, and uninstalled it. That is the whole reason for this release, and the first thing done about it was to count rather than to guess.
- Reaching sound was eight taps and five hundred and six words. It is one tap and fifty-six now.
- The way past it already existed and was losing a contrast fight. It was flat grey text beside a bright amber button, and it wore a word you only learn by using the app. It is a button now, and it is called Start the sound now.
- The guides were measured too, and cleared. Every room carries one, and together they run past two thousand words — but none of them open by themselves, and that was settled two releases ago. They were not the problem, and were left alone.
- What was left was the Sound room itself: around six hundred words and thirty-three controls in one scroll, with the sleep timer at the very bottom of it. Moving the timer is the part of that worth shipping on its own.

## Everything, where you would reach for it

- Every room was measured for the same fault: where does its main action sit, and how much has to be scrolled past to reach it. Six rooms had the answer wrong in the same way.
- The pattern was always the same. The thing a room exists to do was placed after everything that can be adjusted about it, so the page read as a list of settings for a feature nobody had switched on yet.
- It is the wrong way round for a first visit and harmless for a tenth, which is exactly why it survives: the person who could tell you it is wrong has already left.
- Nothing was removed to fix it, and no control moved out of the room it belongs to. In every case the action moved up above the tuning, and in one case the tuning simply stopped opening itself uninvited.
- Four rooms were measured and left alone, because they were already right. The sound transport never scrolls away, Lune Guard puts its button straight after the thing it demonstrates, and two pages have no action to bury.

## A door at your hour

- SonoLune has been able to open a quiet door at an hour you choose for some time. It arrived switched off, and it lived somewhere nobody goes.
- An app made for daily use had no way to arrive in the day it was made for. That is not a missing feature; it is a finished feature nobody was offered.
- Each door lives in the room it opens, AND all of them together in one place. The evening one and The Hours are in Night, the quiet hour in Focus, the morning one in Arc beside the wake arcs it would set — it arrives in the EVENING to ask about tomorrow, so the room you are trying to fall asleep in was exactly the wrong place for it. Help still lists every one of them, because moving them to their rooms took away the only screen where you could see all the ways SonoLune can reach you and switch them on together. Both surfaces read the same setting, so they cannot disagree.
- The wake door says which hour it means. It sits at eight in the evening on purpose — it exists to catch you the night before, while you are still awake to set tomorrow — but it read like a wake alarm at eight at night, directly beneath a Dawn Wake at 6:45 AM. Two contradictory wake times on one screen. It now reads “asked at 8:00 PM”, and the hour itself has not moved.
- Turning any of them on opens one sheet that names every permission it needs and why, asks for them one at a time, and ticks each off as it arrives. Nothing is ticked in advance, because a switch shown on before its permission exists is a promise the app cannot yet keep. Once every door in a room is on, the panel folds to a single line naming the hour, one tap from the whole thing again.
- Onboarding asks on a page of its own, fifth of nine, with the hour each door keeps written beside it — The Hours included, which the first draft left out. It sat second to last at first, which read beautifully and was reached by almost nobody: the button that skips ahead sits on every page, so anyone who took it early never met the offer at all.
- Once a door is on, its panel collapses to a single line naming the hour it keeps, one tap from the whole thing again. It stays in the room where it was set rather than retreating to Help, because Help is where this feature spent its whole life being unfindable.
- Turning any of them on opens one sheet that names every permission it needs and why, requests them one at a time, and ticks each off as it arrives. Notifications for all of them; the light window for the evening door and The Hours, both of which close themselves without it; exact alarms so the hour you chose is the hour it keeps. That last one is marked optional, because it is about accuracy and not about whether the door works at all.
- Nothing counts them. No streak, no total, and a day you skip simply passes. A door you ignore leaves no trace, which is the only version of this worth shipping.

## A phone-wide quiet that lets go

- The Presence Field can cover every screen on the phone, and until now that version of it had none of the mercies the per-app one has. It arrived at full depth the instant it armed, so there was no stretch in which to finish what you were doing. It had no ending of its own, so the only way out was switching the whole suite off. And when it did speak, it asked you to open another room — at the exact moment it had finished making the screen hard to read.
- All three came from the same place. Every clock in the per-app field starts when a chosen app comes to the front, and a phone-wide field has no such moment. There was never anything for a clock to start from, so there was no clock.
- Quiet arrives over the time you choose. Pick a stretch and the field opens almost invisible and reaches your depth at the end of it, rather than landing on you at once. The picker used to be called “Quiet begins after”, which described the wrong shape — it reads as a wait, when the number is really a span. Both this one and the per-app one say “arrives over” now.
- The field can let go on its own. Never, thirty minutes, an hour or two hours, and Never is what it does unless you say otherwise. Leaving a chosen app has always ended a per-app quiet; this is the same courtesy for one that covers everything. It is an ending and never a renewal — nothing here brings the quiet back by itself, because a quiet that returned on its own would be something happening to you rather than something you chose.
- And it says so before it starts. Arming a phone-wide field now asks first, and names the bulb in the notification as the way out in a hurry. That sentence is deliberately not written anywhere inside the field itself: learning to reach past the quiet while you are in it is learning to undo the thing you asked for. Before is consent. During would be a back door.
- The cue you get at the end is one you can follow without the screen. It used to say “Tap to open Night” or “Tap to start a Focus”, which a per-app field can carry — leaving the app makes the phone readable again — and a phone-wide one cannot. It now reads “Time to put the phone down · tap to take a breath”, and the tap opens a breath where you are rather than sending you anywhere.
- That cue arrives even if you never set a time. It used to be fired only by an onset finishing, so a phone-wide field left on Instant — which is what it is unless you change it — went its whole life without ever saying anything. It now speaks once after twenty minutes regardless.
- The depth slider is in charge of depth again. While the quiet was arriving, the slider was writing one value and the ramp was writing another every few seconds, so moving it lasted about five seconds before being quietly overruled by a clock you could not see. The slider now names where the quiet is heading, and the ramp goes there instead.
- Arming a phone-wide field asks for the notification first, and then tells you the truth about it: with the notification there is a way out that needs no screen, and without it the dialog says so plainly rather than promising a bulb that will never be drawn.
- Let go after now offers ten and fifteen minutes as well as thirty, an hour and two.
- The cue at the end of a phone-wide quiet does three things it was not doing. It no longer offers to take you somewhere: its choices were Step into Night and Settle here, and a field that has made every screen hard to read cannot honestly send you anywhere you could then read. It no longer outstays the field: it used to sit for ten minutes on top of a screen that had already let go, and lifting the field now takes the card with it. While the quiet is still holding, the sentence explaining why stays where you can read it. And it no longer moves around: it was being placed by the same code that flashes brief messages at random points, so the one sentence that mattered appeared somewhere different every time.
- The field depth in the notification could read 100% with the bar short of the end. The bar eases toward its target by a fraction of whatever distance is left, and that fraction rounds to zero before the last point is covered, so it parked one short and stayed there. It only happened on some approaches, which is why it looked like a ghost.
- Every way SonoLune can reach you is listed in one place again, and the evening door sits on the way into Night rather than inside it. Both halves of that were wrong before. Help had stopped listing them at all, so there was no screen where you could see every way the app can reach you and switch them on together — and Night was offering you an hour to choose while you were already settled into the room that hour opens. Choosing when the evening should reach you belongs on the way in; a room you are winding down in is not a settings page.
- Focus stops offering you a focus hour while you are in one. The invitation reads “a quiet hour is here if you want one”, and it was sitting on the screen six minutes into a running session. The rule was already written down for the notification — never offer somebody the room they are already standing in — and the panel that configures it was the one place not following it.
- The Presence Field says it is in beta, and the label is the way to report what you find. It reaches every screen on your phone and it is the newest thing here, so meeting a fault in it should not leave anyone wondering whether their phone is broken — or hunting for somewhere to say so.
