# SonoLune 2.4.1

**A way in, a screen you can read, and the guards that stood at one door** · 4 Sep 2026

A release almost entirely about things that were already built and only worked from one direction. The caution before the Presence Field covers your whole phone stood in front of the arm button and nowhere else, so three other ordinary actions reached the same state in silence — and it asked the wrong question even there, because a per-app field with no apps chosen is a whole-phone field. The two clocks that make a phone-wide field bearable, its gentle opening and its ending, hid themselves and refused to run for exactly the field they were written for. Seven of the ten founding moments could never arrive, because the reader that turns a moment's hour into a time knew five of the ten ways they are written, and two of those five name hours it is right to stay quiet through. Your drift timer could be wiped as the app opened, by a save that ran before the load. And the light stopped watching for protected screens the moment it covered your whole phone, so taps on a Play Store or UPI sheet went nowhere with nothing said. Much of this came from a real phone; the rest was found by asking, of each guard that had failed, which other paths reach it. And then a second pass, from twenty early readers, that found something older and worse than any of it: in dark mode SonoLune had been letting Android recolour its own window, so the app could open to a screen you could not read — every launch, for as long as anyone has reported it. One line, missing from one variant of the theme. Alongside it, the first two minutes: eight doors instead of nine pages of description, a first atmosphere loud enough to hear without headphones, Feather offered by a phone that has measured itself rather than buried in a settings page, and a typeface that is finally SonoLune's rather than on loan from whichever font the phone was wearing. And one more of the same shape, found last: the card that shows you the way out of a settled screen could be drawn over by any other cue a second after it appeared, because how much a cue mattered was being judged by whether tapping it went anywhere — and on a whole-phone field it goes nowhere by design. Since then the release grew a beginning. A first run now opens on eight doors rather than nine pages of description, each naming what it will do and ordered by the hour you are actually standing in, so the app starts by doing something instead of by explaining itself. True black reaches the rooms it was missing: the switch had always worked, but ten rooms painted a near-black of their own over the ground underneath, so turning it on appeared to change nothing but the bars. A phone with its font and display size turned all the way up found thirty-three layouts that could not hold their own contents, across every suite and most of the pages behind them, and not one of the app's thirty-five dialogs could scroll. Night can now hold calls and notifications back for as long as it has the screen. Presence, still the newest thing here, no longer loses your dwell to a video sent to a floating window. A cold start could leave Night's own quiet switch reading on while the phone stayed unquiet, and a phone turned all the way up could crash outright setting a time in a Lunar Arc; both are closed. And what a patronage opens is written down in one place at last, instead of being scattered across three rooms.

## Highlights

- You asked, and this release is the answer. Nearly all of it came from what people wrote in over August: that the app was overwhelming to begin with, that it could open too bright to read, that it was hard to know where to start, and that the words broke apart at a large font. Every one of those is answered here. The rest of the release is what was found while going to look.
- A first run now opens on eight doors instead of nine pages of description. Fall asleep, settle the whole phone, focus for a while, quiet the apps that pull, calm my screen, let quiet moments find me, rest my eyes, put a moon on my home screen. One tap each, and every door says what it will do — including whether sound starts, because a first tap should never surprise anyone with audio. The three that need a permission explain themselves in SonoLune's own words first, and "Not now" opens the room anyway. They are ordered by the hour you are in, so falling asleep does not lead at nine in the morning. The full tour is still there, one button underneath them. If you already have SonoLune you will not be sent to this screen, because it belongs to a first run — open it whenever you like from Help and Settings, under "Start from a door".
- True black now reaches the rooms it was missing. The switch itself always worked, but ten rooms paint a near-black backdrop of their own over the ground underneath — Mind, Night, Presence, Lune Guard, Help, Connect, the Lunar Arc, the onboarding tour, the first-run doors and About — so with the setting on, only the bars turned black and every page stayed midnight blue. Those backdrops collapse with the ground now. Cards, veils and horizon glows keep their lift on purpose: on an unlit pixel, that lift is the only thing that makes a card a card.
- Every suite got something, and the largest of them was invisible until somebody turned the font up. At maximum font and display size, thirty-three layouts could not hold their own contents — in all seven suites and most of the pages behind them. A fixed box holding text that grows, or a row with no give, are the same defect wearing two faces. They are fixed, and pinned by a test that pumps seven suites and nineteen pages at one and a third and twice the type size, in portrait, in landscape, and in the short window a suite is actually given under the nav bar. An overflow is a real exception in a test, so each one now reports its own file and line.
- Night can hold the outside world back. A switch in Night's own settings asks Android to keep calls and notifications quiet for as long as Night has the screen, and gives everything back the moment Night ends, however it ends. Nothing is read and nothing is recorded. Focus has had this since it shipped, and an evening is where it was most missing. It sits with the other switches that shape a night rather than floating alone in an empty screen, it is shown whether or not Night is running, it is felt the moment you throw it rather than waiting for the next entry, and turning it off mid-night returns the world at once. A cold start could show the switch on while the phone stayed unquiet regardless — the silence is asked for when Night is ENTERED, and a cold start that opens already inside Night crosses no such line, so only toggling the switch off and back on ever actually asked for it. It is asked for once more at the end of every restore now, so a Night you were already standing in when the phone restarted is exactly as quiet as one you just walked into.
- Presence keeps improving, and it is still the newest thing in SonoLune. Sending a chosen app to a floating window used to end the field outright: Android reports that as the app being left, so the sound bed stopped and the dwell clock spent its grace and reset while you were still watching. It now tells a window that is merely unfocused from one that is genuinely gone, and holds the clock rather than ending it. Covering the floating window itself is not done, and is not a small thing: it would need a permission to read what is on your screen, which this app has deliberately never taken.
- And dozens of fixes too small to have a line of their own. The Field Journal inside the app carries the complete notes for this release, section by section — the guards that only ever worked from one direction, the moments that could never arrive, the protected screens that swallowed your taps, the timers that could be wiped as the app opened, and the rest of it.
- The support page was selling people things they already had, and one line of it was simply untrue. Every one-time tier includes months of Patron of the Lab — three, nine, or two years by tier — and while those months run the Lab is fully open. The page went on offering the monthly patronage anyway, under a sentence saying that becoming a patron "simply continues it when the included months settle". Google Play charges from the day you tap. Somebody with two years still to run could have paid for all of it twice, having read the opposite on the card. The card now names the month those included months actually end, and says plainly that there is nothing to buy today.
- And the three tiers spoke only to a stranger. Someone already paying the monthly patronage has every supporter refinement while it holds, so a card telling them it "opens every supporter refinement" was answering a question they had not asked — and the months each tier includes would run alongside the subscription they were already paying for. Both facts are on the cards now, for that reader only. Nothing is blocked: either purchase can still be made, deliberately, by somebody who wants to give more than they need to. That was always what the three tiers were for. It only had to stop being an accident.
- A supporter's own saved room was being deleted as the app opened. Whether you have supported SonoLune is read from disk, and until that read finishes the answer is the same false you get for having never supported. One line took that false as proof and erased the active room it found. Arrivals were unaffected, which is why this looked like "arrivals persist and composed moments do not" rather than what it was. Nothing is destroyed now on the strength of an answer that has not been read yet.
- With your sound held, switching "The light is moving with this" on lit the Living Field over silence and left it there. Pausing a field that was already following correctly took the light down — the two only ever met on sound's own listener, so turning the follow on itself was never asked whether anything was actually playing, because a light switch is not a sound event. The switch still turns on the moment you tap it. What moves is held back until the sound does, and says so: "The light will move when this plays" rather than a promise the room could not keep. The same switch inside the Light suite carried the identical gap and now answers the same way.
- Mind says what SonoLune is. The home screen opened on a moon, a phase, a whisper and a greeting, and the first words naming a function were the invented suite names, which mean nothing to a stranger. A single line now sits under the wordmark, and Start Here is a real button that opens the map of the whole system rather than replaying the tour. Both retire once answered, so a returning user loses nothing.
- The warning before the field covers your whole phone now stands at every door you can walk through yourself. It guarded the button that arms the field and nothing else, so turning off "Only these apps" while the field was running, taking the ✕ off your last chosen app, or removing an attention pack each handed over every screen with nothing said. Saying "not now" leaves everything exactly as it was — the switch on, the apps kept, the field still holding. Refusing to cover your phone is not a request to end what you had. A field arriving on a schedule you set still asks nothing, because nobody is there to answer and a question that blocked would stop it arriving at all.
- And it was asking the wrong question even at the door it did guard. "Only these apps" with nothing chosen is not a small field: it is a whole-phone field wearing that switch, which the picker has always said in small print underneath. Every check that treated the switch as proof of a limit was answered by the wrong thing: the two clocks and the slow settling, the three quieting levers, the notice in your shade, the words offered when it is time to step away, and the page's own headings.
- The two clocks that make a phone-wide field bearable were invisible and inert. A system-wide field opens gently over a stretch you choose and lets go by itself at the end — and both controls hid themselves, and both refused to run, whenever that switch was on with nothing chosen. So the one field with no boundary and no app to leave was the only field denied the two things written for exactly that problem. A third, the slow settling, stood aside for a per-app version that could not start either.
- Nothing could start those clocks when the field grew. Removing your last chosen app turns a small field into a whole-phone one, and the field went on with no gentle opening and no ending at all until it was next armed by hand. The app could stop a clock; nothing anywhere could begin one.
- The three quieting levers switched themselves off for the field they were built for. Closing edges, blur behind, and the dulling of bright buttons all stood down whenever the field covered your whole phone — which is the reach that needs them most, and the one belonging to everybody who has not granted the app-reading permission.
- The notice in your shade said "Field in your chosen apps" over a field covering everything. That notice is the way out the warning itself points at, so it is the last thing in the app allowed to be wrong about where the field is. It corrects itself the moment the last app leaves now, rather than waiting for something else to change.
- Seven of the ten founding moments could never arrive. A moment names the hour it was made for in its own words — "three o'clock", "after dinner", "the morning after it fell" — and the reader that turns those into a time knew five of them. The other five were skipped on every pass, silently, since the day they shipped; two more name hours inside the quiet window and are held back on purpose. Three could ever reach you. All ten can now, and a check runs against the real library so a moment written in new words can never again mean never.
- A moment can still settle with the play button reading pause, and this release does not fix it. Tapping one from your shade sets its layers and lights them in the picker, while the one control that says whether the room is running disagrees with the room. Two attempts at it have failed, both reasoned from reading rather than measured — six different guards can quietly decline to start the sound, and from outside they look identical. What this release adds is a way to make the app name the one that refused, which is how the next attempt will be aimed at something rather than guessed at. The sound itself plays; it is the button that is wrong.
- Removing a chosen app from a live field now asks once, and only once. Reaching for the ✕ next to it is usually the same moment the quiet just arrived and you would rather it had not — a reflex tap that undoes a choice made calmly, days earlier, for reasons that were still good. The field stays exactly as escapable as it always was: the bulb in your shade lifts it from anywhere, and the whole-phone caution already names that escape before the field ever arms. This sits beside those, at a lighter weight, and only while the field is armed — building the list itself, before anything is running, is still instant, because a screen you are actively editing must never be interrogated.
- A moment tapped while your space was held used itself up. The notice correctly said to return first; tapping anyway threw away which moment it was, then refused, and the notice is gone once tapped. It is spent only once the moment actually settles now, so returning later still gives you the thing you were offered.
- A reminder that never rings was sitting under an alarm, wearing almost the same name. "A gentle wake" is an evening nudge to set tomorrow's wake — it does not ring, and it was rendered directly beneath the Dawn Wake alarm card. It is called a wake reminder now and says plainly that it is not an alarm. The doors also say what they are before they say how they feel: a notification at an hour you choose, inviting you in. Ignoring one does nothing at all.
- The switch that lets a moment arrive at its own hour now sits with the moments. The Hours had been reachable only through Help and settings, several sections deep, with a line on the Arrivals page explaining where it had gone — so the one control governing the page you were standing on was three taps away in another room. It is on both screens now, and it is the same switch rather than a copy, so the two can never disagree. In Night, the evening door also has its own heading instead of sitting fourteen pixels under the room's own settings as though it were one of them.
- Each door now says where it is aimed. "Next: tonight at 10:38 PM", or a plain reason when nothing is pending. A scheduled thing that will not say when it next happens leaves waiting-correctly, held-back-on-purpose and never-registered looking identical, and that silence has cost this app four rounds of fixes before.
- Taps on a protected sheet, from the Play Store or a UPI app, went nowhere. Android refuses touches under anything drawn over such a screen — that is a real protection, not a fault — and SonoLune is supposed to lift the light when one appears. It stopped watching for them the moment the field covered your whole phone: it looked for about a second after you left the app and then gave up for the rest of the session. It watches for as long as the light is up now.
- And when it did lift, it could not come back. The light left for a protected sheet and stayed gone until the app was force-stopped — the note saying "cannot draw right now" was written down and never cleared. Two of those in a row shut the light service down entirely. It tries again every couple of seconds and returns on its own the moment the screen is ordinary again.
- The app was also learning the wrong lesson. A store sheet draws inside the app that opened it, so when the light was refused it blamed that app and marked it permanently protected — and the light never returned there. A refusal is a fact about a moment; it was being kept as a verdict about an app.
- The notice now says why the light went. "Light paused — a secure screen is open. It returns when you leave it." It appears only while that is true, on the one surface still visible over such a sheet, and there is nothing asked of you.
- If you use the accessibility window, the light could come back blank and look like the permission had lapsed. It had not. Android restarts that kind of service freely — on a restart, under memory pressure, after an update — and everything it knows about your light lives in memory and starts empty. SonoLune only told it anything when the light CHANGED, so on a settled evening it was never told at all: running, permitted, and drawing nothing until you happened to touch something. It now asks for the current light the moment it comes back.
- Your drift timer could be erased as SonoLune opened. The length and the time remaining were written correctly and read correctly, and wiped in between: the app saves that timer from about thirty places, several of which run before the saved one has been read, and each wrote the empty value it was still holding. And the arc now starts again with the sound it belongs to rather than waiting for a tap — the audio survives a cold start, so it was the only thing still stopped.
- A third pack could play over a field that only holds two. The limit was checked before the download began and never again, and a download is minutes — so anything that started while there was room arrived into a field that had since filled. It is asked once more, against the field as it stands, immediately before the pack joins it.
- Granting Do Not Disturb still said the moment would run without it. The app opened Android's settings screen and asked whether you had granted it in the same breath — before you had seen the toggle. It waits for you to come back now, which fixes every permission the moments ask for, not only that one.
- One suggested focus moment now leads the moments rail, and the rest are where they were. Focus sat at the far end, behind every space moment, so the thing most people open Mind to start was the one thing they had to scroll to find.
- An outside eye-rest cue could be switched on and reach nobody. The breath cue and the quiet return are the only two ways it arrives, and turning both off left the bell ringing on its rhythm, waking the phone, and showing nothing. The last of the two can no longer be switched off; turning the whole thing off is still one tap and is the honest way to stop it.
- The card that shapes those cues was named and placed as though it belonged only to the one beside it. Three cues read that shape: the outside breath cue, Focus Eye Rest, and the breath pause the Presence Field offers while you rest in it. Two of the three live in other rooms, so anyone tuning it for the eye rest was also changing what Focus and the field show. It is called "How cues look" now, it says which three cues read it, and both other rooms link into it — Focus already offered "Shape Eye Rest in Lune Guard", and the field offers the same beside its breath pause. It stays reachable whether or not outside cues are switched on, because two of the three have nothing to do with that switch. The rhythm settings beside it are a different case and do rest while it is off: an interval and a window for a bell that is not ringing genuinely have nothing to act on.
- The Presence page leads with the reach you are actually using. It opened on settings for whole-phone coverage even when your field was limited to apps, with the section governing your live field below a screenful of controls. It also now says plainly that the behaviours around the field apply either way, which was true, documented, and written down only where no reader could see it.
- Releasing a field left its session arc still counting. "Release This Field" empties the room outright, and the drift timer went on running down over the silence it had just made — then faded and ended something that was not there. It ends with the room now. Pausing still holds the arc where it is, because a pause is a room you mean to come back to.
- The Hours sits with the moments it delivers. One line under the moments rail in Mind — let a moment arrive at the hour it was made for — because that rail is where somebody thinking about moments looks, and the switch had been living in Help and on Arrivals and nowhere near them. It is the same switch in all three places rather than a copy, so they cannot disagree about whether it is on.
- Scrolling the wallpaper page could put you into Night. Lune Field is built from the Night room, so it had quietly inherited the swipe that enters Night — and a page of wallpaper controls would hand you the whole evening environment you had not asked for. The swipe still scrolls and the moon still answers to it; only the door is closed. The line underneath that mentioned the Night filter has gone with it, since the wallpaper never needed one.
- And the notice explaining a paused light stays out of the way when it has nothing to explain. With the accessibility window granted, taps reach a protected screen through the light anyway — nothing is being held back, so saying so was simply wrong, and the line could stick there with no way to clear it. It is shorter too, because the shade was cutting the sentence in half and losing the part that promised the light would come back.
- SonoLune could open unreadable, and only ever in dark mode. The line that tells Android not to re-tint this app was missing from the theme the app actually runs under — present in light mode, absent in dark — so the system was free to recolour SonoLune's own window on every launch. One reader described opening it to a screen he could not read, from the first release he ever installed; switching to the Light room fixed it, and he had to do that again every single time. Another, running Android's own Extra Dim at night, saw only the bar at the bottom until he tapped something and scrolled. It is one setting, and it now lives in one shared place that every variant of the theme inherits, so no later edit can drop it from one mode and keep it in the other.
- And the app can no longer bury its own controls. The screen dimmer reached 88% black over SonoLune's own interface whenever the light was not covering the whole phone — with the only slider that lifts it underneath. That is a room with the light switch on the inside of a locked door, and one reader sat in it across every release without ever finding the way out. It is held to the same depth the Presence Field already stops at. Nothing about the dimmer changes for your phone; it is only SonoLune's own screen that is guaranteed to stay usable, because that is where the way out lives.
- One door makes sound, and it says so before you take it. A small speaker sits on the card for Fall asleep and nowhere else. A first tap that unexpectedly starts audio is the opposite of a calm arrival, so Focus applies its light and opens the timer instead, and offers the sound one tap further on, in the room that already asks.
- A door that cannot keep its promise without a permission explains itself first, then opens either way. Settle the whole phone and Calm my screen reach past this app and need the light window to do it; Let quiet moments find me needs notifications, or nothing can ever arrive. Each says what it needs and why before Android asks anything — the same sheet the Arc and Night already use — and “Not now” still opens the room with whatever the app can do unaided.
- The first atmosphere is now loud enough to hear on a phone speaker. The mixes are tuned for headphones, and their loudest layer sits between a quarter and two thirds: considered through earphones, close to nothing across a room. Somebody stopped using SonoLune over exactly that, and her brother had to show her by hand that the problem was the layer rather than the phone's volume. Only the very first one is lifted, the balance between layers is kept, and every slider is where it always was.
- The phone now says when it is struggling, instead of waiting to be asked. Feather mode has been here since 2.1 and lives in Help under “This device”, which is the last place somebody with a stuttering app goes looking — one reader with a slower chip only learned it existed by being told. SonoLune measures its own frames for a few seconds after launch and, if the phone is visibly labouring, offers Feather once. Once, ever. Nothing is switched on without a tap.
- SonoLune wears its own typeface now, on every phone. It had never named one, so it inherited whatever the phone's font picker last set — a different face and different measurements on every make, which is why the same screen looked unlike itself in different hands. It is Inter throughout, chosen for a tall x-height that makes small text read larger, open letterforms, and low stroke contrast, all of which matter more here than they would elsewhere. Your font SIZE setting still works exactly as it did.
- And text can no longer be smaller than it was drawn to be. The size setting was capped at the top and floored nowhere, so a phone set to Small shrank labels that already sit at eleven and thirteen points. Larger still scales all the way up; only shrinking below the design size is declined.
- Setting a time in a Lunar Arc could crash the app outright, at the largest font and display size a phone offers. The time picker clamps how large its own numerals are allowed to grow, and the sheet around it was clamping the same text a second time — two clamps compose to a range whose floor can reach its ceiling, and Flutter's own clamped scaler refuses that combination outright rather than silently picking one end. A phone already turned all the way up met a red screen the instant it tried to set a time. It reads the same size now through a scaler with no range to collide with, so the picker's own clamp has room to do its job.
- An arc that lands in the small hours now arrives without a sound or a buzz. Nothing is cancelled and no schedule is overruled — a Night arc is late on purpose — but between eleven at night and seven in the morning everything except a wake alarm comes in on a genuinely silent channel and waits in the shade. The channel it had been using was named silent and had vibration switched on. A wake alarm is untouched: waking you is the one thing it is for.
- A hand-raised night gets a morning too. The release that eases the light back over twelve minutes at dawn belonged only to nights some schedule governed — so somebody with no schedule at all, entering Night at eleven and falling asleep, woke to a screen still at a quarter brightness with the blue pulled out and nothing anywhere to give it back. Two dialogs in the app promised that release. It now covers a night nothing else is watching, using the same offline sunrise estimate the automatic ones use, and never sooner than the first dawn after the night began — so an unusual night runs long rather than being taken away from you.
- The card that shows you the way out could be wiped a second after it appeared. When the Presence Field has blurred an app past reading, closed its edges in and dulled its buttons, that card is the only thing left on screen that does anything — and it was the one cue in the app that nothing protected. A whisper from Mind landing next overwrote its sentence outright; an eye-rest cue landing first discarded it before it was ever drawn. Either way the screen let go with nothing on it to act on, and the next offer is four minutes away. It holds its place now for as long as it is meant to be readable, and every other cue waits.
- And it was worst on a whole-phone field, for a reason nothing in the app could see from the inside. How much a cue mattered had been decided by whether tapping it opened something — and a field covering your whole phone has nowhere to send you, so the card standing over the screen with the least left on it was read as the least important thing there. The same judgement was also cutting it short and making it see-through: it was shown for a little over half the time it asked for, at a strength that varied each time it appeared. It now lasts exactly as long as it says, fully solid, whichever reach the field has.
- An eye-rest cue falling inside that moment is skipped rather than drawn over it. A break comes back on its own rhythm; somebody shut inside a screen they cannot use has nothing that comes back for them. Nothing is lost that was reaching you anyway — the reminder still arrives in your shade if you have those switched on, and a break was already held back whenever the screen is off, so a phone you have put down behaves exactly as before.
- Two names pointed at the wrong place. Long-pressing the SonoLune icon offered "Home" and "Mind" as two separate shortcuts that opened the same screen — one door listed twice, and the first of them under a name the suite bar itself stopped using. And the card that shapes the cues, renamed in this same release so it would stop implying it belonged to only the one beside it, was still announced by its old name when opened from a shortcut, still naming one cue out of the three that read it. The duplicate is gone, the shortcut that stayed is called what the room is called, and the message names all three.
- Back would not leave. It walked a remembered list of up to twelve screens — a list that is saved and restored when SonoLune opens — so a back press on a freshly opened app stepped through places you had been in earlier sessions, and would not offer to close until it had worked through them all. Reported from devices as "back does not exit the app", and it was exactly that. Back now does what it does everywhere else on the phone: it closes whatever you opened on top, and from any suite it returns to Mind, and from Mind it closes SonoLune. Two presses from anywhere in the app. Reopening where you left off is a different thing and is untouched — it was only ever the back gesture that should not have been reading that list.
- A control that could not be used, and could not be got past. With Android's Adaptive Brightness on, the Screen Brightness slider is held back — it writes the phone's real brightness, and adaptive would undo anything it set a second later, so a live slider there would visibly do nothing. That much was right. What was missing was the rest of the sentence: it said "Disabled while Adaptive Brightness is active" and tapping it did nothing at all, so the state most likely to leave SonoLune hard to read was the one state with no way forward in it. The slider now says there is something to do, and offers to switch Adaptive Brightness off — after saying plainly that it is a phone-wide setting that stays off until you turn it back on. It asks first, and leaving it on changes nothing.
- The badge said the light was reaching your apps while the light was off. Switching it off from the notification bulb or Quick Settings stops the overlay being drawn at all, and the Light Suite went on showing a green tick over "Applied system-wide", listing warmth and gentler blue and dim as though every one of them were still carrying across. It says the light is off now, and where it was switched off, and that your settings are kept for when the bulb goes back on. Being held and being switched off are said differently, because only one of them lets go on its own.
- A moment that would not open answered with the name of a catalogue. Tapping an arrival you do not have yet — often one that had just invited itself at the hour it was made for — opened a card headed "Soundsoft Lab Sessions", which is a product nobody in that moment had gone looking for. It names the moment now, and says the thing that is actually true of arrivals: the ones marked Open to all stay open to everyone, always, and support opens the rest. Nothing about who can open what has changed; only what the card says.
- And the support card could come back every time you opened Arrivals. An invitation that arrives at its hour stays parked when something turns it away, so that it can still settle once you are free — a held space, a running focus session, Night. That is right for a door that opens later. It was also being applied to a moment that is simply not yours yet, which does not open later on its own, so the parked invitation put the support card in front of you on every single visit to the page. The offer is made once now, and answered once. The waiting kind still waits.
- The Hours invited people to moments they could not open, and could not have brought them anything else. It aimed at the whole library without asking whether the moment it picked was yours, so for anyone who has not supported the app, every invitation was a door that answered with the support card — a notification they had switched on themselves, keeping none of its promise. Only aiming at what you can open would have been worse rather than better: the one arrival open to everyone is named for 11pm, and nothing is delivered through the quiet night, so the feature would have read ON and been quietly incapable of ever firing. It now asks both questions together — can this be opened, and can it ever arrive — and the switch declines, in as many words, when the answer is that nothing can. The same rule it already followed for the light window, applied to the second thing it cannot promise. A switch already on closes itself the same way if that ever becomes true.
- And a second moment is now open to everyone. So the ground goes to true black — which is where nearly all the lit pixels are, and therefore nearly all the benefit — and the cards resting on it stay exactly where they were. The home-screen widget's sky lands on black too, since a widget sits in the open with your wallpaper around it.
- The first screen sorts itself by the hour. It offered “Fall asleep” at the top at nine in the morning, which reads as an app that has not noticed what time it is. Focus leads through the working day, Night through the evening, sleep from nine at night onward. Nothing is ever hidden — the list is reordered, never filtered, because somebody works nights and somebody naps at two in the afternoon. A short line above it says which part of the day each door suits best, so doors that move between visits read as attention rather than as the screen being unsteady.
- Two of those doors are ones you would never have gone looking for. Rest my eyes opens Lune Guard, which nobody finds by browsing because the people who need it are already too tired to browse. Put a moon on my home screen opens the living wallpaper — never near the top, because nobody arrives needing one, but it is the most striking thing SonoLune makes and worth meeting. And the door that quiets the apps that pull is an invitation rather than a telling-off: naming a habit and implying a verdict on it would be the one card on that screen doing so, in an app whose whole position is a field rather than a fence — and it would promise something the Presence Field does not do, because nothing here blocks or stops anything.
- Fall asleep and the door beside it no longer read as the same door. That door was called Enter Night — the one card on the screen that named a feature rather than something a person wants, on the screen where you are meant to know nothing yet. - Two more titles were sharpened the same way. "Ease off the scroll" made "the scroll" a noun only this app uses; it is "Quiet the apps that pull" now, and its line says the actual mechanism — they dim the longer you stay, nothing is blocked. "Let it find me" left "it" undefined on the one screen where nothing had been defined yet; it is "Let quiet moments find me". Neither door's behaviour changed, only what it says about itself before you have used it once.
- And the screen says what SonoLune is before it asks what you want. The opening line named three suites — focus, rest and presence — which is the menu rather than the promise, and undersold the app: nothing in it would let you guess at a screen dimmer, a blue light filter, a sleep sound mixer, eye-rest breaks or a living wallpaper. It now reads as a calmer phone for sleep, focus, putting it down and everyday life, because SonoLune is not three modes you enter — it is a layer the phone runs under.
- A Focus session you had not started yet showed 00:00 at the size of a headline. It is the largest thing on the screen and the one shape that reads as broken rather than ready — the first thing a new person met when they opened Focus. A session with no length has no time to show, so the crescent sits there instead, and the numerals arrive with the first length you tap. The Mind Programmer opened on the same dead 00:00 and now does the same. Its clock counts up rather than down, so what it waits for is the first tick rather than a length: a session you have paused keeps its numerals, and so does one that has finished, because both have something to say. Only a clock that has never moved is replaced.
- The Light Suite could turn itself grey once you scrolled it. The page background was being painted by the surface preview, which meant that past about the halfway mark the whole room took on the preview's neutral swatch — a flat mid-grey behind every heading and slider, unlike any other room in the app, and most visible when you arrived from a door that had just applied a warm light. A page's background is the app's ground now, and nothing else.
- The one sentence a newcomer needs now reaches every room. Mind is where everything you start comes back to, and nothing on screen says so — the note that does had been living inside Sound, because until this release there was one way to arrive anywhere and it always opened Sound playing rain. Seven doors now land somebody in Night, Focus, Presence, Light, Arrivals, eye rest or the wallpaper page, and the note reached none of them. It is drawn once, for whatever room you are in, and it withholds itself on Mind — telling somebody standing in the hall where the hall is reads as an app that is not looking. “Got it” has always meant everywhere.
- An attention pack now says what it just changed. Tapping one writes a dozen settings at once — the apps, the delay, the grace, and which of the three quieting levers apply — and those levers live as switches further down the same page, so anyone who scrolled afterwards met a screen of toggles with no way to tell which the tap had set. It names them. And where a pack holds one back it says why: the watching one leaves words and colour alone, because blurring a film is not reducing stimulation, it is destroying the thing you sat down for.
- There are two ways to support SonoLune, and the page explained the difference only after asking you to choose between them. One contribution keeps the app forever. A small monthly patronage keeps it while it holds. Either way it is the same app, and the only real question is what happens when you stop. A short table said exactly that, and it sat below the three one-time cards, so the shape of the choice arrived after three prices instead of before them. It now runs above both. Which one leads has not moved.
- The notice over a locked refinement was quietly arguing against the cheaper way in. Its first line read as reassurance, a one-time contribution and no subscription, sitting directly above the line that offers the monthly patronage. That second line was set smaller and fainter than anything else on the notice, which made the least expensive door the hardest thing to read at the one moment somebody decides whether to pay at all. Both lines carry the same weight now, and the first says what it gives you rather than what the other one is not.
- True black now reaches every screen, not two. It was applied where the ground was asked for by name — the Light Suite, Sound, and the bars at the top and bottom — and every other room carried its own hard-coded near-black, so switching it on produced a black frame around a page that had not changed. Twenty-three screens follow it now, from Mind and Focus and Night through to Help, the Arc and the Lab. The Arc's daylight field keeps its parchment, because a page that is deliberately paper is not a ground that should go black.
- And the warm light no longer buries the room you change it in. The dim was already held to a level that keeps SonoLune's own controls readable; the warm and blue wash was not. Arriving from a door that applies a gentle light landed you on the Light Suite reading every heading and slider through an olive film — at the exact moment those labels are the thing you need to read. Held to the same ceiling now. What the light does to your screen is unchanged; only what it does to its own controls is.
- The light presets stopped looking broken. That row scrolls, and an edge fade was the only thing saying so — but it covered the last twentieth of the width, about forty pixels, so the fourth preset was sliced through the middle of its own word with a hard edge. It was reported twice as the presets being clipped, which is exactly what a cut looks like. It dissolves now, which is the difference between a layout fault and "there is more this way".
- And the note that says where home is now reaches Arrivals. That page is declared outside the suite shell — no bottom bar, none of the shell's furniture — so of the eight doors on the first screen, the one that lands there was the only one that never explained where everything comes back to. Same note, same key: dismissing it there dismisses it everywhere.

Long-pressing the SonoLune icon already offered Focus, Mind, Light and Lune Field — four doors that each open a room and leave it exactly as they found it. Enter Night is the odd one out among them: it starts Night the same way the door on the first screen does, with the same guard if Focus is holding the field and the same word if Night is already running. Which shortcuts a launcher actually shows is Android's decision, not this app's.
- The Almanac stopped selling what it never had to sell. Its footer read "Free, and always will be" — the one sentence on a page about moonlight that was about money, at the exact moment somebody was looking at the sky rather than a price. Nothing on that page is locked and nothing there was ever going to be, so the line had nothing to defend. Focus Flow is a tab; every visit added one more. The controller is held directly from the moment its listener is attached now, which is the only moment doing so is certainly safe.

It sits in a row that gives the pill the whole of the remaining width now.

Arrivals is a lazy list, and its cards sit below the fold at the window sizes the sweep uses; the wind-down offer in the Presence Field only exists after a real release, which nothing in that sweep ever performs. ## A way in

- Eight doors on the first screen, one tap each, and every one of them produces the thing it names rather than another screen of prose. Existing installs never pass through a first run, so the same eight doors sit in Help and Settings under "Start from a door".
- The order follows the hour. The list is never filtered — somebody works nights and somebody naps at two — only what sits at the top changes.
- Falling asleep and entering Night read alike and are not the same door: one is sound-led with the light following it down, the other is the whole evening environment, silent. At eleven at night both rank high and sit together.
- A door that needs a permission says so first, in SonoLune's own words, through the same sheet Night and Focus already use. No raw Android dialog is ever fired at somebody who has not read a sentence about it.
- The nine-page tour is unchanged and still there, on a button beneath the doors, and reachable afterwards from Help.
- Three door titles were sharpened further. Enter Night became "Settle the whole phone", so it named what happens rather than a feature; "Ease off the scroll" became "Quiet the apps that pull"; "Let it find me" became "Let quiet moments find me", naming the thing doing the finding. What each door does has not moved.

## True black that reaches the room

- A centralised ground can only reach a surface that lets it show. Ten rooms painted their own full-screen near-black as the first thing in the body, so the ground was never a visible pixel on them.
- Those backdrops now collapse with the ground. Only the bottom layer does: veils, stars, horizon glows and cards keep their lift, because on true black that lift is what tells a card from the space around it.
- One exception, made deliberately. Mind's horizon lift is skipped when the ground is black and there is no atmosphere photo to lift — on an unlit ground it is a large grey wash, which is the exact glow the switch was thrown to remove.

## Readable at any size

- Thirty-three layouts across every suite could not hold their contents at maximum font and display size. Six were photographed; the rest were found by measuring.
- Not one of the app's thirty-five dialogs could scroll. Thirty now do. Landscape at twice the type size is a window about as tall as a title, two lines and a text field, and a dialog that cannot scroll has no answer to that but to overflow.
- A hard-coded gap is a fraction of the smallest window it will ever see. The Lunar Arc and guide sheets kept a flat clearance above the nav bar that took nearly half a landscape window, and then subtracted it a second time. The guide sheet's pages measured zero pixels: it showed its furniture and none of its content.
- The splash printed "SonoLune" across the moon's face on a short window. The light notification cut "Tap the bulb to lift the light." in half — the one place the way out of a phone-wide field is written down.
- The instrument matters more than the fixes: seven suites and nineteen pages are now pumped at two type sizes, in three window shapes, and each failure names its own file and line.
- The largest size could crash the app outright rather than merely misrender it. Setting a time in a Lunar Arc built two text-scale clamps that composed into an impossible range, and Flutter's own scaler refused it with a red screen.
- Two more cards were found after the sweep first shipped, in the two shapes it could not reach on its own: Arrivals' cards sit in a lazy list below the fold at the sweep's window sizes, and the Presence Field's wind-down offer only exists after a real release. Seeded and scrolled to, both overflowed — one by 370 pixels — and both are fixed the same way, with Wrap.
- A third shape the sweep could not see at all: a Sound pack tile guessed its own width from the screen instead of the rail it actually sat in, three paddings deeper, and was clipped by forty-four pixels with no yellow stripe — a silent clip is not an overflow, so nothing had ever failed loudly enough to be found. It measures the rail it is actually given now.
- And Mind's own completion card overflowed twice at once in a short landscape window — sixty-three pixels short at the bottom of a fixed-height card, and its own button beaten to sixty-one pixels of width by the dismiss ✕ sitting next to it. The card scrolls within its own bound now; the button is the side that yields.
- A Living Moment card's "Open once" pill ran off its own edge at the DEFAULT font, not only the largest one — a Spacer splitting the remaining width in half rather than giving all of it to the pill that needed it.

## Night, and the outside world

- A switch in Night's settings holds calls and notifications back for as long as Night has the screen.
- It is shown whether or not Night is running, because it governs the night you are in rather than a decision about tomorrow.
- Everything returns the moment Night ends, however it ends. Turning the switch off mid-night gives the world back at once.
- Nothing is read and nothing is recorded. The permission is Android's own and is named plainly in the sheet that asks for it.

## Presence, still finding its shape

- A chosen app sent to a floating window ended the field, stopped the sound bed, and reset a dwell clock that had been earning its time honestly.
- Paused is not gone. The field now tells an app that is merely unfocused from one that has genuinely been left, and holds the clock instead of spending it.
- The clock is held, never the settling. Time spent watching in a floating window is real time with the thing you chose, so it goes on counting.
- Covering the floating window itself is not built. It would need permission to read the contents of your screen, and that is a decision rather than a piece of code.
- A notice that read as stuck. The bar explaining that "Cover more surfaces" had stepped aside held the bottom of the screen for seven seconds, which is a long time to sit over something somebody is trying to read. It is four seconds now, and says the same thing in half the words. The undo beside it is unchanged.

## What a patronage opens

- One page now lists everything in the Lab: the sessions, Passages, Tonal and Human Warmth, and every composed moment, read from the live feed rather than written out by hand.
- It marks what is free to everyone forever, and it says plainly what closes if a patronage ends and what stays yours whatever happens.
- It sells nothing on its own. The page opens from the support page, and its one button walks back to the card that carries the price.

## A page that stopped selling you what you had

- The patron card read the raw subscription flag rather than the one that folds in a tier’s included months, so a supporter holding the Lab for another two years saw the plain offer.
- The sentence beneath it said the charge would wait for those months to settle. It would not. That is corrected, and the real date is named instead, worked out from the same purchase record the rule itself reads, so the words cannot promise a different day than the app enforces.
- The offer is still there and still works. It is quiet now, because a filled button reading "become a patron" sat directly under a paragraph saying there was nothing to buy, and a card should not argue with itself.
- What a tier means to somebody already paying monthly is written down for the first time: you keep everything if the monthly ever ends, and the included months would let you stop paying it.
- None of this changes who can buy what. Every purchase that worked before this release works after it.

## A guard fitted to one door

- Nearly everything in this release is the same fault seen from a different room. A check was written at the place somebody happened to walk in, and then trusted as though it stood everywhere.
- The whole-phone caution guarded the arm button. Three other ordinary actions reached the same state without passing it.
- The protected-surface check was correct and simply stopped running once a field went system-wide.
- The pack limit was asked before a download and never again, so minutes of network time sat between the question and the answer.
- A permission was read while its own settings screen was still open, so the answer was always no.
- None of these is visible by reading the function that holds it. Each is correct on its own. The fault is always in which paths reach it, and that only shows up on a real phone.

## The field that covers everything

- A field limited to chosen apps has a boundary anyone finds by accident: leave the app and the quiet lifts. A whole-phone field has none, and at depth it is meant to make the screen genuinely hard to stay on.
- That is the point of it, which is exactly why it is worth agreeing to knowingly — and why the way out is named before it is needed rather than during.
- Choosing "Only these apps" and choosing nothing is still a whole-phone field. The app now treats it as one everywhere, instead of in one place out of twelve.
- Its two clocks — the gentle opening, and the ending that comes on its own — are visible and running for it now.
- Removing a chosen app while the field is armed now asks once before it happens, at a lighter weight than the whole-phone caution beside it. Building the list itself, before anything is running, stays instant — that screen must never be interrogated.

## Moments that could not arrive

- A composed moment names its hour in the same words a person would use, and something has to turn those into a time.
- That reader knew five of the ten ways the founding moments are written — four by name, and one plain clock form. The other five were skipped every pass, with nothing anywhere reporting it.
- Two more name hours between eleven at night and seven in the morning, and are held back deliberately. A moment that wakes you has failed at the one thing this app is for.
- The library is now checked against the reader, so a moment written in new words fails loudly here rather than quietly never arriving.

## Protected screens and the light

- Android refuses touches on a protected sheet, from the Play Store or a UPI app, while anything is drawn over it. That is a real protection and it is working as intended.
- SonoLune lifts the light when it sees one. It had stopped looking about a second after you left the app, for any field covering the whole phone.
- When it did lift, it could not return without a force-stop, and it blamed the app you were buying from rather than the sheet.
- All three are fixed, and the notice in your shade now says what happened while it lasts.
- Separately, and for the same reason a thing can look lost without being lost: the accessibility window came back empty after Android restarted it, because nothing was told to fill it again until the light next changed.

## The screen you could not read

- A style redeclared for dark mode replaces the light-mode one outright — Android does not merge the two — and the dark copy had lost the line that forbids the system from re-tinting this app.
- So it held in light mode and was absent in dark, which is why it never showed up here and did show up, every single launch, for the people who reported it.
- Everything shared now lives in one place that every variant inherits, and that place holds exactly one item so nothing else can ride along with it.
- Separately, and for the same reason: SonoLune's own dimmer could reach 88% black over SonoLune's own controls. The depth your phone gets is unchanged; only this app's screen is now guaranteed to stay usable, because the way out is on it.

## The first two minutes

- Seven early readers described the same wall: too many options, too much text, no obvious first move. One never redeemed a free code because he could not work out the basics.
- The depth was never the problem — the people who read the nine pages liked them. Being the only entrance was.
- Eight doors now, each naming what it will do and then doing it. Sound starts at exactly one of them and says so first.
- And the things that made a first minute go wrong quietly: an atmosphere too faint to hear on a speaker, a lite mode nobody could find, and type that shrank below the size it was drawn at.
