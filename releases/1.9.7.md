# SonoLune 1.9.7

**The last insider pass before 2.0, a first field that lands true, a match that asks first, one clear way to move, and the whole app made lighter on the phone** · 2 Jul 2026

*An Insider build: it went to testers and never reached the public store.*

The last insider pass before the public 2.0, a quiet cleanup that smooths the final rough edges before release. Choosing your first sound field now lands the soundscape strip on the very card you picked, even for the quieter fields deeper in the shelf like Quiet Room, so what plays and what is lit agree from the first open. In Focus, matching a room now waits for screen-overlay access before it turns Calm Light on, the same way the Calm Light switch already does, so it can never read as on while nothing paints; the sound matches at once and the light follows once access is given. And the full-screen left and right swipe between suites has been retired, since it could fire by accident against a slider or a scrolling row; the suite bar and its tabs, with their soft directional slide, are now the single, deliberate way across, and crossing Light and Night keeps its calm cross-fade. Running underneath is the broad steadiness pass that holds every screen together at the largest accessibility fonts and display sizes, so nothing clips or trails off the edge. And beneath what the eye can see, a deep performance pass taught the app's always-breathing touches, the suite bar's glow, Mind's moon and aurora, the hearth's warmth at the screen's edges, to redraw only on the frames where their light truly changes, to keep their motion inside their own layer, and to rest entirely while unseen, so the same calm asks far less of older and lower-end phones and sips less battery, with not one pixel rendered differently. Nothing here changes what anything does, it is the same SonoLune, simply surer on its feet as it steps into 2.0.

## Highlights

- Your first field opens where you chose: picking a starting sound field, even one deeper down the shelf like Quiet Room, now settles the soundscape strip onto that exact card rather than a few short of it, so what you hear and what is lit agree from the very first open.
- Match light and sound asks before it lights: matching a Focus room light now waits for screen-overlay access the same way the Calm Light switch does, so it can never show as on while nothing paints. The sound matches straight away, and the light follows once access is given.
- One clear way to move between suites: the full-screen left and right swipe has been retired. It could trigger by accident against a slider or a scrolling row, so the suite bar and its tabs are now the single, deliberate way across, and crossing Light and Night keeps its calm cross-fade.
- Night scrolls true: a drag that began in the quiet space between cards was being claimed by the exit gesture and moved nothing, and a slow drag from the very top quietly went nowhere, so the page sometimes felt stuck. Every drag scrolls now, and pulling down from the top to leave Night answers from anywhere on the page, not only the empty spots between the cards.
- Full pages open true, not blank: the Field Journal (from the "Release archive" note or the first launch after an update) and the support page (from any "Unlock" or "Support SonoLune" tap) had begun landing on a blank dark screen. Both now open as their own full page, cleanly above the suite bar, with a back that returns you exactly where you were.
- Comfortable at every size, everywhere: Night's intensity presets, Light's live preview, the system-presence rows, the Mind moment list and the Lunar Arc tiles all hold their full text and layout at the largest fonts now, no clipping, no trailing "…", and no small pane that quietly swallows a scroll.
- Whole even at the far edge of accessibility: with the largest font, the fullest display size and bold all set together, the shared field poster, the Save-a-Light-phrase sheet, Night's presets and wallpaper names, the About rows, the week-day chips and the Focus room strip each stay in frame, and saving a custom Light phrase always settles cleanly.
- Every note reaches its button: the alarm and notification-permission dialogs in Lunar Arc, the "Cover More Surfaces" note in Light, and the Save-soundscape sheet all scroll to their action at the largest fonts, so the button is never pushed off-screen, and the arc tiles' time and note, the custom-minute field, the wake-ring buttons and the Labs finish options all hold their words in place too.
- Steady wherever you land it: the Light permission prompt now scrolls you to the exact permission row on any screen size, resolution or orientation, instead of guessing an offset that could land on the Warm slider.
- Every locked Light control lands you on the right spot: tapping a preset, slider, or moment while system-wide light is still off now scrolls precisely to the row that grants it, on any screen size, resolution, or orientation.
- Steadier still at the largest fonts: starting a Focus without a length scrolls right to the minutes field, the Lunar Arc time picker holds its digits without clipping, and the new-release note scrolls within its own frame instead of overflowing.
- Lighter on the device, identical to the eye: the always-breathing touches across the app, the suite bar's glow, Mind's moon, tilt and aurora, the hearth's warmth in Light, now redraw only on the frames where their light truly steps to a new shade, keep their motion inside their own layer, and rest their engines entirely while invisible or under reduced motion, so the same calm runs noticeably smoother and gentler on older and lower-end phones, with not one pixel rendered differently.

## A final pass over the rough edges

- Choosing a first sound field from deeper in the shelf (Quiet Room and its neighbours) now lands the strip on that exact card. Before, a further card could settle the strip a little short and leave a different soundscape lit while yours played, which read as confusing on the very first open.
- In Focus, Match light and sound now checks for the screen-overlay permission before turning Calm Light on, exactly as the Calm Light switch already did. Without access it matches the room sound at once and gently offers the permission, rather than flipping a light that could not actually paint.
- The universal left and right swipe that switched suites is gone. It could fire unintentionally when you dragged a control or a horizontal strip inside a suite; the tab bar, with its soft directional slide, is now the one way across, so a move is always something you meant.
- Night's page no longer feels stuck: a drag that started in the quiet space between cards was being claimed whole by the exit gesture, and a slow drag from the very top was cancelled by an inverted guard, so only a flick reliably moved the page. Both were unwound. Every drag scrolls now with a gentle bounce at the edges, drags that begin between the cards coast on release like any other, and the pull-down farewell at the top answers over the cards themselves rather than only the gaps between them.
- The support page and the Field Journal are full-screen pages, and reaching them from inside a suite had begun dropping them behind the suite's own moving frame, where they rendered as a blank dark screen. They now open on the app's top layer, above the suite bar, and each one's back returns you to the exact suite you left.

## Held steady at every size

- Night's intensity presets, Light's all-at-once preview, the system-presence rows, the Mind moment queue and the Lunar Arc tiles all hold their full text and layout at the largest accessibility fonts, words shrink to fit rather than trailing into "…", and the Lunar Arc moon no longer overlaps its "Ready" badge.
- A saved Sound atmosphere returns highlighted after a cold start, "fade sound at session end" no longer crowds its card edge, the Help guide title shows in full rather than dotting, and a night arc's note reads on two lines instead of being cut short.
- Setting up a Mind arc scrolls as one page now, so a drag never lands in a small inner pane that quietly does nothing.
- Labs notes plainly that Software Rendering trades smoothness for compatibility, so it is clear why scrolling eases when it is on alongside a living field.
- Every dialog reaches its button at the largest fonts: the alarm and notification-permission notes in Lunar Arc, the "Cover More Surfaces" note in Light, and the Save-soundscape sheet all scroll so their action is never pushed off-screen.
- A last sweep of tight spots holds too: a Lunar Arc tile's time and its note keep their place instead of clipping or breaking a word, and the custom-minute field, the wake-ring buttons and the Labs finish options (None / Gentle / Deep) shrink to fit rather than trailing a letter onto a second line.
- And a few more hold at any size or resolution: a locked Light control scrolls you exactly onto the row that grants system-wide light (never onto a stray slider), starting a Focus without a length lands on the minutes field, the Lunar Arc time picker keeps its digits whole, and the new-release note scrolls within a bounded frame instead of overflowing.
- One last pass at the most extreme combination, the largest font, the fullest display size and bold text all at once: the shared field poster now scales as a single piece to stay whole inside its frame, and the Save-a-Light-phrase sheet, Night's intensity presets and wallpaper-choice names, the About rows, the week-day chips in a Lunar Arc draft, the support-sheet section titles and the Focus room strip all keep their words and layout in place rather than trailing off or clipping a card.
- Saving a custom Light phrase now settles on the frame after its dialog closes, clear of a rare teardown-timing edge, so the save always lands quietly with its gentle word of confirmation.
- A final follow-up pass held a last handful of edges at that same extreme setting: the "Support SonoLune" pill, Light's "Colour chorus" heading and its "Save current cycle" row, the update note's own buttons, Night's time row and its "Night environment is settled" line all shrink or wrap to fit now rather than spilling past their frame, and the Night permission note scrolls gently to its buttons when its words grow taller than the screen.
- Returning to Sound finds your soundscape again: the gallery's sideways scroll and the page's own scroll had been sharing one remembered place, so parking on a soundscape deeper in the gallery and switching suites brought you back to the end of the page rather than the section you left. Page and gallery now each keep their own place, the remote sound-pack row alongside them, and the Light colour rails, preset strip and the Focus room strip were quietly steadied from the same shared-place fault.

## Quieter for the hardware too

- The suite bar's slow breathing glow rides on every screen, and it had been redrawing its glass, blur and glows sixty times a second around the clock. It now redraws only on the frames where its light truly steps to a new shade, the same breath to the eye, and the app's single largest standing cost is simply gone.
- In Mind, the moon, its tilt and its aurora breathe inside their own layer now, so a breath no longer asks the whole page to repaint with it; the resting suggestion cards beside the top one hold truly still instead of quietly re-rendering every frame; and the drifting clouds and the water's shimmer each settle into a layer of their own rather than dragging the veil and stars along with every step.
- In Light, the hearth's warmth at the screen's edges rests its engine entirely whenever it sits below its threshold, and it, the living gradients and the moonlit divider all rest under reduced motion, where before they kept the whole app's frame clock running for a glow that was not even there. The "Enable system-wide light" invitation pulses within its own frame now instead of repainting the page around it.
- The session-complete overlay's small breathing mark had been re-blurring the whole frosted card with every pulse for as long as the overlay stayed open; it breathes alone now, and the Shape-SonoLune invitation card does the same.
- When SonoLune rests in the background, Mind's tilt sensor now rests with it and wakes as you return, and the atmosphere's haze no longer repaints the full screen every second for a star rotation that was never drawn, both a quiet, steady saving of battery.
- None of this changes a single rendered pixel: the same frames appear in the same rhythm, and only the identical frames in between are no longer paid for.
