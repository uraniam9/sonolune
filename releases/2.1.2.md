# SonoLune 2.1.2

**A shorter way in, and listening that finally counts** · 9 Aug 2026

*An Insider build: it went to testers and never reached the public store.*

Three quiet repairs, all of them about being met. The first atmosphere — rain and warm light, built to be the first thing felt rather than read — sat behind eight pages of description, and Skip walked past it into a silent room; Skip now carries you to it instead, and it still opens only because you choose it. The onboarding named five rooms when there have been seven since 1.9.2, so a newcomer was told to look for five, found seven, and was never told Field and Arc existed at all. And a sustained atmosphere finally counts as time spent here: SonoLune recorded Focus, Night, held spaces and reflections, and recorded nothing for Light and Sound, which meant someone who came every evening for the sound was told by Rhythm that they had never been here, and could never be offered the quiet invitation that exists to bring people gently back. Sharing was repaired too — the card called "Share the field" was sending a plain store address rather than the field itself — and a finished focus session now offers, quietly and only from the second of the week, a way to hand this to someone. Nothing is taken away.

- A one-minute session no longer reads "1 minutes", in either place it was written.

## The field moves with the sound

- The Living Field could already be told to follow the Sound Atmosphere, and it did — to a rhythm fixed in advance. Three unchanging rates that knew only whether something was playing, so open rain, a hearth, a forest under wind and a slow tide all breathed in exactly the same way. The light was moving near the sound rather than with it.
- Each room has its own movement now, read from what is actually playing. Rain is fine and quick and barely leans; a forest under wind leans slowly and widely, which is the very thing the field reading calls being beneath moving branches; a tide is slower than either and moves far more; a hearth is small and irregular and never settles into a rhythm you could count.
- Layers combine into something neither of them was. The largest movement in a room is the one you notice, so it leads rather than being averaged away, and a crowded field does not simply move at maximum — otherwise every rich atmosphere would end up moving alike, which was the original complaint arriving by another road.
- The inner layers quieten it rather than animating it. A low pulse or a guiding tone is there to steady a room, so the field holds still around it.
- It reads the same thing the field reading is written from, so the light and the sentence on screen can never disagree about the room you are in.
- And it can never flash. Every rate is capped an octave below anything that could read as a flicker, the wash is floored so it may breathe but never blink out, and the ceiling is enforced again at the last moment before it is drawn. This app has refused strobe twice; that has not changed.
- None of which was reaching the screen. The four values the field moves by crossed from Flutter to the overlay through a hand-written list of key names, and were not on it — computed correctly, sent correctly, read correctly at the far end, and dropped in silence in between. Depth arrived as zero, and at zero the field is perfectly still, so a lost setting looked exactly like a deliberate calm.
- The wash breathes at a depth that does not saturate. An earlier pass raised its swing fourfold — measured, correctly, against a clock that could not yet advance, so what was being scaled was a constant and the result was never seen moving. Once time started, that same number drove a brightness change of over a hundred percent which clipped hard against both limits: every room ended up at the identical range, which is why they all looked the same and all of them pulsed. Rooms differ again, and the swing is a breath rather than a flash.
- The slow movement carries it now, and the fast detail rides on top. Shimmer runs up to a beat and a half a second and rain sits near one; weighted as heavily as they were, they drove the brightness about once a second, which is a pulse. The large sway — one movement every four to twenty seconds — does almost all of the work, and the fine terms are texture again, which is what they were for.
- One mode, the smooth one, had not been given the motion at all and looked the same whatever was playing. All of this is depth only: every rate stays exactly where it was capped, an octave below anything that could read as a flicker.
- Strobe and Flash are held under three flashes a second. They set their own rate, outside the caps everything else here obeys, and reached about nineteen and nine — the first of those sitting in the middle of the range photosensitive response peaks at. They had never once flashed, because the clock below had them frozen too, so this was a hazard waiting on a repair rather than one anyone had met. Both are slower now, and the sound no longer multiplies the rate: loudness moves depth everywhere else in this app, and this was the one place it could push a frequency upward instead.
- And underneath both of those, the clock had stopped. Time was being read as seconds since 1970 — about 1.79 billion of them — and held in a number that carries only seven digits, so the nearest value it could represent was a hundred and twenty-eight seconds away. A second later was the same instant. A minute later was the same instant. The field was not moving slightly; it was standing perfectly still and stepping every two minutes, and every repair aimed at how far it moved was multiplying a number that never changed. It counts from when it opens now, where a tenth of a second is still a tenth of a second.

## Across surfaces, actually

- The light preview can hold a bright app, a dark one, or colour-rich content, and can sit still, scroll, or alternate. Choosing Alternating quietly threw the first choice away: it drew two flat rectangles, white and near-black, whatever card was picked. So "Light across surfaces" appeared to stop working the moment you asked for the one motion it matters most for — the switch between a bright app and a dark one, which is the thing a filter is actually judged on and the only thing a still preview cannot show.
- The cards were working the whole time. Static and Scroll both honoured them; this one mode was discarding the answer. It now alternates between the real surfaces, with their cards and their content, so what is previewed is the switch people actually make.
- Colour-rich content and the neutral card alternate against a bright app rather than against themselves, because that is the contrast that strains eyes — and a surface alternating with itself would show nothing at all.

## The gathering

- A focus session is spent in other apps. Every way this app had of marking an ending — a low pulse, a haptic, the screen darkening at the edges — lived on the Focus page, which is the one screen nobody is looking at while a session runs. On the screen you actually are looking at, the last thirty seconds were identical to the first, and then it simply stopped.
- So the field gathers. Over the final half minute it draws in: a third deeper by the close, and its movement stilling until it is almost held. The ending is felt as an approach rather than announced.
- It does not warm, quicken, flash or count. That was a deliberate choice against a more urgent one — urgency is the thing the Presence Field exists to take out of a screen, and a pressure cue arriving over whatever you happen to be doing would be the app becoming what it was built as an answer to. A room drawing breath says the same thing without asking anything of you.
- The curve is back-loaded, so the first twenty seconds are barely perceptible and you should not be able to say when it began. And it is held rather than frozen at the end — a tenth of the movement remains, because nothing here blinks out.
- It reaches both overlay paths, so an ending feels the same whichever one happens to be drawing. And the fix went where the truth was: a lane called focus progress had been sent to this part of the app for a long time and read by nothing at all, so the gathering rides the seconds actually remaining instead — which is what "how close is the end" honestly means.

## A moment that moves

- A Living Moment composed three of the four things a room is made of — the light of an hour, a colour laid over it, and what you hear. The fourth, the field that actually moves, was left wherever it happened to be. So Natural Forest gave you a green veil and canopy sound over a wall that did not stir, which is a photograph of a forest rather than a forest.
- Seven of them carry their own field now. Forest leans slowly and widely between canopy shade and the green a leaf turns with the sun behind it. Firelight runs quicker and shorter between deep ember and the flare above it. Dawn is the slowest of them, one long movement from blue hour into first gold. Shore travels furthest, deep water to the pale of shallow over sand.
- A moment does not switch "follow the sound" on for you. It did, on the argument that this is what makes a field feel alive — true, and still the wrong call: that is a preference about how the field behaves everywhere, not a property of any one room, and a person could turn it off and find it back on after opening any moment. A setting that will not stay set is worse than a field that only drifts. Turned on once, it stays, and then every room breathes.
- And the sound is what makes it live rather than merely coloured. The field already reads its movement from what is playing, so canopy sound tells the light to lean like branches without either being told about the other. A moment supplies the colour; what you are listening to supplies the weather.
- Five rooms were also playing the wrong thing, which an audit of what each sound actually contains found rather than what each was called. Moon Library was an ocean, in a library — while the preset written for that room, rain against a window with a lamp and a page, had never once been used. Incandescent Room was a sea inside a house named for a steady lamp. Shore Shade and Atrium Mist both carried thunder, in rooms promising a held horizon and a silver-green drift. And The Long Dusk was a coastal dusk, handing surf to someone who chose it for a sky.
- And a sixth was two rooms that were the same room. Firelight and Lantern Rain both played fire and rain in a sheltered space — the same ingredients, which is fair, but also the same mix, which made them one soundscape wearing two names. In the room called Firelight the rain was the louder of the two. A hearth leads with its fire and hears rain beyond the window; a lamplit room leads with the rain and keeps a small flame in it. Same layers, opposite rooms, and now they sound it.
- That one could not be solved by choosing a different sound, because no fire-led one exists and inventing one reaches sixteen files. A composed room is a set of layers AND a balance between them, and the balance is what was missing — so a moment may now name its own, while the sound stays recognisably the preset it is. Nobody moved a slider, so nothing behaves as though they had.
- Each is now the sound its name already promised, and three of them are presets that existed and were never reached. Two rooms had been wearing the identical veil colour as well, a few cards apart, while promising different hours — a filament runs deeper and redder than a dawn does, and now it looks it.
- Two rooms deliberately have none. Incandescent Room and Fluorescent Studio are named for a steady lamp and clear task light — a drifting colour would make them worse rooms, not richer ones. They leave the field exactly as they found it rather than switching off one you chose.
- It is gated the way an arrival already was: a room that asks for a living field gets one only if you have the field to begin with, and otherwise composes without it. Nothing is refused, no colours are half-applied, and a field you set yourself is never quietly replaced by another room's.
- And one room is built to be fallen for. The Long Dusk moves through three colours rather than two — last gold, then rose, then the blue that arrives before dark — because dusk is the one thing in nature that does, and never goes back. It is set in the mode that slides rather than steps, so the room is never quite the colour it was a moment ago and never announces the change.
- And a room that cannot show you its field says so, instead of arriving quietly reduced. It used to compose without one in silence, and silence reads as completeness — someone who has never seen the canopy move would reasonably conclude that a still green wash IS Natural Forest, and that everyone gets the same one. The moment now names the part that is absent in its own words: the canopy moving between shade and sun, ember light rising and settling, the slow turn from blue hour into gold.
- What it offers is the free thing, not the price. There is a room where the whole of it opens for thirty seconds, and that is what the line points at — no cost is mentioned anywhere in it. Someone who has felt the field has something to decide about; someone who never saw it has nothing, and finding out later that it existed is the version that feels like something withheld.
- The rooms are told apart by every lane the field has, not only by colour. Depth was the one never curated — each moment landed on a single floor, so how much of a screen a room occupies was identical in all of them, which is a large part of why they read alike. A fire fills a room now and a library barely tints one; nothing shares a depth, a pace or a full set of settings with anything else.
- And a third colour appears only where there genuinely is one. Dawn passes from blue hour through rose into gold and never turns back; a fire has its ember, its flame and the pale gold above it. A forest is shade and sun, a lamp is lamp and dark — padding those out would be decoration pretending to be observation.
- It was also, briefly, impossible to find. The rail is weighted by the hour — two or three rooms are pulled forward and the rest keep their order behind them — and this one was in none of those lists, including the evening it is named after. So the room built to be discovered sat several cards along a sideways rail where nobody would reach it. It leads the evening now, on merit, and until it has been opened once it leads every hour, with a small mark that says what it offers rather than that it is new. Opened once, the mark goes and it becomes an evening room like any other; a pin that stayed would be an advertisement.
- It is free to open, and it is the only moment that spends the day's open window on itself. Thirty seconds of the whole thing, field and all, before support is mentioned at all — the argument for a living field made by the field rather than by a dialog. Every other room composes quietly without one instead, so the window is spent on the room built for it rather than on whichever you happened to reach first.

## The field lets go

- A Presence Field set to particular apps stayed on screen for several seconds after you left one — over the home screen, over whatever you opened next — and lifted at once only if you opened SonoLune itself. A field whose whole promise is that it belongs to one app had been following you out of it.
- It was never that Android is slow to say you left. Measured on the device this was reported from, it publishes the app you stepped away from in about nineteen thousandths of a second, and names the one that replaced it inside a second. SonoLune was not asking. The watch that notices a switch is started by one message, sent as the app goes to the background, and that message was addressed to nobody in particular — which since Android 14 is not delivered to a private listener. It had been going nowhere on every modern phone.
- So the watch never began, and the reading the whole field depends on was never taken once. Four rounds of work went into making that reading sharper, and none of it could ever have shown: the function holding it was never called. The message is addressed now, and the reading is taken every third of a second, as it was always meant to be.
- Which is also why turning on "Cover more surfaces" appeared to cure it. That service sends its own note about the app in front, correctly addressed, and the code that catches it starts the watch by hand — so the accessibility permission was never fixing the field. It was restarting the loop the missing message should have started. It goes back to meaning what its name says, and nothing about timing.
- Three more of the app's own messages were addressed the same way and had gone equally unheard: the one that keeps the Quick Settings tile showing the true state, and the two the light service uses to answer the app back. None of them announced their absence. All are addressed now.
- It reads departures now as well as arrivals. An app's own moment of stepping back outranks a reading that still names it, so the field lifts as you leave rather than when whatever followed is finally identified. It does not need to know where you went — only that you are no longer there.
- It is the pausing that counts and deliberately not the stopping. An app is only fully stopped a beat after the next screen is already up, so counting that would have read ordinary movement between screens inside an app as leaving it, and blinked the field off while you were still sitting in it.
- And where a departure and an arrival are both legible, the field moves to the new app rather than lifting and forming again.
- The invitation leaves with the field. The card that offers a breath is its own window on a six-second timer, and nothing cancelled it when you stepped out of a chosen app — so the wash lifted correctly and the card stayed behind over a home screen, saying a field had settled over a screen that was clear. Only the card is dismissed: the wind-down chip still waits for you in SonoLune, because that invitation was never about the moment.
- Two overlays draw the field, and only one of them was being told the screen should be grey — the other, which sits above it, laid a fully coloured wash over a correctly greyed one. They are told the same thing now, which was a real disagreement and is gone.
- The real reason it failed is that two of them were drawing at once. Greyscale here is a wash — layers that mute colour by covering it, because nothing that draws in a window can desaturate what is behind that window — and a wash only reads as grey if it is the last thing painted. The accessibility overlay sits above the ordinary one by design, so it painted after the grey every time and diluted it back toward colour. Two overlays cannot both be last.
- So with coverage on there is one overlay now, and it is the upper one. That was refused before for a good reason: the upper overlay had never been given the field's finer state — how strong the grey should be, the quiet veil that deepens as you linger, the grain and the paper finish — so handing it the whole screen would have traded a washed-out grey for a field missing half of itself. It has all of it now.
- And the two things only the lower overlay could do — the release collapse, and the Labs software rendering — go to whichever overlay is actually drawing, instead of to a particular one that may no longer be there. That was the quiet part of the fault: a feature reaching for an overlay that had been handed away does not fail loudly, it simply stops happening.
- Paper Grain steps out of focus with its own switch. While accessibility surfacing owns the overlay the grain cannot be drawn, and the toggle above already dimmed and said so — but the slider beside it stayed at full strength, still named Paper Grain, still offering to set how present a grain that was not there should be. It dims too, and calls itself Matte, because the matte it sets is real and only the grain is resting. Nothing is switched off: taking away a control that still works would be its own small untruth.
- A departure could also be cancelled by the act of leaving. The second thing Android offers is a "last used" time, and stepping out of an app updates it — so the moment of leaving was being read as proof the app was still there, the two timestamps landing exactly equal where the test needed one to be later than the other. An app's own exit no longer vouches for its presence.
- Only ever to let go, never to appear. The reading taken while "Cover more surfaces" is on also stirs for dialogs and sheets belonging to other apps, so it may end a field early but can never place one over something you did not choose. It is trusted only while fresh, so switching that setting off changes nothing about where the field goes.
- And the page no longer explains any of this to you. It carried a note saying the field could take a few seconds to lift, blaming Android for reporting arrivals and never departures, and pointing at an accessibility permission as the remedy. Every part of that was wrong, and asking for a permission to work around our own mistake is not a note but a toll. It is gone, along with the sentence beneath the per-app switch that promised a second or two of catching up.

## When the field cannot be seen

- The Presence Field is drawn with the light overlay, and holding the light stops that overlay being drawn at all. So a field armed with the light held was not dimmed or reduced — it was not there. Nothing was on screen.
- Three surfaces said three different untrue things about it. The field's own page read "The field is holding, softly". The notification dropped the field from the sentence entirely and said only that the light was held. And beneath it a reading said "Field depth 90%" — a depth measured of nothing.
- The page now says the light is held and the field is drawn with it. The notification names both facts at once, so the field is present in the sentence rather than absent from it. And the depth reading stands down rather than measuring something that is not there.
- It waits rather than being off. Nothing is lifted, nothing is re-armed, and bringing the light back brings the field straight in — because it never left.
- The two remain two switches. Entering the field does not reach across and undo a light you deliberately held, and lifting the field does not quietly bring that light back. The app only stops describing a state it is not in.
- The button that holds the light kept its bulb throughout. It used to show the field mark while a field was settled, on the stated grounds that it lifted the field — it never did, and it stays as it is deliberately: it is the one control that works with SonoLune closed, and lifting a field would need the app running. One button, one meaning, and the line above it now says what became of the field.

## The depth it actually is

- "Field depth" in the notification was not the field's depth. It was a shaped display figure — gentle at the low end so a progress bar reads as present there, which is right for a bar and wrong for a number given a name. The Presence Field's own slider sat at half and the shade read 67%; at the deepest it read 90% and could not reach 100 whatever you did.
- It also carried a floor from the sound. With an atmosphere playing, the shallowest possible field still reported around 30% — a figure set by what you were listening to.
- And it wore the field's name in states with no field at all. Light on, no field, and the row still said "Field depth", of nothing of the kind.
- It now prints the depth, which is the same number the slider holds, so the two can be checked against each other. Where no field is settled the row says plainly "Depth". The bar keeps its gentle shaping — a bar is a feeling, and only the reading claimed to be a measurement.
- Help says what the number means, on the Presence guide and in the questions list, rather than leaving a percentage to be guessed at.
- And a running focus session no longer calls itself presence. The shade read "Presence is unfolding" while a focus hour ran — a word chosen before there was a Presence Field, which since there is one pointed at the wrong room entirely. It says "Focus is unfolding" now. The two surfaces that write that line had also quietly drifted apart, each carrying a note asking to be kept identical to the other; they say the same words again.

## Where the field lands

- Choosing apps narrows the field to those apps. The banner above kept saying it reached across your whole phone — fixed text, written before choosing apps existed — so it was wrong in exactly the state where knowing where the field lands matters most. It now reads what is set.
- The section offering it was called "Extend its reach", and "Reaching further" once it was on. Reach is the one thing it takes away. What the permission actually adds is the ability to tell your apps apart, and that is what lets the quiet arrive slowly in one place and never appear in another. It is called naming the apps that pull, which is what the top of the page has always promised.
- And the long press that returns Light to neutral now yields to the field like everything else in that room. Every slider, toggle and preset already stood aside while the field held the overlay and said why; the long press ran straight through, and the field re-asserted over it — so the light appeared to reset and instantly did not.

## Sound, corrected

- The binaural layer was not producing a binaural beat. Both ears were being given the same frequency with a fixed offset between them, and two identical tones offset like that do not beat — they only shift where the sound seems to sit. It played a faint hum slightly off-centre while the app called it entrainment.
- A beat is made by the listener out of two tones the ears never hear together, so the two ears are now given different frequencies and the difference between them is the beat. It also says what it does rather than what it might achieve, and names the one condition it genuinely has: without headphones there is nothing to combine, so nothing occurs.
- A quiet line says so when the layer is on and the sound is going to a speaker, rather than letting someone pay attention to something that physically cannot be there. It sits inside the binaural card now: it was placed between the cards, and the spacing left it far below the layer it described and almost touching the next one, so it read as a caveat about the Tone Generator. A sentence about one thing must not appear to be about another.
- Atmosphere Depth stands its heaviest effects down where they earn nothing. Its widening is a headphone effect and did nothing useful through a speaker while still consuming one of a small, device-dependent pool — and in Feather mode, on the phones that asked SonoLune to travel light, depth keeps the two effects that carry it and releases the rest. Nine layers at once could ask for twenty-eight; it now asks for ten.
- None of the depth tuning changed. The curve, the reverb and the per-layer shaping are exactly as they were.

## Said before it asks

- Allowing notifications used to lead straight to the system prompt, which names nothing. Someone allowing them so a gentle wake would ring had no way to know what else might one day arrive — and consent given for one thing must never quietly cover another.
- SonoLune now says what it would send, before it asks. Your alarms, named first because they are the reason the permission is needed at all; then the doors, offered plainly and switched on or off there and then. Nothing is turned on by the act of granting a permission.
- Three in a day, across all of them together. Each kind was counting only itself — three doors, The Hours, and the quiet invitation — so a full set could stack to seven in a day from an app whose first promise is that nothing nags. They share one ledger now.
- A door always wins, and only The Hours yields. A door arrives at an hour you chose; being quiet because a moment you asked for already came is no loss at all.
- The Night door moved to ten in the evening. At nine it sat one hour after the wake door, which exists to catch you the evening before — two invitations in consecutive hours of the same evening, which is precisely the thing they are built not to be.
- And withdrawing notification permission now closes the doors. They used to sit switched on, aimed at hours they could no longer keep — the same untruth 2.1.1 corrected for the light window, one permission to the left. It only ever closes them: restoring the permission never reopens a door behind you.
- Checked when SonoLune opens, before anything is aimed, rather than waiting for someone to visit the settings that would tell them. The light window is Night's alone and its check can wait for that page; notifications are the floor under all three, so a door left reading ON would have been wrong everywhere until you happened to look.

## Noticing a new version

- The notice that a newer SonoLune is waiting has never once appeared, across many releases. It asked Google Play and nothing else, and that answer is unreliable in a particular way: it reports "nothing new" for any build not installed from the store, so it could never appear in testing either, and it holds its reply on the device for up to a day.
- The website already carries the same record, and SonoLune was already downloading it on every start — to describe a release, never to notice one. The answer had been sitting unread. Either source is enough now.
- It also looks again when you come back to the app rather than only on a cold start, at most once a day. On a phone that keeps SonoLune in memory for weeks, the old check could go a very long time without asking anything at all.
- A release still has to be genuinely out before anything is said. A version prepared but not yet rolled out rides in that record marked as upcoming, and the app refuses to announce one — nobody is ever sent to look for a version that is not there.
- It waits for the date, too. A release carries the day it is meant to reach people, and the notice holds until that day even when everything else says go. So the record can be published once, ahead of time, with the notes already readable — and the notice simply begins on its own, with nothing to remember on the day itself.

## A pass you can see, and rooms told apart

- While a Guest Pass held, the support door hid itself — the pass counts as support, so the invitation quietly stood down for all seven days. Nothing anywhere said a pass was running, how much was left, or where to go when it settled. The first sign was a door that had already closed.
- Connect carries a quiet card for the length of it now. For most of the week it only says the Lab is open, because the pass was promised without a countdown and that is worth keeping; a day is named only at the very end, since being surprised by a closed door is worse than being told it is near.
- And the pass itself was narrowed to what it is for. Seven days used to open every supporter feature at once — Spaces, Atmosphere Depth, keeping your own moments, the card themes. That gave away the half of SonoLune that is bought once and kept forever, and let a week be enough to need nothing further. It opens the Lab now: the sessions, Passages, and the Warmth layers — the part that keeps arriving, and the part worth hearing before anyone pays monthly for it.
- Nothing was withdrawn from anyone. This is what a pass opens from here on; a purchase and a patronage are untouched, and a moment kept during a pass stays kept — those are written free and undated on purpose, so nothing a person made can close on them later.
- In Mind's moments rail, three different kinds of thing ran together as one list: moments you kept, moments delivered to you, and moments that shipped inside the app. They are not alike — an arrival is carried by patronage and can close when it settles, while a moment you kept can never be taken from you.
- A slim seam now names each group — yours, arrivals, and the ones that shipped. It stays one rail and one scroll, and each seam appears only where there is something on either side of it, so a field with no arrivals looks exactly as it did.
- Each seam names what makes its group different rather than what they all are. An earlier attempt labelled the shipped ones "moments", which put a child inside a parent of nearly the same name while arrivals are composed moments too — a hierarchy that read as nonsense. Whose it is, how it arrived, and which came with the app: those are the things that actually differ.
- The heading above them was part of the same confusion. It read "Living Moments — everyday scenes that pair SonoLune sound with full living colour", which described only the last group while sitting over all four. It says "Moments" now, and names what the rail actually holds: rooms you kept, rooms delivered to you, and rooms that came with the app.
- And a moment you kept no longer looks like one delivered to you. Both wore the same warm light and the same starlight mark; a kept moment now carries a cooler tone and a bookmark — the same bookmark on the card that saved it, so the gesture and what it makes resemble each other. Origin was always meant to be visible, and simply was not.
