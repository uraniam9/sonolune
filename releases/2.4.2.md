# SonoLune 2.4.2

**Autumn, three new sounds, and the moment that settled in silence** · 11 Sep 2026

A moment tapped from your shade could settle its light and stay silent, with the one control that says whether the room is running disagreeing with the room. Two releases tried to fix it by reading the code and both failed. This one measured instead, and the sound controller turned out never to have been wrong: it was refusing correctly, on layers that had never been loaded. A single on-off flag deep in the app is written in two places at once - held and active - and three separate paths turned it off without ever turning it back on. Letting go of a moment, starting fresh, or entering Night from a held space each left the app quietly believing your space was still put away, for as long as it stayed open. Every moment applied after that settled its colour over an empty field. A cold start cleared the flag, which is exactly why two attempts at this could never reproduce it. Tapping an Hour also lands somewhere better now: in Mind, where the moment actually is, rather than on a full-screen list of ten other moments that covered the very light it had just turned on. Three lists of moments were audited for whether they come back where you left them, and all three were wrong in different ways - one only wrote your place down when the app closed properly, which swiping it out of Recents is not. The Presence Field became something a scheduled arc has to respect rather than something it can quietly remove. And the Night depth card stopped contradicting itself. A second round added the sound faults underneath all of it: opening SonoLune while Night was running could start your last atmosphere by itself, because the one flag that answers whether audio is playing right now was being set by a call that only asks permission to play. Atmosphere Depth and Spaces were measured together for the first time and compose correctly in the engine - what was wrong was that Spaces had never been registered with the six shared systems Depth belongs to, the worst of which could leave somebody whose support had lapsed listening through glass with no control able to remove it. The offer to let SonoLune reach you now arrives while you are still in your first sitting rather than waiting for a second visit that most people never make. Every door on the first screen names its room in full. And a rail that had been asked to return to a place it could no longer reach stopped giving up and going back to the beginning. Three sounds also arrived that were not there before: Salt Air, an open coast with the sea underneath; Evening Pond, still fresh water at dusk, which this app had never had, because every water in it was either falling or moving; and Cicadas, a high unbroken haze that joins Birds and Leaves as a third micro texture. All three are free. Laid over Leaves, Cicadas turns the field reading autumn - Autumn Wind, Autumn Forest, or on its own the Turning Year - which is the first time it has named a season rather than a place. Each was cut to sit with the sounds already there rather than to any standard level, because an element plays at whatever level its file holds, and the six that shipped with SonoLune are twenty-seven decibels apart between loudest and quietest. The cicada recording was ten seconds long, which the sound engine would have repeated audibly inside a minute; it was rebuilt from overlapping slices of itself into a minute that never repeats, which is only possible because there is no call in it distinct enough to hear twice. And until you turn one of the three on for the first time, each of them says so. They also built something: Autumn, a room made of dry wind, cicada haze and falling leaves, sitting first on the Soundscapes shelf. Its three levels are measured rather than chosen - the leaves are the quietest thing in the app by a wide margin and cannot be raised, so the wind and the haze were brought down to meet them instead, which is why you can still hear a rustle under a wind. And a fault found while building it: a saved field holding all three new layers described itself as "2 layers". A list of layer names written out by hand had been written before those layers existed, and there turned out to be three such copies, each quietly a release behind. All three now read from the one list the app already has to keep complete, and a test fails if a future layer does not join it.

## Highlights

- A moment can settle with its sound playing. This is the report 2.4.1 wrote down as unfixed, and the notes for that release said plainly that two attempts had failed because both were reasoned from reading rather than measured. The third was measured. The sound controller was never the thing that was wrong - it was refusing correctly, on layers that had never been loaded, and its honest answer, "no layers are chosen", was true and led nowhere.
- What was actually wrong was one flag, written in two places at once. Whether your space is HELD and whether it is ACTIVE are the same fact stored twice, and they are meant to move together. Three paths moved only one of them: letting go of a moment, starting fresh, and entering Night from a space that was already held. After any of those the app went on believing your space was put away - so the part that loads a moment's sound applied its light, saw a space it thought was resting, and returned without loading a single layer. Every moment applied for the rest of that session settled its colour over nothing.
- And a cold start cleared it, which is why this hid for so long. The flag is not written to disk, so opening SonoLune fresh always looks correct. The fault only appears once you have held your space and let it go in the same sitting - which is an ordinary evening, and impossible to catch by reading.
- The instrument that was supposed to name the cause could not speak where the fault was. 2.4.1 added a line that names which of six guards refused to start the sound, and put it behind a debug build - while every report of this came from the released app. So for three rounds the one thing that could have answered was, by construction, never running where the question was being asked. A moment that names a sound and arrives mute now says so in plain words, in the app everybody actually has.
- A tapped Hour lands in the room, not the catalogue. It used to open Arrivals - a full-screen page listing every moment, sitting above the rest of the app - so an invitation to one named room was answered with a shelf of ten, and the page covered the very light the moment had just laid down. That is the whole reason it could look as though nothing had happened. It opens Mind now, where the moment is already drawn as the active card, and where the light is visible behind it.
- Three lists of moments were asked whether they come back where you left them, and all three were wrong. The Space rail wrote your place down only when the app closed properly - and swiping SonoLune out of Recents is not closing properly, it is Android ending the process, so the ordinary way to leave was the one way that lost it. Both rails also abandoned your place whenever a moment was active, jumping to that moment's card instead. And Arrivals had no memory at all, opening at the top every time. All three now write your place as you scroll and return to it, and a moment you have open no longer overrules where you actually were.
- The Presence Field is an authority now, like Focus and Night. Those two turn away a scheduled arc that would fight them. The field could not: an arriving Mind arc quietly removed it and applied itself over the space where it had been. Mind has had a guard against exactly that since 2.0 - and the guard was dead, because the field was taken down a moment before it was asked. An arc carrying a space or a living moment waits now, and says so, and your quiet stays where it was. The first version of this fix was dead too, for a different reason, and a phone found it: the check read the arc's own description looking for a colon that the scheduler had already written as %3A. Every Mind arc that names a moment is written that way, so the guard was reading a string no arc has ever carried.
- A Night arc no longer walks into Night over a settled field. With the field up, the notification landed in Night and the light did not arrive until you tapped something - two things writing to one overlay, one of them undoing the other. It stops at the Field room instead and tells you Night is ready once you lift. Nothing about Night changes when the field is not up.
- The Night depth card was showing two numbers and contradicting itself. One is how deep the light is; the other is how far through the night the clock has gone. Both were right, and nothing said they measured different things. But the sentence underneath was wrong: at two-thirds through the night it still read "most of the depth still to come", when the curve had already delivered four fifths of its travel. It now names what is actually left, worked out from the same curve the number above it uses, so the two explain each other instead of arguing.
- The first screen says what it is before it asks what you want, and each door names the room it opens. The three things that separate SonoLune from everything in its category - no account, no ads, nothing leaves your phone - were written at the very bottom of that screen, under all eight doors and the button past them, which is the one place nobody reads: the whole point of the doors is that you tap one and go. They lead now. And every door carries the name of the room it opens, so tapping "Calm my screen" teaches you where the Light suite is instead of just arriving there.
- And SonoLune can finally ask what you think of it. The review ask was armed by two events in Focus and shown on one Focus screen - while the rule that decides who is worth asking has always counted nights and returns to a held space. So somebody who uses this app for sleep, which is what most people use it for, became eligible and was never once invited. It is asked from anywhere now, and never inside Night, never during a session, never over a held space or under the field.
- SonoLune can offer to come back to you again. Every way it reaches a person - the evening door, the focus return, the wake reminder, moments that arrive at their own hour - ships off, and that stays true: each is a real choice, asked for as it is flipped. But the screen that offers those choices lives inside the introduction, and tapping a door on the first screen finishes the introduction and takes you straight to the room, so the offer was never shown to anybody who came in the front way. An app for daily use had no way into anyone's day, a second time, by a different road than the first. It is offered on a later visit now - once, never in the first sitting, and closing it is a complete answer.
- Opening SonoLune while Night is running no longer starts your sound by itself. The audio service keeps one flag that answers a single question - is anything actually playing right now - and a freshly opened app asks it so the play button can match a soundscape that survived being swiped away. That flag was also being set by the call that merely opens the audio gate, and Night sends that call whenever anything changes. So a cold start in Night asked "is anything playing?", was told yes over complete silence, and did the honest thing with a dishonest answer: it brought back the last field you had. Permission to play is not playing, and it no longer says it is.
- Atmosphere Depth and Spaces were measured together for the first time, and the sound engine turned out to have been right all along. The two share one filter per layer on purpose, their curves add, and Spaces works with Depth at zero. What was wrong sat everywhere else: Spaces had never been introduced to the six shared systems its sibling belongs to. It was missing from the one place that hands back what is not yours, from the thirty-second window that lets you hear a refinement before it is ever offered, from the line that tells you what your field currently is, and from the moment an arc ends and the processing should let go.
- And one of those could trap you. If your support lapsed while the field was set to Room, the glass stayed - and the only control that could take it off had just been locked, including the button marked Open. A control may be stopped from making things worse. It must never be stopped from being undone. Taking the glass away is always allowed now, whatever your support says, and a lapse hands it back the same way it has always handed back Atmosphere Depth.
- Spaces is something you can hear before you are asked to pay for it. Every other refinement in Sound opens for thirty seconds in your own field, and only afterwards mentions support - the app does not sell what has not been felt. Spaces was the one paid control that had never been given that, so tapping Window or Room met a wall where the rule says a window. It opens now, the chip lights while it plays, and it settles out gently instead of stopping.
- The offer to let SonoLune reach you arrives while you are still here. It used to wait six hours, for a good reason - the question means more to somebody opening the app again than to a stranger a moment after an advert. The reasoning still holds; the assumption behind it did not. Almost nobody was coming back to be asked. It comes after five minutes now, in whichever room you are sitting in rather than only one, and never over playing sound - somebody five minutes into an ocean at bedtime is not to be interrupted. It still never comes between your first tap and what that tap does. The offer itself says the thing that earns it: there are no push notifications in SonoLune and no server that could send one, so nothing decides on your behalf that something is worth interrupting you for. The Hours joined it, which is the feature that actually arrives at an hour a moment was written for and was the one thing missing from a card named after exactly that. And once you have switched something on, the way out reads Done rather than Not now - that button dismisses, it never undid anything, so the only word it carried described the one thing tapping it would not do.
- Every door now names its room in full. Five of the eight said Light, Sound, Night, Focus and Field, while the rest of the app - the guide, the about page, the store - has always said Light Suite, Sound Atmosphere, Night Environment, Focus Session and Presence Field. The short forms read like a single setting. "Light" is a switch; "Light Suite" is a room with things in it. Somebody arriving for a screen dimmer had no way to learn the app was larger than the one control they came for, and the Calm my screen door now says so in its own line.
- Two doors now carry the words people actually searched for, and the app says its own name again. Blue light filter is in the store listing and appeared on no door at all - so somebody who installed for exactly that read eight cards and found nothing that said it. The sleep door had the opposite problem: a second store listing aimed at sound searches opens by saying most sleep apps hand you a finished track while SonoLune hands you the layers, and the door those arrivals landed on offered a finished track. And the first screen, which somebody reaches thirty seconds after tapping an advert, carried a crescent and three promises and never once said SonoLune. A picture is not a name. It sits beside the crescent now, in space that was already empty.
- A list that was asked to return to a place it can no longer reach stops at the end instead of the beginning. Both moment rails saved where you left them, and both refused to move at all if that place was further along than the list had so far been built - waiting for it to grow. A list like that does not grow on its own; it builds as it is scrolled. So it waited for something that could only happen if it moved, gave up, and left you at the very start, which is the furthest possible place from where you were. It walks now, as far as the list goes.
- Three sounds that were not there before. Salt Air is an open coast - wind that has crossed water, with the sea underneath it. Evening Pond is still fresh water at dusk, which this app had never had: every water in it was either falling or moving. And Cicadas is a high unbroken haze with distant birds behind it, taking its place beside Birds and Leaves. All three are free, like every other element.
- Lay Cicadas over Leaves and the field knows what you have made. With Wind it reads Autumn Wind; with the forest, Autumn Forest; with rain, Autumn Rain; and on its own, the Turning Year. The field reading has always named what you built rather than listing it, and this is the first time it names a season instead of a place. The light follows the same reading: what the field is called and how the light moves are written from one description of the room, so the two can never disagree about it.
- A new sound has to be cut to sit with the ones already there, and that is a measurement rather than a taste. There was no house level to aim at: the six elements SonoLune shipped with do not agree with each other, and the loudest and the quietest of them stand twenty-seven decibels apart. So each of the three was aimed instead at the narrow band it will actually be heard in, and checked there against whatever already lives in it.
- The cicada recording was ten seconds long, and the engine rejoins a loop every eight and a half. Ten seconds is a sound you would hear repeating inside a minute. The usual answer - laying a file over a slowed copy of itself - would have made it three times as busy, which is not the calm thing that was recorded. It was cut into overlapping slices instead, reordered, and crossfaded back into a minute that never repeats. That only works on a recording that never changes, and this one does not: across the whole of it the level moves by barely a decibel, and there is no call in it distinct enough to hear twice. The rebuilt minute measures the same in every band as the ten seconds it came from.
- Autumn is a room, and it leads the shelf. It is the dry end of the year heard from indoors, it is free, and it is built entirely from sounds that did not exist in SonoLune a week ago. It leads for that reason: a sound nobody opens is a sound nobody has.
- You can still hear the leaves underneath the wind, and that took measuring rather than taste. A room built around a rustle cannot be balanced by turning the rustle up, because leaves is by a wide margin the quietest thing SonoLune has and an element stops at full. It had to be balanced the other way about, by lowering everything else onto it - which is the opposite of how a mix is usually reached, and it is the difference between standing in autumn and listening to wind with something going on behind it.
- A room you had just chosen could not be carried into a focus session. The list that offers a field when a focus session starts was written out by hand, one entry per room, with a matching branch each - so Autumn arrived on the Sound shelf and simply was not there. That list is now built from a table, the eighteen branches have become one, and a test fails if a room is ever added without joining it. That is the fourth hand-written copy of the same set found in this release, and the fourth to have quietly fallen behind.
- A button on the Mind screen was losing the end of its own name. Lune Guard read as Lune Gu on a Galaxy F13 with nothing turned up, which is the part worth saying: this app has a whole suite of checks for the largest system font, and not one of them could see this, because it was wrong at the ordinary one. The card decided to put its buttons side by side whenever it had 286 pixels, and 286 was a guess about how much room the words need. It was about eight pixels short. It measures the words now, so the answer moves with the font instead of being fixed in advance.
- Two gated taps answered the wrong thing, and one of them answered nothing at all. The row that offers to let the light move with your sound carries a control that names support — and its tap did nothing whatsoever once the day's free look had been spent, which is most of the time, since the look is once a day. It asks now, and when the light has not yet been opened past SonoLune it says that instead, because support is not what is missing there. And tapping Open on the Spaces card raised the offer to pay. Open is the state you are already in: nothing was reached for, so nothing should have been asked for. It says what Spaces would add and leaves it there.
- That row also stopped underselling itself. It used to say the light could move with this, which describes a capability and sells nothing. It names the thing now — the Light Suite moving with the Sound Atmosphere — and says why it is worth having: every sound moves it differently. Rain is fine and constant, wind leans and passes and reaches furthest of the six, and a fire is the only thing in the app that genuinely flickers.
- The Hours could not be switched on at all, and the reason it gave was not the real one. It refuses when it has no moment it could bring you - which is right - but it could not tell "there is nothing here for you" apart from "nothing has been downloaded yet". On a phone that had never reached the feed, and on one where every arrival is already open, it said the same thing: that opening the other arrivals would give it hours. That was simply untrue. An empty library now means ask again later. The switch stays where you put it, nothing is scheduled while there is nothing to schedule, and the day a moment does arrive it starts working by itself. If the feed then turns out to hold nothing you can use, it closes honestly, with the real reason.
- The very first screen ran off the side of itself at the largest system font. The name beside the crescent - SonoLune, and Soundsoft Lab under it - was the one thing on that screen still sized to whatever width it wanted, so at the biggest font setting it pushed almost a hundred pixels past the edge of the phone. The door tiles below it carry a comment saying they were fixed for exactly this, once. The header was simply never looked at. Nothing moves at ordinary text sizes; it only wraps now when it cannot fit.
- And the card that offers Atmosphere Depth now speaks about the room you are in. It used to describe the control - more warmth, space and presence - which is the right sentence for somebody shopping and the wrong one for somebody already sitting in a field they like. In Autumn it now says that depth gives the wind a floor to move across and lets the leaves fall somewhere with walls. Nothing is gated differently and nothing is being sold: tapping it opens thirty seconds of the real thing in your own field, once a day, before support is mentioned at all. This is only a better reason to tap.
- A saved field of three new layers said it held two. The line under a saved soundscape counts what is in it by walking a list of layer names - and that list had been written out by hand, before Salt Air, Evening Pond and Cicadas existed. It was not alone: three separate copies of the layer set were found, each a release behind, including the one that lifts a first-run field to an audible level. They all read from the same list now, the one the app already has to keep complete in order to decide what to drop when the field is full, and a test fails if a new layer is ever added without joining it.
- A sound you have not tried yet says so. Each of the three carries a small mark until the first time you turn it on, and then it is gone for good. A tap that could not be honoured does not count - if your field is already full you were shown a message and heard nothing, and the mark stays where it is. And because the micro textures sit behind a folded card, that card carries the mark for Cicadas until you open it.
- And you can ask for SonoLune in your language. A card in Connect, beside suggesting a feature and reporting a disturbance. It fills itself in with the language your phone is set to, written in that language, so sending it is one tap. It is a question, not a promise - SonoLune is written in English and this is how it finds out what is wanted.

## The moment that settled in silence

- A composed moment applied from your shade, from Mind, or from Arrivals could set its light and load no sound at all, leaving the transport reading pause over a room that was never running.
- The cause was one flag with two names. Holding your space sets it inactive and suspended together; returning sets both back. Three paths cleared only the second: letting go of a moment, starting fresh, and entering Night from a held space.
- With it stuck, the part of the app that settles an atmosphere applies the light and returns before touching the sound - correct behaviour for a space that is genuinely resting, and wrong for one that only looked that way.
- It also meant the app was quietly stopping sound again the moment anything changed, because a resting space is not supposed to be playing.
- The flag is never written to disk, so every cold start looked correct. Two earlier attempts at this failed for that reason.
- The three paths now write both halves. A source check refuses any future edit that clears one and forgets the other.
- And a moment can no longer arrive mute in silence: if the layers do not reach the field, the app loads them itself, and if they still cannot arrive it says so in plain words rather than only in a debug build.

## Where an Hour takes you

- Tapping an Hour opened the Arrivals page: a full-screen list of every moment, drawn above the rest of the app.
- And that page is opaque, so it covered the very light the moment had just laid down. That is why a moment could settle correctly and still read as nothing having happened: the thing you were meant to notice was underneath the thing announcing it.
- It opens Mind now. The moment is already drawn there as the active card, with its status, and the light is visible behind it.
- The one thing lost is the moment's longer writing: the rail card shows its first line where the Arrivals card showed them all.
- A tap made while SonoLune is already open is honoured too. Going to a room you are already standing in does not rebuild it, so the moment would have been dropped in silence.
- The guards a moment passes - a held space, Night, a running focus session, the sessions gate, the light window - are one piece of code shared by the rail and the notification, rather than a second, weaker copy.

## Three lists that forgot where you were

- The Space rail kept your place in memory and only wrote it down when the app closed properly. Swiping SonoLune out of Recents is Android ending the process, so nothing was written - the ordinary way to leave was the one way that lost your place.
- Both rails in Mind also gave your place up whenever a moment was active, jumping to that moment's card instead. That rule was written to fix a different fault, which had already been fixed on its own.
- Your place is not a guess: it is the last thing you did with that rail. And settling a moment already writes its card's position, so keeping your place lands on that card anyway whenever you have not moved since.
- Arrivals had no memory at all and opened at the top every time. It now remembers like the other two.
- All three write as you scroll, and all three retry while a list is still growing - a list measured before it has finished laying out reports itself as far shorter than it is, and a jump into that lands at the top.
- Bringing an active moment into view is still there, as the fallback for a list nobody has scrolled yet.

## The field an arc has to respect

- Focus and Night each turn away a scheduled arc that would fight them. The Presence Field did not - an arriving arc removed it and applied itself over the space where it had been.
- Mind has guarded against exactly this since 2.0. The guard was dead on the arrival path: the field was taken down a few hundred lines before Mind was ever asked.
- The first fix for it was dead as well, and only a real phone could show that. An arc describes itself in a short encoded string, and the scheduler writes a colon as %3A - so a check looking for "space:" found nothing on every arc that has ever existed. Night had been asking the same question the same way since 2.3, so it was misreading these too, and turning away the focus starts it is supposed to let through.
- An arc carrying a space or a living moment now waits, lands in the Field room, and says the field has the screen.
- The Night arc is turned away by the same rule, so an evening cannot start on top of a field somebody is already sitting in. Landing outside Night is the fix rather than a repair to the overlay, because two writers arguing over one surface is not a thing to arbitrate - it is a thing to stop happening.
- Arcs that only open a page are unchanged: they still lift the field, because you tapped a cue drawn over another app in order to go and look at something.
- A Mind arc carrying a focus start is unchanged too. It opens Focus and touches nothing the field is holding.

## A card that argued with itself

- Night Intensity showed a depth and a journey - how deep the light is, and how far through the night the clock has gone. Both were right; nothing said they measured different things.
- The line underneath was wrong. Two-thirds through the night it still read "most of the depth still to come", when four fifths of the deepening had already happened.
- It names what is actually left now, worked out from the same curve the number above it uses, so the two agree: this much now, this much to come, this much at its deepest.
- The beginning and the end of the night stay described in words rather than numbers, which is the rule that card already had.
- And a second Night control that once misreported itself has had its remains cleared out. A check that asked whether the light still matched the chosen preset was removed from use two releases ago, because it could not work: it rebuilt the expected light from the same values the current light was built from, so the two always agreed and a preset you had cleared kept coming back. The dead code was kept as the record of why. The record is kept and the code has gone - which leaves the app with no compiler warnings at all for the first time.

## Night was starting your sound by itself

- Reported from a phone: with Night running, opening SonoLune fresh could start the last atmosphere you had used, unasked.
- That question exists for one good reason: an atmosphere survives being swiped out of Recents, so a reopened app has to ask the service what is true rather than assume silence.
- That flag was also being set by the call that only opens the audio gate. Night sends that call every time anything about it changes, and with the sacred hum off - which is how it ships - nothing was playing at all.
- So a cold start in Night asked whether anything was playing, was told yes over silence, and brought the field back. Permission to play is not playing. Only the transport says so now.
- The same correction quietly fixes the wind-down chime and the focus threshold bell, both of which claimed sustained playback for a sound under a second long.

## Atmosphere Depth and Spaces, measured together

- The two share one filter per layer by design, their curves add, and Spaces works with Depth at zero. That part was right and stays exactly as it was.
- What was wrong is that Spaces had never been registered with the systems Depth belongs to. Six of them, each found by asking the same question in a different place.
- Your field summary names the room you are in - Window or Room - beside the depth, the way the line describing a saved room already did.
- When a session arc ends and fades, the glass fades with it. The processing genuinely lets go now instead of staying attached over silence.
- With Depth at zero, Spaces no longer reserves audio effects it cannot use. On a phone with few to spare those reservations could crowd out the one filter Spaces itself needs, and only on the phones nobody can test.
- The Presence Field responds to Spaces as well as Depth. Both move the field away from you; only one of them was being listened to.
- The depth card stopped using the name of the control below it. Its middle readout said "Space" while Spaces sat a few cards down saying "Room" - two cards disagreeing about one word. It reads Surround, which is what that part of depth actually is.

## Glass you can always take off

- If your support ended while the field was set to Room, the muffle stayed and nothing could remove it. The control that put it there had been locked, and the button marked Open led to the support page rather than to Open.
- The rule this breaks is one SonoLune has broken before and written down: a gate may refuse to make something stronger, and must never refuse to weaken it. Every step toward Open is free.
- And a lapse hands it back on its own, the way it has always handed back Atmosphere Depth, Neuro layers and stereo width. Spaces simply was not on that list.
- Spaces also gets the thirty-second window every other refinement has. Tapping Window or Room plays it in your own field first, with the chip lit while it does, and settles out gently before support is mentioned at all.
- Nothing is left behind when that window closes: the glass thins out with everything else that was borrowed.

## The offer arrives while you are still here

- The offer to let SonoLune reach you waited six hours, so it could never interrupt a first sitting. That was written for somebody who comes back, and most people were not coming back to be asked.
- It comes after five minutes now - long enough that the light has been warm, or the field has been up, and short enough to reach somebody who is still here.
- It appears in whichever room you are sitting in. It used to appear only in Mind, and no first-run door opens Mind, so the people it was written for could not reach it.
- It never rises over playing sound. Somebody five minutes into an ocean at bedtime is not somebody to hand a settings sheet.
- It now says why it is different from every other app asking this question: there are no push notifications in SonoLune and no server that could send one, and nothing decides on your behalf that something is worth interrupting you for. You choose the hour, or there is no hour.
- The Hours is offered alongside Night and the gentle wake. It is the one that literally arrives at the hour a moment was written for, and it was missing from a card named after that. Focus is still left to its own room, where somebody who has actually held a session will meet it.
- The card is called Invitations at an hour you choose. It read A door at your hour while the switches beneath it said invitation and reminder - three words for one idea, on the one card whose whole job is to be acted on.
- And the way out reads Done once anything is on. It said Not now with both switches already flipped, which is worse than untidy: that button dismisses and never undid anything, so the only word it carried named the one thing tapping it would not do.
- And it still never comes between your first tap and what that tap does. That was always the stronger half of the rule and it is untouched.

## Every door names its whole room

- Three names for one thing: the tag on the card, the room it opens, and what the guide and the store call it. Only two of them ever met.
- The short forms read as a single setting. "Light" is a switch you turn on; "Light Suite" is a room with things inside it.
- That matters most to whoever installed SonoLune for a screen dimmer and never learned it was anything else.
- The Arrivals door now says The Hours, which is what that feature is called everywhere and what its own line already describes.
- Calm my screen reads: less blue, warmer, dimmer than your phone allows - or like paper. Less blue leads because that is the term in the store listing, and it was on no card here.
- Fall asleep reads: a slow ocean, yours to layer - and the light comes down with it. There is a second store listing aimed at sound searches, and it opens by saying most sleep apps hand you a finished track while SonoLune hands you the layers. The door those arrivals landed on then offered a finished track.
- And the app says its own name again. The first screen carried a crescent, three promises and a question, and the word SonoLune appeared on it nowhere - a picture is not a name, and this is the screen somebody reaches thirty seconds after tapping an advert. It sits beside the crescent now, with Soundsoft Lab under it, in space that was already empty: no reading beat, no height, and the heading is still the question.

## A place a list could no longer reach

- Both moment rails remember where you left them. If that place was further along than the list had so far been built, they refused to move at all and waited for it to grow.
- A list like that does not grow on its own. It builds as it is scrolled. So it was waiting for something that could only happen if it moved.
- After a dozen attempts it gave up and left you at the very beginning - the furthest possible place from where you actually were, and the exact complaint this was all written to fix.
- It walks now: as far as the list goes, which builds more of it, and on again until it arrives.
- If the rail genuinely ends short - a moment deleted, a room that got smaller between two openings - you land at its end. That is near where you were. The start is not.

## SonoLune in your language

- A card in Connect, beside suggesting a feature and reporting a disturbance, asks which language you would rather read SonoLune in.
- It reads your phone's own language and writes it in for you, in that language, so sending it is one tap.
- It is a question and not a promise. SonoLune is written in English, and this is how it finds out whether anyone wants otherwise.
- Nothing is sent on its own: it opens a draft in your mail app, carrying the languages your phone is set to, and you decide.

## Autumn

- A room of dry wind, cicada haze and falling leaves, first on the Soundscapes shelf.
- Free, and not a locked arrangement: it uses one of your three element slots and both texture slots, so there is room to put something of your own on top of it.
- The three levels are measured rather than chosen. Leaves is the quietest thing in the app and cannot be raised past full, so the wind and the haze were brought down to meet it - wind about seven decibels over the haze, haze about seven over the leaves.
- That ordering IS the room. The wind is what you notice, the haze sits behind it, and the leaves are the detail you hear in the gaps, which is what autumn sounds like from indoors.
- The field reading already called cicadas-and-leaves autumn, so the room and the name it produces agree by construction. With the light following, the field barely sways and mostly shimmers - which is what those three sounds actually do.

## A room you could not take into Focus

- The field picker that opens when a focus session starts was a hand-written list, one row per room and one branch per row. Autumn shipped and was not in it, so a room you had just chosen could not be carried in.
- It is built from a table now and the branches have collapsed into one. Adding a room without adding it here is a test failure rather than something somebody notices on a phone.
- Four such copies have now been found in this one release, in four different files, none of which knew about the others. The pattern is always the same: a set that already exists somewhere, written out again by hand because it was easier at the time, and kept in step afterwards by nothing but memory.

## A button that lost the end of its name

- Lune Guard read as Lune Gu in the Lune Circle card, on a Galaxy F13 at default font settings.
- The card put its buttons side by side whenever it had 286 pixels to work with. At exactly that width each button ends up 138 wide, its own padding takes 40, the icon and its gap take 26, and the label is left with 72 for a word that wants about 80.
- So the threshold was roughly eight pixels short of the thing it existed to protect, on every screen that landed between 286 and about 300. Wider phones were fine, which is why it went unseen.
- It asks the words how much room they need now, using the same measurement the text itself lays out with. That answer moves when the system font does, which no fixed number can.
- Worth noting: the app checks every screen at the largest font, and none of those checks could catch this. It was wrong at the ordinary one.

## Two taps that answered wrongly

- The light-follows row carries a small control that names support. Once the day's free look had been spent — which is most of the day, since it is once a day — tapping it did nothing at all.
- It asks now. And when the light has not been opened past SonoLune yet, it says that instead: support is not the missing piece there, and asking for it would be the wrong answer to the right tap.
- Tapping Open on the Spaces card raised the offer to pay. Open is the state you are already in, so nothing was reached for and nothing should have been asked for.
- It still explains what Spaces would add, because a tap that changes nothing deserves a sentence. It simply no longer asks you to buy the absence of a thing — the same principle that already makes taking the glass off always allowed.

## What the light does with your sound

- The offer used to read "the light can move with this", which names a capability and says nothing about why you would want it.
- It names both rooms now — the Light Suite moving with the Sound Atmosphere — and says what makes it worth having: the movement is different for every sound.
- Rain is fine and constant with almost no lean. Wind is the opposite: it leans, it passes, and it reaches further across the screen than any of the other five. A fire is the only thing in the app that genuinely flickers.
- The line above it still says what is happening right now, so the card describes the idea and this moment at the same time.

## The Hours, and a reason that was not true

- The Hours refuses to switch on when it has no moment it could bring you. That rule is right: a switch should not promise what it cannot do.
- But it could not tell an empty library from an exhausted one. With nothing downloaded yet it gave the same answer as it would to somebody whose arrivals were all closed — that opening the other arrivals would give it hours it can reach. On a phone where every arrival is already open, that was simply untrue.
- An empty library now means ask again later. The switch stays where you put it, and nothing is scheduled while there is nothing to schedule.
- The day a moment does arrive, it starts working on its own. And if the feed turns out to hold nothing you can use, it closes itself then — with the real reason.

## The first screen at the largest font

- At the maximum system font the name beside the crescent pushed about a hundred pixels past the right edge of the screen.
- It was the last thing on that screen still taking whatever width it wanted. "Soundsoft Lab" alone, at that size, measures wider than the space the row is given on a narrow phone.
- The door tiles directly below carry a note saying they were fixed for this exact fault once already. The header above them was never checked.
- Nothing moves at ordinary text sizes. The name wraps only when it genuinely cannot fit, and the crescent keeps its place beside it.

## The depth card talks about your room

- Atmosphere Depth used to describe itself - warmth, space, presence. That is a sentence for somebody shopping, not for somebody already sitting in a field they like.
- It now names what depth would do to the room actually playing. In Autumn: a floor for the wind to move across, and walls for the leaves to fall in.
- Keyed on the layers rather than the room, so a field you built by hand gets the same sentence as the shipped one made of the same things.
- Nothing changed about what is gated. Tapping it still opens thirty seconds of the real thing in your own field, once a day, before support is mentioned - and somebody who has already supported keeps the plain description, because they are not being invited anywhere.

## A saved field that said it held two

- Reported from a phone: a soundscape saved with all three new layers described itself as "2 layers".
- The line under a saved room counts what is in it by walking a list of layer names, and that list had been written out by hand before those layers existed. Nothing was broken; the copy had simply not been told.
- It was not alone. Three separate hand-written copies of the layer set were found, each a release behind - the saved-room label, the shelf ordering, and the pass that lifts a first-run field to an audible level, which measured its loudness across the OTHER layers only.
- All three now read from the list the app already has to keep complete, because it is the list that decides what to drop when the field is full. A test fails if a future layer does not join it.

## Three new sounds

- Salt Air, an element. Open coastal wind with the sea underneath - not Wind and Ocean played together, which is a different thing. It fills the band between them that neither one does.
- Evening Pond, an element. Still fresh water at dusk with a chorus over it. Every water this app had was either falling or moving; this one holds still.
- Cicadas, a micro texture. A high unbroken haze with distant birds behind it, beside Birds and Leaves.
- All three are free, and they count against the same limits as everything else: the two elements against the three you can hold at once, Cicadas against the two textures.
- They join everything an element joins - the field reading, the light that follows your sound, a saved soundscape, and a session that comes back after a cold start.

## A field that can name a season

- Cicadas and Leaves together read as autumn. With Wind it is Autumn Wind, with the forest Autumn Forest, with rain Autumn Rain, and with neither, the Turning Year.
- Evening Pond with Cicadas is a Long Evening. With rain it is Rain on Water; beside a fire, Lantern Water. Salt Air with the ocean is an Open Coast, and beside a fire, a Driftwood Fire.
- On their own they name themselves: Salt Air, Evening Pond, and for Cicadas, a Cicada Haze.
- The light follows them the way it follows everything else, and each moves differently. Salt Air gusts and eases. The pond is nearly still and carries all its movement as shimmer. Cicadas barely moves at all - which is exactly what the recording does.

## What it takes to add a sound

- A sound that arrives from the Lab carries its own volume in the feed, so it can be trimmed after the fact without touching the file. An element cannot: it plays at the level its file holds, and that is the end of it. The mastering IS the mix.
- Salt Air was cut until the band it fills matches Rain in that same band to within a tenth of a decibel. Evening Pond sits level with Rain where it is strongest. Cicadas stands well over Wind where it identifies itself, and under both new elements everywhere else.
- The three micro textures separate cleanly: Birds live in one band, Cicadas in the next, Leaves in the one above. Playing all three at once does not muddy any of them.
- The cicada recording arrived ten seconds long - shorter than the sound engine wants, and audibly repeating inside a minute. It was rebuilt from overlapping slices of itself, reordered, into a minute that never repeats.
- None of this has been listened to yet, and that is worth saying plainly. Every claim above is a measurement. The listening comes next.

## Something you have not heard yet

- Each of the three new sounds carries a small mark until the first time you turn it on, and the Autumn room on the shelf carries one too.
- The mark is spent by hearing the sound, not by touching the tile. If your field is already full, the tap is refused with a message and nothing plays - so the mark stays where it is.
- Micro Textures is a folded card until you open it, so a mark on the Cicadas tile would be a mark nobody sees. The card carries it instead, and hands it over when you open it.
- Once it is gone it stays gone, including after a restart.
