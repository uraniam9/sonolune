# SonoLune 2.2.1

**The layers that were named are here, and the field counts them** · 16 Aug 2026

Nine new layers arrive in Sound Atmosphere — rare passing sounds, musical warmth, and the sense of people at a distance — and the app now agrees that a field made of them is a field. The Presence Field gets the other half of this release: three controls that were switched on and covered up, a field that changed its mind every time you came back to an app, and a Visiting Hours arc that could take your chosen apps with it when it left.

## Highlights

- The three arriving layers are real. Passages carries a far bell, a night owl and a distant train — rare sounds that pass through the field, minutes apart, so the room stops looping and starts happening. Tonal Warmth carries a slow pad, soft chimes and a singing bowl, all in one key so they rest together. Human Warmth carries a cafe murmur, the room next door and a far courtyard: presence, never words.
- Each pack keeps one layer open to everyone, always — a far bell, a warm pad, a cafe murmur — and nothing here is needed to use anything above it. The pack names all three either way, marks which are held, and answers when you tap one, so what is there is visible rather than hidden behind a price.
- A field made only of these now counts as a field. The play button stayed dark, the notification said the space was open, and the light would not follow — because the app looked only for its own built-in elements and found none. One layer of a pack is a room, and everything that asks now agrees.
- They stay where you leave them. A pack survived neither closing the app nor being saved into one of your own soundscapes; both now come back holding what they held, at the distance you set.
- Each pack can sit softer, as made, or nearer. One control for the whole pack rather than one per layer — the balance inside a pack is composed, so it moves as one thing rather than coming apart. What you choose is kept, and travels into a soundscape you save.
- The light lets go when the sound does. With the light following your atmosphere, releasing the field — or simply turning off the last layer — used to leave it running, lit and moving, following something that was no longer there. It waits a few seconds before deciding, because an empty field is also what changing your mind mid-swap looks like, and a light that goes out while you are still choosing is worse than one that lingers a moment.
- A saved soundscape comes back whole. Opening one said the light was following and then did nothing until the switch was turned off and on by hand, and the line naming the room counted neither the arriving packs it held nor whether it had been saved behind glass — so a room saved through a window came back described as though it were in the open.
- They arrive rather than start. A pad appearing in a third of a second lands as an event, which is the one thing this rail should never be; a pack now grows into the field over a couple of seconds.
- A way to look for what is new. The app only ever checked for arriving content at a cold start, so a pack could be live for a week and reach nobody — there was nothing to tell you to go and look. Sound now checks quietly when you open it, and there is a plain "Check for new" beside the arriving region that answers even when the answer is nothing yet.
- Soundsoft Lab Sessions is named for what it is: the deliberate end of the Lab — isochronic pulses, binaural depth, spoken and sub-audible layers, built into sessions with a shape. Audio only, never flashing light, and it asks once before it plays anything.
- The notification names what is playing again. A field made only of arriving layers had no built-in element to name, so it reached the notification as nothing at all — it now reads the pack, and counts the rest rather than listing them, so it still fits when three are resting over the field.
- With Night running, the notification had stopped mentioning sound at all. Play, pause, or set a pack down and nothing changed — Night and the field are described together again.
- The play button no longer changes its mind. At launch it drew a guess and corrected itself a moment later in front of you; it now waits the half-second it takes to know, and looks undecided rather than decided and wrong.
- Reaching for something the monthly carries now opens onto the monthly. Every door led to the one-time tiers instead — including the one built specifically to carry a lapsed supporter to the patronage, which delivered them to the tier they already owned.
- And the arriving region stops claiming the app is finished. It said everything else in SonoLune was done and only this part was still being made, which was never true and was never going to become true.
- Visiting Hours is new, and it is the opposite of a blocker. You agree in advance that an app is open for a stretch — twenty minutes of the one that usually takes an hour — and at the chosen time a notification arrives whose tap opens THAT app, not SonoLune. The field settles over it when the time is up. Nothing closes and nothing is taken away; the agreement was yours, made calmly, and the app simply keeps it on your behalf.
- A visit changes nothing you have set. It borrows the field's settings for as long as it runs and puts every one of them back at the end — the length, the quiet, the apps, the grace. If SonoLune is killed mid-visit, the settings on disk were never touched, so the next launch reads yours and the visit simply never happened.
- And a visit you never take lets itself go. Tap nothing, put the phone down, and it expires rather than waiting to surprise you days later with a field you no longer remember agreeing to. It also refuses to start while Focus or Night has the screen, and says which one rather than failing quietly.
- Visiting Hours could take your chosen apps with it. A visit borrows your app list for as long as it runs and hands it back at the end — but adding or removing an app from the Presence room DURING a visit saved the borrowed list over your own. Everything looked correct until the next cold start, at which point the apps you had chosen were simply gone, hours after the tap that removed them. Your own list is what gets saved now, always.
- A visit over several apps is one stretch, not one each. Switching between two apps the same visit opened handed back a full free-use window every time, so a twenty-minute visit could be run all evening by alternating between them — which is the exact loophole a visit exists to close. The clock, the depth and the release now follow the visit from app to app.
- The three ways the quiet can deepen were invisible if you turned them on. At its deepest the wash is almost opaque, and a blur has nothing left to blur behind it while a damped badge has nothing left to damp — so switching on all three changed almost nothing you could see. With any of them on, the wash now holds back far enough to leave them something to act on.
- Tapping a visit no longer opens onto a black rectangle. The half-second before your app appears says what is opening, for how long, and that the quiet arrives at the end — rather than looking like the app crashed on the way.
- A daily arc is finally daily. Android alarms are single-use: one goes off, and something has to set the next. Only opening SonoLune did that — so an arc fired, and if a whole day passed without the app being opened, it never fired again. It hid behind the arcs people TAP, because tapping opens the app and sets everything again, and it broke exactly the arcs meant to be declinable. A visit you let pass ended its own series. Every arc now sets its own next occurrence the moment it fires.
- And arcs survive a restart. Android drops every pending alarm when a phone reboots, and SonoLune restored only the screen tint — so after a restart nothing fired until the app was opened, while every arc still sat in the list, still switched on, still showing its time. SonoLune now writes down what it scheduled and puts it all back on boot.
- The Presence room says when a visit has borrowed your apps. A visit sets your chosen apps aside and shows its own for as long as it runs; nothing said so, and a room reading "the field appears only in the 1 app below" is indistinguishable from a list that has been emptied. It now says what is held and that it is coming back — counted rather than named, because printing the names would invite removing an app that is not currently in the set.

## Nine layers, and how they were made

- Passages are mostly nothing. Six minutes of quiet air with three to five events in it, spaced unevenly on purpose: the file loops, and evenly spaced events become a metronome on the second pass. A bell that arrives every ninety seconds is weather; a bell that arrives on the beat is a clock.
- The events sit about thirty decibels above their own air rather than as loud as they can be. Set by ear against the bed, not by the usual loudness target — a six-minute file that is mostly silence measures as silence, and normalising it drives the few events up until a far bell arrives as a startle.
- The far bell and the singing bowl are synthesised rather than recorded, because nothing recorded was right. A bell is a specific set of inharmonic partials — hum, prime, tierce, quint, nominal — and the tierce a minor third above the prime is why bells sound minor however they are struck. A bowl is the same idea with different ratios, each mode really a close pair that beats against itself, which is the warble a bowl has and a synth pad does not.
- The bowl gets its strikes with a mallet's contact, a room to ring in, and a thread of real metallic texture underneath — pure tones alone read as synthetic, and no amount of arithmetic fixes that.
- Human Warmth is several different recordings joined end to end rather than one loop. Voices give a repeat away far faster than any texture does, so a loop of one take announces itself within minutes however long it is.
- And they are filtered so they cannot be understood. Intelligibility lives in a narrow band, and blurred speech that is nearly words is worse than words — the ear leans in to resolve it, which is the opposite of what this is for. That is a property of the files now, not something a control might do later.

## Making nine new layers sit under what is already there

- A new layer that merely matches the rain is already too loud for the leaves. Every one of these was measured band by band against every built-in element, rather than against the loudest one and hoped for.
- Three of them were sitting on top of the weather they were supposed to sit beneath — the warm pad, the chimes and the bowl each peaked louder than rain in rain's own strongest band. They are trimmed to sit under it.
- The Human layers were competing with the body of every element at once, so they were moved out of the bottom end entirely. Voices do not need the low frequencies a rain bed lives in, and giving each thing its own lane is the difference between a mix and a mud.
- The fire's crackle was being covered too, one octave above where the filtering stopped. A gentler filter than intended had let the murmur sit right on top of it.
- Two packs can rest over the field at once rather than three. Each is another sound running continuously; three of them on top of three elements stops adding to the field and starts covering it.

## What had never been exercised

- Nothing here had ever run with a real pack in it, and it showed. A pack could be in the field while the app believed nothing was playing — one missing idea, read in about forty places, so the play button, the notification, the light sync and the environment all agreed on the same wrong answer.
- Choosing a pack did not even reach the mixer on an empty field. The push to the audio engine was conditional on something already playing, so a pack tapped into silence stayed silent.
- A field of only packs was never saved. The check for "is there anything worth resuming" looked for built-in elements alone, so the snapshot was built and then thrown away — and resuming a saved session actively cleared the packs it had just been asked to restore.
- Cached offline, the packs came back wrong in two ways: they lost their category, so the three rails vanished and everything reappeared in the Sessions card; and they lost their publish date, which decides who a dated pack opens for. A supporter reading from the cache was handed the full pack they are gated out of online.
- The arrival fade was being cut short by the very next line of code, which set the volume outright while the fade was still starting. Not a rare race — the field is pushed again on every rebuild, so it happened nearly every time.
- Downloaded sound is kept in a folder named for its version, so when a layer is remade the new one lands beside the old rather than on top of it — and the old was never removed. Every improvement to a sound would have left its previous copy behind for good, on storage nobody could see or clear. A pack now takes its earlier versions with it as it arrives.
- And the app stopped re-proving every downloaded byte over and over. Checking a sound has not been damaged means reading all of it, which is the right thing to do before playing it and the wrong thing to do every time a screen opens. It happens where it protects you — before a download is kept, and before a layer is played — and not merely to decide whether to draw a tick.
- The distance control did nothing until something else happened to redraw the screen. The Sound suite only rebuilds when a value it is watching changes, and this was not one of them — so a choice made a minute earlier appeared to take effect when the play button was next pressed. It was also being dropped entirely if the field was paused, rather than waiting to be heard.
- The same oversight held the play button dim. Waiting until the app knows whether sound survived is the right thing to do, but the answer arriving was not something the screen was watching — so on a launch that restored arriving layers the button stayed faded and untappable for the whole session rather than for half a second.
- And a pack asked for before the list of packs had loaded was quietly forgotten. Opening a saved soundscape moments after launch asked for something the app could not yet name, and it was dropped rather than held — so the room came back missing exactly the layers that make it that room. It waits for the list now.

## Doors, and where they open

- Reaching for a Lab session opened the support page on the one-time tiers, whatever had been reached for. The monthly was on the page, further down, and nothing took you to it.
- That included the door built to carry a lapsed supporter to the patronage — the one the code describes at length as the fix for a dead end. It routed correctly and then landed them on the tier they had already bought.
- Both land on Patron of the Lab now, and the button that gets you there stopped promising the tier: it said "open today's sessions" while opening the subscription.
- Spaces asks before it sends. It jumped straight to the support page with no word about what had been reached for — the only gated surface in the suite that did not first say what it was.
- Soundsoft Lab Sessions asks once before anything from it plays, and says what it is doing, what it will never do, when not to use it, and that it can be undone. Withdrawing takes the sound out of the field rather than off the menu, or it would be consent in name only.

## The quiet, where you could actually see it

- Three of the Presence Field's controls were switched on and did almost nothing. Not a conflict between them — they were being covered. The wash reaches near-opacity at full depth, and two of the three work by changing what is UNDERNEATH it: blurring a page that is no longer legible, and pulling the colour out of badges that are no longer visible. With any of them on, the wash is now held below the point where they stop having anything to act on.
- They were also being skipped entirely on the instant setting, where the field jumped to its endpoint rather than travelling there — and travelling there is where all three do their work.
- So Instant fades now. It set the quiet to full on its first beat, which is the one arrival this suite should never make: a wash that appears between two frames is a jolt, whatever it is made of. It still begins the moment you arrive — there is no free window, that is what Instant means, and the quiet is on screen from the first frame — but it settles over a few seconds instead of being there before your eye has moved. The three quiet styles get to show what they are on the way, and the guide finally describes what happens rather than what was intended.
- The field stopped changing its mind when you come back. Leaving a chosen app for a moment and returning showed sometimes grain, sometimes a full wash, sometimes only the colour. The clock keeps running while you are briefly away, which is correct and deliberate, and nothing was telling the screen — so a return could sit on whatever was there before you left.
- An app you had told not to deepen was painted the moment it opened and then never moved again. A field that appears once and stays still is the least explicable thing the suite can do; it now does what was asked, which is nothing.
- Tapping a set no longer discards an app you tuned by hand. Take "the scrolling ones", shape one of them yourself, tap the same set again, and your work was quietly replaced. A set is a claim about a category; an app you opened and tuned is a claim about that app, and the more specific one wins. Putting an app back on the shared settings gives up that claim, so a set may shape it again.

## Visiting Hours, and the first real visits

- The tap lands in SonoLune for a moment before your app opens, and it has to. The field that ends a visit is driven from inside SonoLune — the clock, the deepening, the release — so the app must be running before the other one comes forward. It arms the field FIRST and opens the app second, in that order: the gate is enforced outside SonoLune and re-read on every app switch, so an app that came forward first would arrive while the gate was still empty and the field would sit out the beginning of its own visit.
- A visit borrows your chosen apps and gives them back, and the giving back only ever worked in memory. The Presence room stays open during a visit, showing the visit's app with a cross beside it and a plus on every app below — and any of those taps wrote the borrowed list to disk over your own. The restore then put your list back on screen and never re-saved it, so nothing looked wrong until the next cold start.
- An app added during a visit now stays added. Writing only the live list meant your addition was swept away by the restore a few minutes later, with nothing to say why.
- A visit over several apps now shares one clock. Each switch between them was being read as arriving somewhere new, which is right for the ordinary per-app field — every chosen app carries its own timing — and precisely wrong inside a visit that agreed one length for all of them. The release beat had to be carried across too, or the second app fired it the instant it came forward.
- A visit names its apps. The room read "org.telegram.plus" beside an app you know by name, because the names were dropped on the way to the notification and could not be recovered at the other end. They travel with it now, and an older scheduled visit falls back to what it showed before rather than to a blank.
- The hand-off draws the agreement instead of nothing. It was deliberately blank on the argument that anything drawn in that half-second is friction — right about speed, wrong about emptiness, because a black rectangle is what a crash looks like. It is also the last moment the agreement can be stated, so it now names the app, the length, and says the quiet ARRIVES rather than something going wrong later. Nothing waits on it; it is gone the moment your app is ready.
- And an app's own settings say what the visit is doing to it. They describe what the app goes back to afterwards, which is correct and read as every switch being off while you sat inside a field with all of them on. Both are on screen now, each labelled as what it is.
- A visit now brings everything the suite has, rather than three of its levers. It turned on the visual quiet and left the grain, the felt pulse and the wind-down chime as you had them — which for most people is off, so an arc agreed to in advance arrived weaker than a field anyone could set by hand in ten seconds. Those three are the ones with a cost off the screen: they draw, they are felt, they are heard by the room. That is why they are wrong as a silent default anywhere else, and right in the one place where the choice was made calmly and in advance.
- The pulse was also the one setting a visit could switch on and never hand back — it lives apart from the rest of the field, so the snapshot taken at the start of a visit had never included it.
- The breath switch was reading the wrong setting. There are two: one floats the breath cue on a rhythm for as long as the field is up, the other opens a breath and a choice at the release. The per-app switch is captioned as the second and was wired to the first, so it described one thing and reported another — and a visit, which turns the second on, showed its own breath as off in the panel added to say what it was doing.
- The notification leads with "Tap to open". Every other arc can be read and ignored with nothing lost; this is the only one that is a door rather than an announcement, and the only one that quietly expires if it is left alone.

## Arcs that keep their own appointments

- An Android alarm is single-use. One goes off, and something has to set the next — and the only thing that did was opening SonoLune. So an arc fired, and if a full day passed with the app unopened, it never fired again. Nothing looked wrong: the arc was still listed, still switched on, still showing its time.
- It hid behind the arcs people tap. Tapping opens the app, and opening the app sets every alarm again — so anything you actually take keeps itself alive, and anything you let pass does not. Which means it broke precisely the arcs built to be declinable: an invitation you decline once is an invitation you never receive again.
- Every arc now sets its own next occurrence the moment it fires, before anything else it might do. The branches that end early — a held space, a suppressed arc — are the ones met most often, and each of them used to end the series along with the occurrence.
- It counts from the time the arc was DUE, not the time it arrived. Without exact-alarm permission Android is allowed to deliver late, and counting from arrival would walk a seven o'clock arc a few minutes later every day until it had drifted into a different hour.
- Restarting the phone no longer disarms everything. Android drops every pending alarm on reboot, and SonoLune restored the screen tint and nothing else — so after a restart no arc fired until the app was next opened. It now writes down what it scheduled and puts the whole schedule back, landing on the right weekday even if the phone was off for days.
- The restore is a replay, not a recalculation. Everything in it — when, what it says, what it opens — was worked out once and copied across, rather than worked out a second time in the part of the app that only runs after a reboot. Two sets of the same rules drift, and the one that runs least is the one that drifts unseen.

## What the notification and the reading were saying

- Night named the light and dropped the sound, in two places at once — which is why fixing one of them changed nothing. The shade is written in the app and can also be overridden by the part of SonoLune that draws the light, and both had the same omission: Night answered first and returned before the soundscape could be reached. The one that draws the light is never told WHICH soundscape is playing, only that one is, so it now stands aside for the half that knows the name.
- A field carried entirely by an arriving pack still read as an open space in one last place. The test for "is there sound here" was a hand-written list of the built-in elements, written before the packs existed and never extended — so the notification had the right words in hand and discarded them one step early. It now asks the same question everything else asks.
- The field reading stopped describing a sound that is not playing. A shaped-but-paused field said you were surrounded by open rain, over silence — the one thing a reading whose whole job is to name what you are experiencing must never do. Only the tense moves: the field is named as held, and comes back exactly as it was.
